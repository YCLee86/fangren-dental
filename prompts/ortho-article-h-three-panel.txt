Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape.

LAYOUT — ONE image divided into THREE panels by thin hand-drawn lines — lines only, drawn
by hand in the same warm dark brown as the rest of the linework, thin and slightly uneven.
No outer border, no boxes, no gutters, no arrows, no numbers.
  • PANEL A fills the LEFT HALF of the image, full height.
  • PANEL B is the TOP RIGHT quarter.
  • PANEL C is the BOTTOM RIGHT quarter.
THE THREE PANELS ARE THREE MOMENTS FROM ONE ORDINARY WEEK, NOT A COMPARISON AND NOT A
SEQUENCE. Nothing in the picture ranks them, and no mark, tick, cross, star or arrow
appears anywhere.

============================================================
PANEL A — LEFT HALF: FIXED METAL BRACES, A GROUP PHOTO
============================================================
Late afternoon under a Taiwanese shop arcade, at a small drinks stall. FIVE friends aged
about seventeen to twenty-two are STANDING together in a loose, casual huddle, taking a
photo of themselves. It is the loudest, happiest second of their afternoon. THEY ARE ALL ON
THEIR FEET — nobody sitting, nobody posed at a table — arranged the untidy way real friends
stand: different heights, slightly different distances from us, someone half a step behind.
Framing: we see them from about the hips up, heads well into the upper part of the panel,
faces LARGE and easy to read.

THE ONE TAKING THE PHOTO — at the left of this panel, a young man of about eighteen holds
his own phone up and out at arm's length toward the group, the way anyone takes a selfie;
we see the back of the phone. Everyone's eyes go to that phone, which is inside the panel,
so nobody looks out at the viewer.
HIS SMILE IS THE KEY THING IN THIS PANEL, AND IT IS A CLOSED-TEETH GRIN, NOT AN OPEN MOUTH.
His lips are pulled back wide and his eyes crinkle with laughter, but HIS UPPER AND LOWER
TEETH ARE LIGHTLY TOGETHER, so BOTH ROWS SHOW AT ONCE. Do not tip his head back, do not
open his mouth wide, do not show the inside of his mouth or his tongue.
HE WEARS FIXED METAL BRACES ON BOTH ARCHES AND BOTH SETS MUST BE VISIBLE: five or six small
plain square silver-grey brackets across his UPPER front teeth joined by one thin straight
wire, AND five or six more across his LOWER front teeth joined by another thin straight
wire. Two rows of brackets, two wires, one above the other, with the line where the teeth
meet running between them. Draw them simply, the way a magazine illustration would — small
squares and one line each — never a photographic close-up, never shiny metal, never yellow
or dirty teeth.

THE SECOND ONE IN TREATMENT — standing FURTHER BACK on the right of this panel, half behind
another friend so that only her head, shoulder and one arm show: a young woman of about
twenty, laughing with her mouth open. She is also in fixed braces, and ONE ORTHODONTIC
ELASTIC is visible INSIDE her open mouth.
HOW THE ELASTIC MUST BE DRAWN: one single fine line, stretched at a slant across the dark
opening of her mouth, running from a small hook on an UPPER canine bracket downward and
backward to a small hook on a LOWER back bracket. It lives entirely INSIDE the mouth,
behind the lips, in the dark gap between the upper and lower teeth.
IT MUST NOT: touch, cross or rest on the lips; appear anywhere on the skin outside the
mouth; hang out of the mouth; be gripped between the teeth like a straw or a stick; loop
around the head or the ears; be attached to anything outside the mouth; be thick, coloured
or doubled. It is ONE thin line inside an open mouth and nothing else. Keep her clearly
smaller and less prominent than the young man at the left — a second person who happens to
be in treatment too, not a second focus.

THE ONE WHO HAS NOT STARTED — near the middle of the group, a girl of about seventeen, hair
in a high ponytail, a bag strap across her chest. SHE IS GENUINELY LAUGHING — eyes crinkled
into happy curves, cheeks raised, shoulders up — BUT THE BACK OF ONE HAND IS RAISED IN
FRONT OF HER MOUTH, covering her teeth completely, an automatic habit she does not notice.
Happy, not sad: no downturned eyebrows, no tears, no blush of shame, not turning away.

THE OTHER TWO — a young man of about nineteen making a peace sign low near his chest, and a
young woman of about twenty-two with a drink held up in the air, mid-laugh. Open, easy
smiles.
HOW THEY STAND — friendly but not clingy. The two young men may stand shoulder to shoulder
or rest an arm across each other's shoulders. NO ONE PUTS AN ARM AROUND ANY OF THE YOUNG
WOMEN, and nobody's hand rests on a young woman's shoulder, waist or back.
BEHIND THEM — only what shows above and between their shoulders: the top of the drinks
stall counter with a stack of clean cups and a jug, a striped awning across the top, a
concrete pillar, a potted plant, and a slice of sunlit street at the edge with the green
tops of two trees. Plain sealed drink cups on a small table cut off at the bottom.

PANEL B — TOP RIGHT: A CLEAR ALIGNER COMES OUT BEFORE DINNER
============================================================
Early evening in a busy neighbourhood eating house, seen from slightly ABOVE at a raised
three-quarter angle. FOUR friends aged about eighteen to twenty-five sit around a round
table in the middle of dinner, all talking at once; past them, one more occupied table
further back says the place is full. The food is real and busy: a plate of stir-fried green
vegetables, a plate of sliced meat, a covered soup pot with a ladle and a few short strokes
of steam, bowls of rice, chopsticks in hands and on rests, a teapot.

THE ONE WITH THE ALIGNER — a young man of about twenty-two at the NEAR side of the table,
closest to us, drawn larger than the others because of where he sits. HE HAS JUST TAKEN HIS
CLEAR ALIGNER OUT SO THAT HE CAN EAT, AND HE IS PUTTING IT AWAY — a small private thing
done in the middle of a conversation.
HOW HIS HANDS MUST BE: LOW and CLOSE TO HIS OWN BODY, just above his lap or at the edge of
the table, turned INWARD toward himself. One hand holds the thin transparent tray, pointing
DOWNWARD toward the small open case held in his other hand, an inch or two above it, about
to drop in. HIS EYES ARE DOWN ON THE CASE.
HE IS NOT SHOWING IT TO ANYONE. He is not holding it up, not out, not at chest height, not
toward the table, not toward us. He is not grinning at the others while he does it, not
pleased with himself, not explaining anything. Nobody at the table has turned to watch him,
nobody points, nobody has stopped eating — it is as unremarkable as taking off a watch.
His own bowl and chopsticks wait in front of him, untouched for the moment; he is not
eating at this exact second.
A SECOND PERSON AT THE SAME TABLE, a young woman of about twenty on the far side, has
already put hers away: her small closed case sits beside her bowl while she reaches in with
her chopsticks, talking to her neighbour. She is not watching him and not mirroring him.

PANEL C — BOTTOM RIGHT: THE SAME ALIGNER, WORN OVERNIGHT
============================================================
The middle of the night, a small bedroom, quiet and dim. This panel is plainly DARKER than
the other two — the walls and bedding are deep dusty blue in shadow, and the only light is
a small low bedside lamp throwing a soft warm pool over the pillow and the near edge of the
quilt before falling away into the blue dark. Low key but clean and luminous, never murky,
never flat black.
THE SAME YOUNG MAN FROM PANEL B — same face, same hair — is asleep on his side, one cheek
on the pillow, the quilt pulled up, one hand loose beside his face. HIS MOUTH IS CLOSED and
his lips sit slightly FULL AND PUSHED FORWARD, a little rounded, the way lips do when
something thin is over the teeth inside. That is the only sign — the aligner itself is not
visible, and his teeth do not show.
ON THE BEDSIDE TABLE, catching the lamp light: THE SMALL CLAM-SHELL CASE LYING OPEN AND
EMPTY, both halves clearly showing nothing inside, because the tray is in his mouth. Draw
the empty case plainly and large enough to read at a glance. Beside it, the little lamp and
a glass of water. Nothing else on the table.
The room is lived in but calm: a plain quilt with a small pattern, a book lying face down
with a blank cover, a chair with clothes over its back, a window with the curtain drawn.
He is peaceful and comfortable, not restless, not uncomfortable, not ill.

ACROSS ALL THREE PANELS
============================================================
THE MARKS — In panels A and B put SHORT PARALLEL WHITE HAND-DRAWN STROKES at the same
slant, three or four together, in two or three small groups: beside the laughing friends,
at the raised drink, above the busy table. Solid where they begin, thinning to dry flecks
at the far end. They must NOT cross the dividing lines, must never be one long continuous
ribbon, never curl or loop, never cross anyone's face, and never look like smoke, steam or
a spirit. Steam from the soup is drawn the same way, as a few short strokes. Panel C has no
such marks — it is silent. No musical notes, stars, exclamation marks or other cartoon
symbols anywhere.
THE ACCENT BLUE goes in exactly three places in the whole image: the awning's stripe in
panel A, the aligner case in panel B, and the bedside lampshade in panel C.
PEOPLE — Simplified illustrative people, drawn as East Asian teenagers and young adults:
minimal features (small dot or short-line eyes, a simple line mouth, a small nose),
natural realistic head-to-body proportions, believable ages. Not photorealistic, not
faceless, no oversized heads, no noodle limbs, and NO large glossy anime eyes. Everyone in
this picture is between about fifteen and twenty-five years old — school and university
age. There are no middle-aged adults, no parents, no children and no elderly people
anywhere in the image.
NOBODY LOOKS AT THE VIEWER. Every face is turned toward something inside the picture.
KEEP THE FACES SIMPLE EVEN WHEN THEY ARE DRAWN LARGE. However close the framing, every
face stays minimal: eyes as small dots or short curved lines, a simple line mouth, a small
nose, cheeks as a soft flat blush at most. NO eyelashes, NO highlights or shine in the
eyes, NO detailed irises or ringed pupils, NO shading blended over the skin, NO nose
shadow, NO lip line or lip colour, NO chin or cheekbone modelling. EVERY PERSON IN THE
PICTURE IS DRAWN WITH EXACTLY THE SAME SIMPLIFIED FACE STYLE — never render one person
more realistically or more finely than the people around them.

CLOTHING — clothes are NEVER flat single-tone shapes. Model each garment with two or three
tones of its own colour: soft coloured-pencil shading in the folds, along the sleeves,
under the collar and at the hem, with the real details drawn — collar, cuffs, sleeve seam,
the ribbing at a cuff, the knit of a jumper, the drawstring of a hoodie. Everyone wears a
clearly different colour; no two people wear the same hue.

TEETH — Every tooth in this picture is clean and near-white: no yellow, no beige, no decay,
no dark spots, no missing teeth, no red or swollen gums, no blood. Draw teeth simply and
illustratively — a soft row, never a photorealistic mouth interior, never individually
outlined in heavy black.

STYLE — Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration, light and airy. Linework in
warm dark brown or soft charcoal, NEVER pure black: thin, hand-drawn, the weight varies,
strokes taper and sometimes break. Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light
marker, flat fills with two or three tones per hue and no smooth decorative gradients,
FILLED IN rather than left as a pale wash — every person, garment, food and object carries
its own solid colour, and bare paper is left only in the background and at the edges of the
picture. Colour edges a little loose, not always meeting the
line. A fine even paper grain over the whole image. Nothing is glossy, nothing is rendered
in 3D, nothing carries a photographic highlight. The airy high-key treatment applies to
panels A and B; panel C is genuinely dark and low key — clean and luminous, but plainly
darker than the other two, and never murky or black.

COLOUR — Light, muted and gentle: pale cream and off-white as the ground, warm sand, dusty
sage, soft warm grey, pale butter yellow, muted clay, soft terracotta. MUTED BUT FULLY LAID IN: every hue
sits one step deeper than a pastel, warm and solid, so the picture reads as a properly
coloured illustration and NEVER as a pale line drawing with a thin wash over it. Skin,
hair, clothes, wood and food are all clearly coloured. Still gentle — no neon, no harsh
colour, no dark heavy shadows. Colour throughout, never greyscale.
The ONE accent colour of this picture is a muted mid blue, used sparingly and only where
the scene description names it. Everything else stays in the warm neutral family. Do NOT
wash the whole picture in blue, do NOT use a bright or electric blue, and do NOT let blue
touch skin, faces or the sky.

CRITICAL — NO WRITING ANYWHERE IN THE IMAGE. No text, letters, words, numbers, logos,
signage, captions, labels, watermarks, price tags, menus, screen interfaces, app icons,
buttons or numeral clock faces, in any language. Cups, packaging, bags, shopfronts,
posters, clothing and every screen carry no printing of any kind. Where writing would
normally appear, leave the surface plain.

AVOID — greyscale; photorealism; 3D rendering; glossy digital painting; anime or manga
styling with large glossy eyes; thick uniform black outlines; chrome, metallic or
iridescent gradients; any recognisable branded, franchise or licensed character, mascot,
figurine or logo; any before-and-after or good-versus-bad comparison; a smile chart or a
row of graded smiles; any gutter, any outer border or frame around the image, any panel numbers or captions
(the three panels described above, separated by thin hand-drawn lines only, are correct); speech
balloons, thought bubbles, arrows, circles, spotlights or labels pointing at anyone's
mouth; a dental clinic treatment room; a reclining dental chair; an overhead operating
lamp; a drill, syringe, needle, probe or tray of instruments; plaster casts or a tray of
dental models (the braces and the one elastic in panel A, and the clear
aligner trays and their cases in panels B and C, are the exception — they must be in the
picture, and they are the ONLY dental things in it); hands inside a mouth; a photorealistic open mouth or mouth
interior; blood, swelling, pain, fear or embarrassment; anyone crying, sulking or covering
their face in shame; anyone looking at the viewer; middle-aged or elderly people; any young man with his arm
around a young woman's shoulders or waist.
ALSO AVOID — a magazine, a leaflet, a poster or a phone notification advertising anything;
