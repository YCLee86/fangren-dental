Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape.

SCENE — ONE image divided into TWO EQUAL HALVES, left and right, by a single thin
hand-drawn vertical line — one line only, drawn by hand in the same warm dark brown as the
rest of the linework, thin and slightly uneven. No boxes, no borders around the outside,
no third panel, no arrows and no gutter: just one line down the middle.
THE TWO HALVES ARE TWO DIFFERENT LIVES ON THE SAME DAY, and they are NOT a comparison.
Both halves are equally busy, equally loud, equally warm and equally happy. Neither half is
tidier, emptier, brighter, calmer or more crowded than the other; the same number of people
appear in each; nobody in either half looks better off than anyone in the other. Do not put
a tick, a cross, a star, a mark or any symbol of approval anywhere.

LEFT HALF — Late afternoon outside a small Taiwanese drinks stall under a shop arcade.
FOUR friends aged about seventeen to twenty-two are squeezed shoulder to shoulder, taking a
photo of themselves, and it is the noisiest second of their afternoon.
THE ONE TAKING THE PHOTO IS THE ONE WITH THE BRACES — a young man of about eighteen at the
centre of that half, holding his phone up at arm's length toward the group with one hand,
his other arm slung around a friend's shoulders. HE IS LAUGHING WITH HIS MOUTH WIDE OPEN,
head tipped back, eyes squeezed shut, completely unselfconscious. HE WEARS FIXED METAL
BRACES AND THEY ARE EASY TO SEE: on his upper front teeth, five or six small plain square
silver-grey brackets, one per tooth, joined by a single thin metal wire running straight
across them. Draw them simply, the way a magazine illustration would — small squares and
one line, never a photographic close-up, never shiny metal, never a mouth full of
machinery. Everyone around him is laughing too, all of them looking at his phone, which
sits inside the picture; nobody looks out at the viewer.
Around them: a plain counter with a stack of clean cups and a jug, two round stools, a
rolled-up awning above, sealed drink cups with completely plain surfaces on a small table,
a potted plant beside a pillar, and a strip of street beyond with the green tops of two
trees.

RIGHT HALF — A round dining table at home in the early evening, in the middle of a meal. FOUR
PEOPLE sit around it and the table is busy and warm: a plate of stir-fried green
vegetables, a plate of sliced meat, a covered soup pot with a ladle in it, four bowls of
rice, chopsticks in hands and chopsticks resting on rests, a teapot, two small side
dishes. Everyone is talking at once — one person is reaching across to pick up food, one
is laughing with their head turned, one is holding a bowl close to their chin mid-sentence.
It is an ordinary loud dinner among people who eat together often.

THE ONE WITH THE CLEAR ALIGNER — a young woman of about twenty, sitting at the near side
of the table. HER TEETH LOOK COMPLETELY ORDINARY: no brackets, no wire, nothing visible on
them. She has just lifted ONE THIN TRANSPARENT ALIGNER out of her mouth and is lowering it
into a small open clam-shell case that sits beside her bowl. Draw the tray as genuinely
see-through — a slim horseshoe-shaped shell of clear plastic, the table showing faintly
through it, its edge a single fine outline, a soft glassy tint, no metal, no wire, no
brackets, no colour, never milky or frosted. Her other hand is already reaching for her
chopsticks. She is doing this the way anyone takes off a watch before washing up —
completely automatic, mid-conversation, still smiling at whatever was just said.
NOBODY IS WATCHING HER DO IT. The other three are busy with the food and with each other;
nobody has turned toward her, nobody is pointing, nobody looks curious or concerned. It is
not an event.

HOLDING THE TWO HALVES TOGETHER — Warm low light falls in both halves at the same angle
from the same side, so the two places read as the same day. In each half put THREE OR FOUR
SHORT PARALLEL WHITE HAND-DRAWN STROKES at the same slant — the same slant in both halves —
solid where they begin and thinning to dry flecks at the far end, standing for movement and
laughter: in the left half beside the laughing friends, in the right half above the busy
table. They must NOT cross the dividing line. Never one long continuous ribbon, never
curling or looping, never crossing anyone's face, never smoke or steam, and no musical
notes, stars, exclamation marks or any other cartoon symbol.
THE ACCENT BLUE goes in exactly three places: the awning's stripe in the left half, the
aligner case in the right half, and one chair or stool.

PEOPLE — Simplified illustrative people, drawn as East Asian teenagers and young adults:
minimal features (small dot or short-line eyes, a simple line mouth, a small nose),
natural realistic head-to-body proportions, believable ages. Not photorealistic, not
faceless, no oversized heads, no noodle limbs, and NO large glossy anime eyes. Everyone in
this picture is between about fifteen and twenty-five years old — school and university
age. There are no middle-aged adults, no parents, no children and no elderly people
anywhere in the image.
NOBODY LOOKS AT THE VIEWER. Every face is turned toward something inside the picture.

CLOTHING — clothes are NEVER flat single-tone shapes. Model each garment with two or three
tones of its own colour: soft coloured-pencil shading in the folds, along the sleeves,
under the collar and at the hem, with the real details drawn — collar, cuffs, sleeve seam,
the ribbing at a cuff, the knit of a jumper, the drawstring of a hoodie. Everyone wears a
clearly different colour; no two people wear the same hue.

TEETH — Every tooth in this picture is clean and near-white: no yellow, no beige, no decay,
no dark spots, no missing teeth, no red or swollen gums, no blood. Draw teeth simply and
illustratively — a soft row, never a photorealistic mouth interior, never individually
outlined in heavy black.

STYLE — Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration, light and airy. Linework in
warm dark brown or soft charcoal, NEVER pure black: thin, hand-drawn, the weight varies,
strokes taper and sometimes break. Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light
marker, flat fills with two or three tones per hue and no smooth decorative gradients,
with plenty of the pale paper left showing through — large parts of the ground, the walls
and the air stay almost bare paper. Colour edges a little loose, not always meeting the
line. A fine even paper grain over the whole image. Nothing is glossy, nothing is rendered
in 3D, nothing carries a photographic highlight.

COLOUR — Light, muted and gentle: pale cream and off-white as the ground, warm sand, dusty
sage, soft warm grey, pale butter yellow, muted clay, soft terracotta. Desaturated
throughout — nothing heavy, no neon, no dark heavy shadows. Colour throughout, never
greyscale.
The ONE accent colour of this picture is a muted mid blue, used sparingly and only where
the scene description names it. Everything else stays in the warm neutral family. Do NOT
wash the whole picture in blue, do NOT use a bright or electric blue, and do NOT let blue
touch skin, faces or the sky.

CRITICAL — NO WRITING ANYWHERE IN THE IMAGE. No text, letters, words, numbers, logos,
signage, captions, labels, watermarks, price tags, menus, screen interfaces, app icons,
buttons or numeral clock faces, in any language. Cups, packaging, bags, shopfronts,
posters, clothing and every screen carry no printing of any kind. Where writing would
normally appear, leave the surface plain.

AVOID — greyscale; photorealism; 3D rendering; glossy digital painting; anime or manga
styling with large glossy eyes; thick uniform black outlines; chrome, metallic or
iridescent gradients; any recognisable branded, franchise or licensed character, mascot,
figurine or logo; any before-and-after or good-versus-bad comparison; a smile chart or a
row of graded smiles; any gutter, any outer border or frame around the image, any third panel, any
comic-strip sequence (the single thin hand-drawn line down the middle described above is
the ONE division in this picture, and it must be there); speech
balloons, thought bubbles, arrows, circles, spotlights or labels pointing at anyone's
mouth; a dental clinic treatment room; a reclining dental chair; an overhead operating
lamp; a drill, syringe, needle, probe or tray of instruments; plaster casts or a tray of
dental models (the clear aligner tray and its small case described above are the
exception — they must be in the picture, and they are the ONLY dental objects in it); hands inside a mouth; a photorealistic open mouth or mouth
interior; blood, swelling, pain, fear or embarrassment; anyone crying, sulking or covering
their face in shame; anyone looking at the viewer; middle-aged or elderly people.
ALSO AVOID — a magazine, a leaflet, a poster or a phone notification advertising anything;
any pop-up, banner or message on any screen; two appliances laid side by side like
products being compared; a tick, a cross, a star or any mark that says one thing is better
than another; a tooth diagram or dental chart anywhere in the picture.
