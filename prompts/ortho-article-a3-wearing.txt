Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape.

SCENE — One single lively scene, no panels, no frames. Late afternoon outside a small
Taiwanese drinks stall under a shop arcade: five friends aged about sixteen to twenty-two
are squeezed together, taking a group photo of themselves. The moment is loud and happy —
this is the noisiest, most confident second of their afternoon, and every face in it is
enjoying itself.

WHAT IS HAPPENING — At the far LEFT edge of the picture, one friend's arm reaches in from
outside the frame holding a phone at arm's length toward the group; we see the back of the
phone and the arm only, and everyone is looking at that phone, which sits INSIDE the
picture at its left edge. So nobody is looking out at the viewer. Draw them crowded
shoulder to shoulder, leaning in toward the phone, filling roughly the middle
three-quarters of the image and standing about two-thirds of its height.

THREE OF THEM MATTER — and none of them is presented as better off than the others. They
are simply three friends at the same table:

  1. THE LOUDEST ONE — a young man of about eighteen, the happiest and most unrestrained
     person in the picture: head tipped back, eyes squeezed shut with laughter, mouth wide
     open, one arm slung around the shoulders of the person next to him. HE WEARS FIXED
     METAL BRACES AND THEY ARE EASY TO SEE: on his upper front teeth, five or six small
     plain square silver-grey brackets, one per tooth, joined by a single thin metal wire
     running straight across them. Draw the braces simply, the way a magazine illustration
     would — small squares and one line, never a photographic close-up, never shiny metal,
     never a mouth full of machinery. He is completely unselfconscious.

  2. THE ONE WITH THE CLEAR ALIGNER — a young woman of about twenty standing on the other
     side of the group, laughing just as freely with her mouth open and her head turned a
     little toward the friend beside her. SHE IS WEARING A CLEAR ALIGNER OVER HER UPPER
     TEETH AND IT IS ALMOST INVISIBLE — that is the whole point of how it must be drawn.
     Her teeth read as a clean ordinary row; over them lies only the very faintest hint of
     a thin transparent shell: one extremely fine curved line following just above the
     edges of the upper teeth, a barely-there pale sheen across their surface, and a
     single small notch where the shell ends near the back. NO metal, NO wire, NO
     brackets, NO squares, NO colour, NO thick outline, NO frosted or milky layer, NO
     obvious appliance. Someone glancing at this picture should read her as simply having
     nice teeth; only a second look finds the thin clear shell.
     Beside her, ONE FRIEND HAS LEANED IN toward her face — a young man of about
     nineteen, eyebrows lifted, grinning in surprise and delight, as if he has just this
     second noticed. He is close but relaxed and friendly, both hands still on his drink;
     he is NOT pointing at her mouth, NOT holding her chin, NOT inspecting her, and there
     is no arrow, circle, magnifying glass or highlight of any kind on her teeth.

  3. THE MAIN CHARACTER — standing just right of the centre of the picture: a girl of
     about seventeen, hair in a high ponytail, a school bag strap across her chest. SHE IS
     GENUINELY LAUGHING TOO — her eyes are crinkled into happy curves, her cheeks are
     raised, her shoulders are up — BUT THE BACK OF ONE HAND IS RAISED IN FRONT OF HER
     MOUTH, covering her teeth completely, an automatic habit she does not even notice.
     This must be unmistakable at a glance: everyone else's mouth is open and hers is the
     one hidden behind a hand.
     IMPORTANT — her feelings are HAPPY, not sad: no downturned eyebrows, no tears, no
     blush of shame, no hunching away from the group, not turning her face aside, not
     standing apart. She is in the middle of the fun, just with her hand up.

THE FIFTH FRIEND — a young woman of about twenty-two at the back of the group, holding a
drink up in the air, mid-laugh, head turned toward the loud one. An open, easy smile.

THE BALANCE — The young man with the metal braces is the most joyful figure in the
picture, and the young woman with the clear aligner is exactly as happy as he is. Neither
of the two is drawn as tidier, prettier, more confident or more grown-up than the other,
and neither is placed at the centre. Do not compare them, do not pair them off facing each
other, and do not put anything between them.

THE NOISE — Show sound and movement with hand-drawn marks, not with symbols: a few SHORT
PARALLEL WHITE HAND-DRAWN STROKES, three or four together at the same slant, placed in two
or three small groups — beside the loud one's head, at the edge of a swinging bag, near
the raised drink — solid where they begin and thinning to dry flecks at the far end. They
are small quick strokes standing for movement and laughter, never one long continuous
ribbon, never curling or looping, never crossing anyone's face, never smoke or steam. Do
NOT use musical notes, stars, exclamation marks or any other cartoon symbol.

THE PLACE — A small drinks stall under a concrete arcade, warm and ordinary: a plain
counter with a stack of clean cups and a jug, a low table with sealed drink cups on it
(the cups and their lids are completely plain, no printing), two round stools, a rolled-up
awning above, a scooter parked at the far right edge seen from behind, a potted plant by a
pillar, and a strip of late-afternoon street beyond with the green tops of two street
trees. Warm low sun comes from the right, so the group is lit from that side. Every
surface has something specified on it; leave no blank shopfront panel for invention, and
hang no signs of any kind.
THE ACCENT BLUE goes in exactly three places: the awning's stripe, the main character's
shirt, and one stool.

PEOPLE — Simplified illustrative people, drawn as East Asian teenagers and young adults:
minimal features (small dot or short-line eyes, a simple line mouth, a small nose),
natural realistic head-to-body proportions, believable ages. Not photorealistic, not
faceless, no oversized heads, no noodle limbs, and NO large glossy anime eyes. Everyone in
this picture is between about fifteen and twenty-five years old — school and university
age. There are no middle-aged adults, no parents, no children and no elderly people
anywhere in the image.
NOBODY LOOKS AT THE VIEWER. Every face is turned toward something inside the picture.

CLOTHING — clothes are NEVER flat single-tone shapes. Model each garment with two or three
tones of its own colour: soft coloured-pencil shading in the folds, along the sleeves,
under the collar and at the hem, with the real details drawn — collar, cuffs, sleeve seam,
the ribbing at a cuff, the knit of a jumper, the drawstring of a hoodie. Everyone wears a
clearly different colour; no two people wear the same hue.

TEETH — Every tooth in this picture is clean and near-white: no yellow, no beige, no decay,
no dark spots, no missing teeth, no red or swollen gums, no blood. Draw teeth simply and
illustratively — a soft row, never a photorealistic mouth interior, never individually
outlined in heavy black.

STYLE — Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration, light and airy. Linework in
warm dark brown or soft charcoal, NEVER pure black: thin, hand-drawn, the weight varies,
strokes taper and sometimes break. Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light
marker, flat fills with two or three tones per hue and no smooth decorative gradients,
with plenty of the pale paper left showing through — large parts of the ground, the walls
and the air stay almost bare paper. Colour edges a little loose, not always meeting the
line. A fine even paper grain over the whole image. Nothing is glossy, nothing is rendered
in 3D, nothing carries a photographic highlight.

COLOUR — Light, muted and gentle: pale cream and off-white as the ground, warm sand, dusty
sage, soft warm grey, pale butter yellow, muted clay, soft terracotta. Desaturated
throughout — nothing heavy, no neon, no dark heavy shadows. Colour throughout, never
greyscale.
The ONE accent colour of this picture is a muted mid blue, used sparingly and only where
the scene description names it. Everything else stays in the warm neutral family. Do NOT
wash the whole picture in blue, do NOT use a bright or electric blue, and do NOT let blue
touch skin, faces or the sky.

CRITICAL — NO WRITING ANYWHERE IN THE IMAGE. No text, letters, words, numbers, logos,
signage, captions, labels, watermarks, price tags, menus, screen interfaces, app icons,
buttons or numeral clock faces, in any language. Cups, packaging, bags, shopfronts,
posters, clothing and every screen carry no printing of any kind. Where writing would
normally appear, leave the surface plain.

AVOID — greyscale; photorealism; 3D rendering; glossy digital painting; anime or manga
styling with large glossy eyes; thick uniform black outlines; chrome, metallic or
iridescent gradients; any recognisable branded, franchise or licensed character, mascot,
figurine or logo; any before-and-after or good-versus-bad comparison; a smile chart or a
row of graded smiles; any panel borders, gutters, frames or comic-strip divisions; speech
balloons, thought bubbles, arrows, circles, spotlights or labels pointing at anyone's
mouth; a dental clinic treatment room; a reclining dental chair; an overhead operating
lamp; a drill, syringe, needle, probe or tray of instruments; dental models, plaster casts
or appliances lying on a table; hands inside a mouth; a photorealistic open mouth or mouth
interior; blood, swelling, pain, fear or embarrassment; anyone crying, sulking or covering
their face in shame; anyone looking at the viewer; middle-aged or elderly people.
ALSO AVOID — a magazine, a leaflet, a poster or a phone notification advertising anything;
any pop-up, banner or message on any screen; two appliances laid side by side like
products being compared; a tick, a cross, a star or any mark that says one thing is better
than another; a tooth diagram or dental chart anywhere in the picture.
