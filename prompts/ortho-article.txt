Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape.

SCENE — One single scene, no panels: the consultation corner of a small, friendly dental
clinic in the late morning. An orthodontist and an adult patient sit at a low round wooden
table, talking across it. This is a conversation, not a treatment: nobody is lying down,
there is no dental chair, no drill, no bright overhead operating lamp, no gloves reaching
into anyone's mouth.

THE TWO PEOPLE — The DENTIST is on the LEFT side of the picture, seen at three-quarters
from behind-left, leaning slightly forward. He is a man of about forty-five in a soft
muted blue scrub top with the sleeves at the elbow, short dark hair, a calm friendly face
in profile, speaking. He holds up a small pale dental model of an upper arch at about
chest height and points at it with the hand nearer the viewer, one finger extended toward
the front of the arch.
The PATIENT is on the RIGHT side, seen from the front-three-quarters but turned toward the
dentist so she is looking at the model, never at the viewer. She is a woman of about forty
— clearly a grown adult, not a teenager and not a child — with hair tied back, in a warm
sand-coloured knitted top. She sits comfortably with both forearms resting on the table,
her head tilted a little, listening and thinking: interested and at ease, faintly smiling,
NOT anxious, NOT frowning, NOT nodding along in agreement. Her own mouth is closed and she
wears no braces on her own teeth.
Her small closed notebook and a soft shoulder bag rest on the chair beside her — she is
about to go home and think it over. There is nothing to sign anywhere: no contract, no
form, no clipboard, no pen in anyone's hand, no price sheet, no money.

ON THE TABLE — laid out neatly on a small pale tray between them, the two kinds of
appliance side by side, drawn large enough to tell apart at a glance and simply, like a
diagram in a magazine rather than a product photograph:
  • on the side nearer the dentist, a short curved segment of teeth wearing FIXED METAL
    BRACES — four or five small square silver-grey brackets, one on each tooth, joined by
    a single thin metal wire running straight across all of them. The brackets are plain
    little squares; the wire is one continuous thin line, not a tangle;
  • on the side nearer the patient, a CLEAR REMOVABLE ALIGNER — one thin transparent tray
    shaped like a horseshoe of teeth, drawn with a light outline and a faint glassy tint
    so it clearly reads as see-through plastic, resting in a small rounded case.
Also on the table: a shallow ceramic cup of water and a low glass jar holding two pencils.
Nothing else. The table surface is otherwise plain wood.

ON THE WALL BEHIND — a flat wall-mounted screen at the far left of the wall, tilted
slightly toward the two of them, showing ONE thing only: a simple illustrative dental
X-ray of a jaw — pale blue-grey, soft-edged, showing a calm even row of tooth roots. It
is drawn as a plain picture, not a photograph and not a computer interface: no window
frame, no buttons, no toolbar, no cursor, no charts, no graphs, no timeline, no arrows, no
before-and-after pair, no side-by-side comparison of any kind, and absolutely no writing.

THE ROOM — Warm, tidy and lived-in, but calm and uncluttered; a real clinic, not a living
room. Every surface has something specified on it and nothing is left blank for invention:
a tall window at the back left with a plain pale curtain drawn to one side, showing a
simple blue sky with two or three soft flat clouds and the green tops of trees; a low
wooden cabinet along the back wall holding a row of books with completely blank spines, a
round-bellied ceramic jar and a folded towel; two potted plants in glazed pots, one on the
floor by the window and one on the cabinet; on the back wall a single framed picture whose
content is an ABSTRACT LANDSCAPE — three or four soft overlapping bands of muted colour
suggesting hills, with no writing, no teeth, no faces and no figures; a plain pale tiled
floor. No shelves of figurines, no toys, no ornaments with faces, no mascots.

WHAT LIGHT DOES — One warm light source only: daylight from the window at the back left,
falling across the table and onto the model in the dentist's hand. In the air between the
window and the table, FOUR OR FIVE SHORT PARALLEL WHITE HAND-DRAWN LINES lie at the same
slant, evenly spaced, solid where they begin and thinning to dry flecks at the far end —
a group of short strokes standing for a shaft of light, never one long continuous ribbon,
never curling or looping, never crossing the people's faces, and never resembling smoke,
steam or a spirit. Keep them small and quiet in the upper left of the picture.

PEOPLE — Simplified illustrative people: minimal features (small dot or short-line eyes, a
simple line mouth, a small nose), natural realistic head-to-body proportions, believable
ages. Not photorealistic, not faceless, no oversized heads, no huge glossy anime eyes, no
noodle limbs. Only these two people are in the picture. Neither of them looks at the
viewer. Together they occupy about half the height of the image; the room reads around
them.
CLOTHING — clothes are NEVER flat single-tone shapes. Model each garment with two or three
tones of its own colour: soft coloured-pencil shading in the folds, along the sleeves,
under the collar and at the hem, with the real details drawn — collar, cuffs, sleeve seam,
the ribbing at a cuff, the knit of her top. The two of them wear clearly different
colours and never the same hue.

STYLE — Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration, light and airy. Linework in
warm dark brown or soft charcoal, NEVER pure black: thin, hand-drawn, weight varies,
strokes taper and sometimes break. Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light
marker, flat fills with two or three tones per hue and no smooth decorative gradients,
with plenty of the pale paper left showing through — large parts of the floor, wall and
air stay almost bare paper. Colour edges a little loose, not always meeting the line. A
fine even paper grain over the whole image. Nothing is glossy, nothing is rendered in
3D, nothing has a photographic highlight.

COLOUR — Light, muted and gentle: pale cream and off-white as the ground, warm sand, dusty
sage, soft warm grey, pale butter yellow, muted clay. Desaturated throughout — nothing
heavy, no neon, no dark heavy shadows. Colour throughout, never greyscale.
The ONE accent colour of this picture is a muted mid blue, used sparingly and only in
these places: the dentist's scrub top, a single narrow painted band along the front edge
of the cabinet, the aligner case, and one of the plant pots. Everything else stays in the
warm neutral family. Do NOT wash the whole picture in blue, do NOT use a bright or
electric blue, and do NOT let blue touch skin, faces, the floor or the walls.
KEEP THE TEETH OUT OF THE COLOUR — every tooth in the picture, on the arch model and in
the two appliances, stays clean and near-white: no yellow, no beige, no decay, no dark
spots, no missing teeth, no red or bleeding gums, no blood, no pain, no fear.

WHERE TEETH MAY APPEAR — teeth exist in exactly three places: the arch model in the
dentist's hand, the two appliances on the tray, and the X-ray on the screen. There are no
teeth anywhere else — none floating in the air, none on the walls, none in the framed
picture, none on the cabinet, none on the floor, none on anyone's clothing.

CRITICAL — NO WRITING ANYWHERE IN THE IMAGE. No text, letters, words, numbers, logos,
signage, captions, labels, watermarks or numeral clock faces, in any language. The screen
carries no text of any kind; book spines are blank; the framed picture is wordless; the
notebook cover is blank; clothing and the aligner case carry no printing. Where writing
would normally appear, leave the surface plain.

AVOID — greyscale; photorealism; 3D rendering; glossy digital painting; anime or manga
styling with large glossy eyes; thick uniform black outlines; chrome, metallic or
iridescent gradients; any recognisable branded, franchise or licensed character, mascot,
figurine, plush toy or logo anywhere in the room; shelves of collectible figures; a
computer interface, window chrome, buttons, charts, graphs, timelines or progress bars;
any before-and-after or good-versus-bad comparison; a treatment in progress; a reclining
dental chair; an overhead operating lamp; a drill, syringe, needle, probe or tray of
instruments; a face mask over either person's face; hands inside a mouth; a photorealistic
open mouth or mouth interior; blood, swelling, pain or fear; anyone signing or being
handed a document; anyone looking at the viewer; a crowd of extra people; empty
undecorated walls and floors with nothing on them.
