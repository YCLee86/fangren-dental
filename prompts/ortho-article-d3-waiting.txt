Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape.

SCENE — One single scene, no panels, no drawn dividing line. The waiting area of a small,
calm dental clinic on an afternoon: a wide low bench along a pale wall, a narrow side
table between two seats, a tall window on the left with the green tops of street trees
beyond it, a potted plant, a low shelf holding a ceramic jar and a folded towel, and a
framed picture on the wall whose content is an ABSTRACT LANDSCAPE — three or four soft
overlapping bands of muted colour suggesting hills, with no writing, no teeth, no faces
and no figures. This is a waiting area only: NO treatment room, NO reclining dental chair,
NO operating lamp and NO instruments are visible anywhere.

THE TWO OF THEM — Two people of about eighteen and twenty-two sit on the same bench, one
on each side of the narrow side table, both waiting for their own appointment. THE SIDE
TABLE IS THE ONLY THING SEPARATING THEM — there is no drawn line.
THE MOMENT — They have just caught each other's eye and each given the other a small nod:
two people who recognise, without a word, that the other one is on the same errand. Both
faces are turned slightly toward each other, both with a small easy smile. It is a quiet,
friendly, almost funny second. Neither is talking, neither is leaning over to look at the
other's things, and neither is comparing anything.

THE TWO OF THEM — Two friends of the same age, each doing the small daily task that their
own choice asks of them. NEITHER OF THEM IS THE BETTER OFF ONE. Draw them with equal
weight in the picture, the same size, the same warm light, the same calm easy mood, both
completely comfortable with what they are doing and with each other. Do not make one look
tidier, prettier, more relaxed, more grown-up or more troubled than the other. Do not put
a tick, a cross, a star, a mark or any symbol of approval anywhere.

  • THE ONE WITH FIXED METAL BRACES — HE WEARS THEM AND THEY ARE EASY TO SEE: on his upper
    front teeth, five or six small plain square silver-grey brackets, one per tooth,
    joined by a single thin metal wire running straight across them. Draw them simply, the
    way a magazine illustration would — small squares and one line, never a photographic
    close-up, never shiny metal, never a mouth full of machinery. HIS TASK IS CLEANING:
    he is handling A VERY SMALL INTERDENTAL BRUSH — a short slim handle with a tiny
    bristle head no bigger than a matchstick tip — which he has just taken out of a small
    soft zip pouch. The pouch and the little brush are ordinary, well-used, everyday
    things he clearly carries with him all the time.

  • THE ONE WITH THE CLEAR ALIGNER — HER TEETH LOOK COMPLETELY ORDINARY: no brackets, no
    wire, nothing visible on them. HER TASK IS THE TRAY ITSELF: she is holding ONE THIN
    TRANSPARENT ALIGNER between her fingers, just taken out of her mouth, and lowering it
    into a small open clam-shell case on the surface in front of her. Draw the tray as
    genuinely see-through — a slim horseshoe-shaped shell of clear plastic, the surface
    behind it showing faintly through, its edge a single fine outline, a soft glassy tint,
    no metal, no wire, no brackets, no colour, never milky or frosted.
    BESIDE HER, A SMALL STACK OF THREE OR FOUR MORE OF THE SAME CLEAR TRAYS sits within
    easy reach, each in its own thin flat case, stacked like a short pile of coasters —
    the ones she has not changed into yet. They are casual and everyday, not displayed,
    not fanned out, not arranged like products for sale.

WHERE EACH THING SITS — He sits on the left with the little zip pouch open on his knee and
the small brush just visible in it, having clearly used it before coming in; his braces
show as he smiles. She sits on the right with the open case resting on her lap, the tray
in her fingers half lowered into it, and the small stack of spare trays tucked into the
open top of the bag beside her.

THE ROOM'S FEELING — Warm afternoon light from the window on the left falls evenly across
both of them. The clinic is tidy and quiet but not cold or clinical: soft cushions on the
bench, a rug, wood rather than white plastic. Every surface has something specified on it;
leave no blank wall panel for invention and hang no signs of any kind.
MOVEMENT — Put THREE OR FOUR SHORT PARALLEL WHITE HAND-DRAWN STROKES at the same slant in
the air near the window, solid where they begin and thinning to dry flecks at the far end,
standing for the afternoon light. Small and quiet; never one long continuous ribbon, never
curling or looping, never crossing anyone's face, never smoke or steam, no cartoon
symbols.
THE ACCENT BLUE goes in exactly three places: a cushion on the bench, the frame of the
window, and the aligner case.

PEOPLE — Simplified illustrative people, drawn as East Asian teenagers and young adults:
minimal features (small dot or short-line eyes, a simple line mouth, a small nose),
natural realistic head-to-body proportions, believable ages. Not photorealistic, not
faceless, no oversized heads, no noodle limbs, and NO large glossy anime eyes. Everyone in
this picture is between about fifteen and twenty-five years old — school and university
age. There are no middle-aged adults, no parents, no children and no elderly people
anywhere in the image.
NOBODY LOOKS AT THE VIEWER. Every face is turned toward something inside the picture.

CLOTHING — clothes are NEVER flat single-tone shapes. Model each garment with two or three
tones of its own colour: soft coloured-pencil shading in the folds, along the sleeves,
under the collar and at the hem, with the real details drawn — collar, cuffs, sleeve seam,
the ribbing at a cuff, the knit of a jumper, the drawstring of a hoodie. Everyone wears a
clearly different colour; no two people wear the same hue.

TEETH — Every tooth in this picture is clean and near-white: no yellow, no beige, no decay,
no dark spots, no missing teeth, no red or swollen gums, no blood. Draw teeth simply and
illustratively — a soft row, never a photorealistic mouth interior, never individually
outlined in heavy black.

STYLE — Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration, light and airy. Linework in
warm dark brown or soft charcoal, NEVER pure black: thin, hand-drawn, the weight varies,
strokes taper and sometimes break. Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light
marker, flat fills with two or three tones per hue and no smooth decorative gradients,
with plenty of the pale paper left showing through — large parts of the ground, the walls
and the air stay almost bare paper. Colour edges a little loose, not always meeting the
line. A fine even paper grain over the whole image. Nothing is glossy, nothing is rendered
in 3D, nothing carries a photographic highlight.

COLOUR — Light, muted and gentle: pale cream and off-white as the ground, warm sand, dusty
sage, soft warm grey, pale butter yellow, muted clay, soft terracotta. Desaturated
throughout — nothing heavy, no neon, no dark heavy shadows. Colour throughout, never
greyscale.
The ONE accent colour of this picture is a muted mid blue, used sparingly and only where
the scene description names it. Everything else stays in the warm neutral family. Do NOT
wash the whole picture in blue, do NOT use a bright or electric blue, and do NOT let blue
touch skin, faces or the sky.

CRITICAL — NO WRITING ANYWHERE IN THE IMAGE. No text, letters, words, numbers, logos,
signage, captions, labels, watermarks, price tags, menus, screen interfaces, app icons,
buttons or numeral clock faces, in any language. Cups, packaging, bags, shopfronts,
posters, clothing and every screen carry no printing of any kind. Where writing would
normally appear, leave the surface plain.

AVOID — greyscale; photorealism; 3D rendering; glossy digital painting; anime or manga
styling with large glossy eyes; thick uniform black outlines; chrome, metallic or
iridescent gradients; any recognisable branded, franchise or licensed character, mascot,
figurine or logo; any before-and-after or good-versus-bad comparison; a smile chart or a
row of graded smiles; any panel borders, gutters, frames or comic-strip divisions; speech
balloons, thought bubbles, arrows, circles, spotlights or labels pointing at anyone's
mouth; a dental clinic treatment room; a reclining dental chair; an overhead operating
lamp; a drill, syringe, needle, probe or tray of instruments; plaster casts or a tray of
dental models (the aligner trays, the aligner case and the small brush described above are
the exception — they must be in the picture, and they are the ONLY dental objects in it); hands inside a mouth; a photorealistic open mouth or mouth
interior; blood, swelling, pain, fear or embarrassment; anyone crying, sulking or covering
their face in shame; anyone looking at the viewer; middle-aged or elderly people.
ALSO AVOID — a magazine, a leaflet, a poster or a phone notification advertising anything;
any pop-up, banner or message on any screen; two appliances laid side by side like
products being compared; a tick, a cross, a star or any mark that says one thing is better
than another; a tooth diagram or dental chart anywhere in the picture.
