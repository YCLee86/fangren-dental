Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape.

SCENE — One single scene, no panels, no frames, no dividing lines. Early evening in a busy
neighbourhood eating house: a big round table with SIX friends aged about eighteen to
twenty-five in the middle of dinner, everyone talking at once. Beyond them, more tables
with more people — the place is full.

THE VIEWPOINT — Seen from ABOVE, looking down at the table at a raised three-quarter angle,
as if standing beside it. The whole round tabletop and all six people around it are
visible, and past them we can see across the room to two or three other occupied tables.
This is not a flat eye-level view.

WHO IS DOING WHAT — TWO of the six are wearing clear aligners, and BOTH of them are dealt
with in exactly the same matter-of-fact way. They are NOT sitting next to each other and
NOT facing each other; put them on opposite sides of the table, and do not link them by
posture, colour or gesture.

  • A YOUNG MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-TWO, at the near side of the table and closest to us, is
    the most prominent person in the picture. He is mid-laugh at something across the
    table, head turned, completely at ease. Raised in front of his chest, well clear of the
    tabletop, HE HOLDS ONE THIN TRANSPARENT ALIGNER between finger and thumb of one hand
    and A SMALL OPEN CLAM-SHELL CASE in the other, the tray about to go into it. The tray
    and case are DRAWN LARGE AND CLEARLY — the tray at least as wide as his hand.
  • A YOUNG WOMAN OF ABOUT TWENTY on the far side of the table has already put hers away:
    her small closed case sits beside her bowl and she is reaching in with her chopsticks,
    talking to the person next to her. She is doing something ordinary — she is not
    watching him, not holding anything up, not mirroring him.

BOTH ALIGNERS MUST READ AS TRANSPARENT: a slim horseshoe-shaped shell of clear plastic
moulded to the shape of a row of teeth, drawn with a single fine outline and a soft glassy
tint, the background showing faintly through. No metal, no wire, no brackets, no colour;
never milky, never frosted, never solid white.
NEITHER OF THEM IS PERFORMING THIS. Nobody at the table has turned to watch, nobody points,
nobody looks curious or concerned, nobody has stopped eating. Taking the tray out before
eating is as unremarkable here as taking off a watch. Neither of them is eating at the exact
moment we see them holding a tray: no chopsticks in that person's hands, no bowl at the
mouth.

THE PEOPLE — Six around this table, mixed young men and young women, all between about
eighteen and twenty-five, all clearly enjoying themselves: one leaning back laughing, one
reaching across for food, one holding a bowl close to the chin mid-sentence, one turned to
talk to a neighbour. Nobody is quiet, nobody is left out, and NO ONE has an arm around any
of the young women.
BEYOND THEM — two or three more tables further back with other diners, drawn smaller,
simpler and softer, just enough to say the room is full. Those distant people have the
simplest faces of all and none of them is looking this way.

THE TABLE — Busy and real: a plate of stir-fried green vegetables, a plate of sliced meat,
a covered soup pot with a ladle and a few short strokes of steam, a plate of fried rice,
six bowls of rice, chopsticks in hands and on rests, a teapot and small cups. Nothing on the
table is brighter or more detailed than the faces and the aligner in his hands.

THE ROOM — An ordinary neighbourhood eating house: plain tiled floor, a few round tables
with mismatched stools, a warm pendant light over each table, a counter along the back wall
with a stack of clean bowls and a big rice cooker, a potted plant in the corner, and a
window at the back with the last warm light of the day. Every surface has something
specified on it; hang no signs, no menu boards, no calendars and no writing of any kind.

THE NOISE — Put SHORT PARALLEL WHITE HAND-DRAWN STROKES at the same slant in three small
groups — above the table, near the person laughing, near the far tables — three or four
strokes each, solid where they begin and thinning to dry flecks at the far end. Never one
long continuous ribbon, never curling or looping, never crossing anyone's face, never
smoke, and no musical notes, stars or other cartoon symbols. Steam from the soup is drawn
the same way, as a few short strokes, not a swirling cloud.
THE ACCENT BLUE goes in exactly two places: the aligner case and one bowl's rim pattern.
PEOPLE — Simplified illustrative people, drawn as East Asian teenagers and young adults:
minimal features (small dot or short-line eyes, a simple line mouth, a small nose),
natural realistic head-to-body proportions, believable ages. Not photorealistic, not
faceless, no oversized heads, no noodle limbs, and NO large glossy anime eyes. Everyone in
this picture is between about fifteen and twenty-five years old — school and university
age. There are no middle-aged adults, no parents, no children and no elderly people
anywhere in the image.
NOBODY LOOKS AT THE VIEWER. Every face is turned toward something inside the picture.
KEEP THE FACES SIMPLE EVEN WHEN THEY ARE DRAWN LARGE. However close the framing, every
face stays minimal: eyes as small dots or short curved lines, a simple line mouth, a small
nose, cheeks as a soft flat blush at most. NO eyelashes, NO highlights or shine in the
eyes, NO detailed irises or ringed pupils, NO shading blended over the skin, NO nose
shadow, NO lip line or lip colour, NO chin or cheekbone modelling. EVERY PERSON IN THE
PICTURE IS DRAWN WITH EXACTLY THE SAME SIMPLIFIED FACE STYLE — never render one person
more realistically or more finely than the people around them.

CLOTHING — clothes are NEVER flat single-tone shapes. Model each garment with two or three
tones of its own colour: soft coloured-pencil shading in the folds, along the sleeves,
under the collar and at the hem, with the real details drawn — collar, cuffs, sleeve seam,
the ribbing at a cuff, the knit of a jumper, the drawstring of a hoodie. Everyone wears a
clearly different colour; no two people wear the same hue.

TEETH — Every tooth in this picture is clean and near-white: no yellow, no beige, no decay,
no dark spots, no missing teeth, no red or swollen gums, no blood. Draw teeth simply and
illustratively — a soft row, never a photorealistic mouth interior, never individually
outlined in heavy black.

STYLE — Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration, light and airy. Linework in
warm dark brown or soft charcoal, NEVER pure black: thin, hand-drawn, the weight varies,
strokes taper and sometimes break. Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light
marker, flat fills with two or three tones per hue and no smooth decorative gradients,
FILLED IN rather than left as a pale wash — every person, garment, food and object carries
its own solid colour, and bare paper is left only in the background and at the edges of the
picture. Colour edges a little loose, not always meeting the
line. A fine even paper grain over the whole image. Nothing is glossy, nothing is rendered
in 3D, nothing carries a photographic highlight.

COLOUR — Light, muted and gentle: pale cream and off-white as the ground, warm sand, dusty
sage, soft warm grey, pale butter yellow, muted clay, soft terracotta. MUTED BUT FULLY LAID IN: every hue
sits one step deeper than a pastel, warm and solid, so the picture reads as a properly
coloured illustration and NEVER as a pale line drawing with a thin wash over it. Skin,
hair, clothes, wood and food are all clearly coloured. Still gentle — no neon, no harsh
colour, no dark heavy shadows. Colour throughout, never greyscale.
The ONE accent colour of this picture is a muted mid blue, used sparingly and only where
the scene description names it. Everything else stays in the warm neutral family. Do NOT
wash the whole picture in blue, do NOT use a bright or electric blue, and do NOT let blue
touch skin, faces or the sky.

CRITICAL — NO WRITING ANYWHERE IN THE IMAGE. No text, letters, words, numbers, logos,
signage, captions, labels, watermarks, price tags, menus, screen interfaces, app icons,
buttons or numeral clock faces, in any language. Cups, packaging, bags, shopfronts,
posters, clothing and every screen carry no printing of any kind. Where writing would
normally appear, leave the surface plain.

AVOID — greyscale; photorealism; 3D rendering; glossy digital painting; anime or manga
styling with large glossy eyes; thick uniform black outlines; chrome, metallic or
iridescent gradients; any recognisable branded, franchise or licensed character, mascot,
figurine or logo; any before-and-after or good-versus-bad comparison; a smile chart or a
row of graded smiles; any panel borders, gutters, frames or comic-strip divisions; speech
balloons, thought bubbles, arrows, circles, spotlights or labels pointing at anyone's
mouth; a dental clinic treatment room; a reclining dental chair; an overhead operating
lamp; a drill, syringe, needle, probe or tray of instruments; plaster casts or a tray of
dental models (the two clear aligner trays and their small
cases described above are the exception — they must be in the picture, and they are the
ONLY dental objects in it); hands inside a mouth; a photorealistic open mouth or mouth
interior; blood, swelling, pain, fear or embarrassment; anyone crying, sulking or covering
their face in shame; anyone looking at the viewer; middle-aged or elderly people; any young man with his arm
around a young woman's shoulders or waist.
ALSO AVOID — a magazine, a leaflet, a poster or a phone notification advertising anything;
any pop-up, banner or message on any screen; two appliances laid side by side like
products being compared; a tick, a cross, a star or any mark that says one thing is better
than another; a tooth diagram or dental chart anywhere in the picture.
