Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape.

SCENE — ONE SINGLE CONTINUOUS STREET, drawn straight on, like a long side elevation of a
Taiwanese shop arcade on a warm afternoon. The pavement runs unbroken from the left edge
of the picture to the right edge, the ceiling of the arcade runs unbroken above it, and
square concrete pillars stand at even intervals along it. The pillars are simply part of
the street.
CRITICAL — THIS IS NOT A COMIC STRIP. There are no panel borders, no gutters, no dividing
lines, no frames and no boxes anywhere. It is one continuous space, one continuous floor,
one continuous ceiling, one continuous light.

THE SAME GIRL APPEARS THREE TIMES along that street, walking from left to right through
one afternoon. She is about nineteen, a student: shoulder-length hair tucked behind one
ear, a canvas backpack over one shoulder, a soft knitted top and wide trousers, white
trainers. SHE MUST BE UNMISTAKABLY THE SAME PERSON IN ALL THREE PLACES — identical hair,
identical clothes, identical bag, identical build and face. Nobody else in the picture
resembles her.

FIRST, AT THE LEFT — outside a small drinks stall, she stands with two friends of her own
age. All three are laughing at something one of them just said. HER TWO FRIENDS LAUGH WITH
THEIR MOUTHS OPEN; SHE LAUGHS WITH THE BACK OF ONE HAND RAISED IN FRONT OF HER MOUTH,
covering her teeth completely — an automatic habit, not shyness. Her eyes are crinkled and
happy, her shoulders are up; she is having a good time. No sadness, no tears, no blush of
shame, no turning away from her friends.

SECOND, IN THE MIDDLE — she stands alone, stopped still in front of a small dental clinic
on the same arcade. Her friends are gone. She is facing the clinic's glass door, seen from
behind and slightly to the side, both hands holding the strap of her bag in front of her,
weight on one foot, her head tilted very slightly up toward the door. She has clearly been
standing there a moment longer than she meant to. Show hesitation only through her posture
and stillness — she is not frowning, not chewing her lip, not clutching herself, not
retreating.

THIRD, AT THE RIGHT — the same girl again, now pushing that same glass door open with one
hand and stepping through it, her body half inside. Warm light spills out of the doorway
across the pavement. Through the glass we see just a little of a calm, tidy waiting area:
a low counter, a bench with a cushion, a potted plant, and a dentist in a muted blue top
standing near the counter, turning toward her — a small distant figure, drawn simply. NO
treatment room, no dental chair, no operating lamp, no instruments are visible anywhere.
Her posture here is settled and ordinary; she is not hesitating any more.

READING ORDER — Because the three moments share one street, the eye must run left to
right: keep the pavement, the shop fronts and the ceiling line continuous behind all three
of her, and let the warm afternoon light fall the same way across the whole width. Do not
change the time of day between them, and do not tint one part of the street differently
from another.

THE STREET ITSELF — Furnish it fully so no surface is left blank for invention: the drinks
stall at the left with a counter, a stack of plain cups and a jug; between the stalls a
parked scooter seen from behind, a stack of two crates, a potted plant beside a pillar, a
folded step-stool, a rolled-up awning; the clinic front with plain glass, a slim door
handle, a low planter beside it; a strip of sunlit road along the bottom edge and the
green tops of two street trees beyond. Hang no signs, no banners and no shop names
anywhere — every fascia and every window stays plain. No other pedestrians crowd the
picture; at most one or two distant figures, and none of them resembles her.

AIR AND LIGHT — Warm low sun from the right, throwing long soft shadows along the
pavement. Where she pushes the door, put THREE OR FOUR SHORT PARALLEL WHITE HAND-DRAWN
STROKES at the same slant just behind her shoulder — solid where they begin, thinning to
dry flecks — standing for the small movement of stepping forward. They are quick short
strokes, never one long ribbon, never curling or looping, never smoke or steam. Use no
other marks and no cartoon symbols.
THE ACCENT BLUE goes in exactly three places: the clinic's door frame, the dentist's top
inside, and one of the drinks stall's stools.

PEOPLE — Simplified illustrative people, drawn as East Asian teenagers and young adults:
minimal features (small dot or short-line eyes, a simple line mouth, a small nose),
natural realistic head-to-body proportions, believable ages. Not photorealistic, not
faceless, no oversized heads, no noodle limbs, and NO large glossy anime eyes. Everyone in
this picture is between about fifteen and twenty-five years old — school and university
age. There are no middle-aged adults, no parents, no children and no elderly people
anywhere in the image.
NOBODY LOOKS AT THE VIEWER. Every face is turned toward something inside the picture.

CLOTHING — clothes are NEVER flat single-tone shapes. Model each garment with two or three
tones of its own colour: soft coloured-pencil shading in the folds, along the sleeves,
under the collar and at the hem, with the real details drawn — collar, cuffs, sleeve seam,
the ribbing at a cuff, the knit of a jumper, the drawstring of a hoodie. Everyone wears a
clearly different colour; no two people wear the same hue.

TEETH — Every tooth in this picture is clean and near-white: no yellow, no beige, no decay,
no dark spots, no missing teeth, no red or swollen gums, no blood. Draw teeth simply and
illustratively — a soft row, never a photorealistic mouth interior, never individually
outlined in heavy black.

STYLE — Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration, light and airy. Linework in
warm dark brown or soft charcoal, NEVER pure black: thin, hand-drawn, the weight varies,
strokes taper and sometimes break. Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light
marker, flat fills with two or three tones per hue and no smooth decorative gradients,
with plenty of the pale paper left showing through — large parts of the ground, the walls
and the air stay almost bare paper. Colour edges a little loose, not always meeting the
line. A fine even paper grain over the whole image. Nothing is glossy, nothing is rendered
in 3D, nothing carries a photographic highlight.

COLOUR — Light, muted and gentle: pale cream and off-white as the ground, warm sand, dusty
sage, soft warm grey, pale butter yellow, muted clay, soft terracotta. Desaturated
throughout — nothing heavy, no neon, no dark heavy shadows. Colour throughout, never
greyscale.
The ONE accent colour of this picture is a muted mid blue, used sparingly and only where
the scene description names it. Everything else stays in the warm neutral family. Do NOT
wash the whole picture in blue, do NOT use a bright or electric blue, and do NOT let blue
touch skin, faces or the sky.

CRITICAL — NO WRITING ANYWHERE IN THE IMAGE. No text, letters, words, numbers, logos,
signage, captions, labels, watermarks, price tags, menus, screen interfaces, app icons,
buttons or numeral clock faces, in any language. Cups, packaging, bags, shopfronts,
posters, clothing and every screen carry no printing of any kind. Where writing would
normally appear, leave the surface plain.

AVOID — greyscale; photorealism; 3D rendering; glossy digital painting; anime or manga
styling with large glossy eyes; thick uniform black outlines; chrome, metallic or
iridescent gradients; any recognisable branded, franchise or licensed character, mascot,
figurine or logo; any before-and-after or good-versus-bad comparison; a smile chart or a
row of graded smiles; any panel borders, gutters, frames or comic-strip divisions; speech
balloons, thought bubbles, arrows, circles, spotlights or labels pointing at anyone's
mouth; a dental clinic treatment room; a reclining dental chair; an overhead operating
lamp; a drill, syringe, needle, probe or tray of instruments; dental models, plaster casts
or appliances lying on a table; hands inside a mouth; a photorealistic open mouth or mouth
interior; blood, swelling, pain, fear or embarrassment; anyone crying, sulking or covering
their face in shame; anyone looking at the viewer; middle-aged or elderly people.
