Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape.

SCENE — One single scene, no panels, no frames, no dividing lines. Early evening, an
ordinary home. THREE people are having dinner together — and the picture is built around
ONE of them.

THE FRAMING — Come in CLOSE. This is a near view from across the table, cropped at about
chest height: the main figure's head and shoulders take up roughly the middle third of the
width and her head reaches into the upper third of the picture. Her face must be large and
easy to read. The table is only the lower part of the picture — the near edge of it runs
along the bottom, and no more than the front half of the tabletop is visible. Do NOT draw
the whole round table, do NOT pull back to show the whole room, and do NOT draw the chairs
and the floor. The room is only a soft, simple background behind them.

THE MAIN FIGURE — DEAD CENTRE OF THE PICTURE: a young woman of about twenty, facing us at
three-quarters, her head turned slightly toward the friend on her left so she is still in
the conversation. She is smiling and relaxed, mid-sentence. HER TEETH LOOK COMPLETELY
ORDINARY: no brackets, no wire, nothing visible on them.
WHAT SHE IS DOING — held up in front of her chest, well clear of the table and roughly at
the level of her collarbone, SHE HOLDS ONE THIN TRANSPARENT ALIGNER between the finger and
thumb of one hand, and with her other hand she holds A SMALL OPEN CLAM-SHELL CASE, the
tray about to go into it. BOTH THE TRAY AND THE CASE ARE DRAWN LARGE AND CLEARLY — the
tray at least as wide as her hand, the case a little wider — because they sit at the exact
centre of the picture and they are the second thing the eye finds after her face.
THE TRAY MUST READ AS TRANSPARENT: a slim horseshoe-shaped shell of clear plastic moulded
to the shape of a row of teeth, drawn with a single fine outline and a soft glassy tint,
the background showing faintly through it. No metal, no wire, no brackets, no colour, never
milky, never frosted, never solid white.
SHE IS NOT PERFORMING THIS. She is not holding it up for anyone to see, not showing it to
the others, not looking at it, not proud of it and not apologetic about it — she is
looking at her friend and talking while her hands do something she does every day. She is
NOT eating right now: no chopsticks in her hands, no bowl at her mouth, no food being
picked up by her.

THE OTHER TWO — one on each side of her, cropped by the left and right edges of the
picture so we see only part of them: a young man of about twenty-two on one side, half
turned toward her, laughing at what she just said; a young woman of about nineteen on the
other side, reaching in toward the dishes with her chopsticks, head turned away toward the
food. They are clearly further back and partly cut off. They must NOT be the same size as
her, must NOT be centred, and NEITHER OF THEM IS WATCHING HER HANDS. Nobody points, nobody
looks curious, nobody has stopped to look. It is not an event.

THE TABLE — Only the front half shows, along the bottom of the picture, and the food on it
is real but simple: a plate of stir-fried green vegetables, a plate of sliced meat, a
covered soup pot with a ladle and a few short strokes of steam, two bowls of rice,
chopsticks resting on a rest. All of it is drawn smaller and looser than she is, and
nothing on the table is brighter or more detailed than her face and her hands.

THE ROOM BEHIND — Kept simple and soft, just enough to say "home": a low sideboard along
the wall with a rice cooker and a stack of clean bowls, a small potted plant, a warm
ceiling light hanging just inside the top edge, and a framed picture whose content is an
ABSTRACT LANDSCAPE — three or four soft overlapping bands of muted colour suggesting
hills, with no writing, no teeth, no faces and no figures. Nothing else. Leave no blank
wall panel for invention and hang no signs, no calendars and no writing of any kind.

THE NOISE — Put THREE OR FOUR SHORT PARALLEL WHITE HAND-DRAWN STROKES at the same slant in
the air near the laughing friend, solid where they begin and thinning to dry flecks at the
far end, standing for the talking going on around her. Small and quiet; never one long
continuous ribbon, never curling or looping, never crossing anyone's face, never smoke,
and no musical notes, stars or other cartoon symbols.
THE ACCENT BLUE goes in exactly two places: the aligner case, and one bowl's rim pattern.

COLOUR WEIGHT — Keep the palette muted, but FILL IT IN: every garment, the food, the wood
and the skin are properly coloured, each with two or three tones of its own hue. This
picture must not read as a pale line drawing with a wash over it — the colour should sit
one step deeper than a pastel, warm and solid, while still being desaturated and gentle.
Never neon, never harsh, never dark.
PEOPLE — Simplified illustrative people, drawn as East Asian teenagers and young adults:
minimal features (small dot or short-line eyes, a simple line mouth, a small nose),
natural realistic head-to-body proportions, believable ages. Not photorealistic, not
faceless, no oversized heads, no noodle limbs, and NO large glossy anime eyes. Everyone in
this picture is between about fifteen and twenty-five years old — school and university
age. There are no middle-aged adults, no parents, no children and no elderly people
anywhere in the image.
NOBODY LOOKS AT THE VIEWER. Every face is turned toward something inside the picture.

CLOTHING — clothes are NEVER flat single-tone shapes. Model each garment with two or three
tones of its own colour: soft coloured-pencil shading in the folds, along the sleeves,
under the collar and at the hem, with the real details drawn — collar, cuffs, sleeve seam,
the ribbing at a cuff, the knit of a jumper, the drawstring of a hoodie. Everyone wears a
clearly different colour; no two people wear the same hue.

TEETH — Every tooth in this picture is clean and near-white: no yellow, no beige, no decay,
no dark spots, no missing teeth, no red or swollen gums, no blood. Draw teeth simply and
illustratively — a soft row, never a photorealistic mouth interior, never individually
outlined in heavy black.

STYLE — Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration, light and airy. Linework in
warm dark brown or soft charcoal, NEVER pure black: thin, hand-drawn, the weight varies,
strokes taper and sometimes break. Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light
marker, flat fills with two or three tones per hue and no smooth decorative gradients,
with plenty of the pale paper left showing through — large parts of the ground, the walls
and the air stay almost bare paper. Colour edges a little loose, not always meeting the
line. A fine even paper grain over the whole image. Nothing is glossy, nothing is rendered
in 3D, nothing carries a photographic highlight.

COLOUR — Light, muted and gentle: pale cream and off-white as the ground, warm sand, dusty
sage, soft warm grey, pale butter yellow, muted clay, soft terracotta. Desaturated
throughout — nothing heavy, no neon, no dark heavy shadows. Colour throughout, never
greyscale.
The ONE accent colour of this picture is a muted mid blue, used sparingly and only where
the scene description names it. Everything else stays in the warm neutral family. Do NOT
wash the whole picture in blue, do NOT use a bright or electric blue, and do NOT let blue
touch skin, faces or the sky.

CRITICAL — NO WRITING ANYWHERE IN THE IMAGE. No text, letters, words, numbers, logos,
signage, captions, labels, watermarks, price tags, menus, screen interfaces, app icons,
buttons or numeral clock faces, in any language. Cups, packaging, bags, shopfronts,
posters, clothing and every screen carry no printing of any kind. Where writing would
normally appear, leave the surface plain.

AVOID — greyscale; photorealism; 3D rendering; glossy digital painting; anime or manga
styling with large glossy eyes; thick uniform black outlines; chrome, metallic or
iridescent gradients; any recognisable branded, franchise or licensed character, mascot,
figurine or logo; any before-and-after or good-versus-bad comparison; a smile chart or a
row of graded smiles; any panel borders, gutters, frames or comic-strip divisions; speech
balloons, thought bubbles, arrows, circles, spotlights or labels pointing at anyone's
mouth; a dental clinic treatment room; a reclining dental chair; an overhead operating
lamp; a drill, syringe, needle, probe or tray of instruments; plaster casts or a tray of
dental models (the clear aligner tray and its small case described above are the
exception — they must be in the picture, and they are the ONLY dental objects in it); hands inside a mouth; a photorealistic open mouth or mouth
interior; blood, swelling, pain, fear or embarrassment; anyone crying, sulking or covering
their face in shame; anyone looking at the viewer; middle-aged or elderly people.
ALSO AVOID — a magazine, a leaflet, a poster or a phone notification advertising anything;
any pop-up, banner or message on any screen; two appliances laid side by side like
products being compared; a tick, a cross, a star or any mark that says one thing is better
than another; a tooth diagram or dental chart anywhere in the picture.
