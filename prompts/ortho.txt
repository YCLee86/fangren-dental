Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape.

LAYOUT — ONE image divided into THREE areas by thin hand-drawn lines — lines only, in the
same warm dark brown as the rest of the linework, thin and slightly uneven. No outer border,
no boxes, no gutters, no arrows.
  • THE LEFT HALF, full height: a small Taiwanese breakfast shop.
  • THE TOP RIGHT quarter: the dinner table.
  • THE BOTTOM RIGHT quarter: the bedroom at night.
ABSOLUTELY NO PANEL LABELS. Do not write A, B or C, do not number the areas, do not put a
letter, caption, title or small white tag in any corner. No writing of any kind.
THESE ARE THREE MOMENTS FROM ONE ORDINARY WEEK, NOT A COMPARISON.

ONE SINGLE DRAWING STYLE ACROSS ALL THREE AREAS — this matters as much as anything else in
this brief. The three areas must look as though the same hand drew them in the same sitting:
the SAME thin, light, even line weight; the SAME degree of simplification in every face; the
SAME figure scale relative to each area; the SAME soft, pale colouring. THE LEFT AREA MUST NOT
BECOME BOLDER, HEAVIER, DARKER-LINED, LARGER-HEADED, MORE CARTOONISH OR MORE ZOOMED-IN THAN
THE OTHER TWO. If one area starts to look like a different illustration, it is wrong.

NOBODY APPEARS TWICE — the five friends at the breakfast shop and the four people at the
dinner table are NINE DIFFERENT PEOPLE. The young man in the DEEP TEAL-GREEN T-SHIRT who
appears at the dinner table and again asleep at night MUST NOT APPEAR IN THE BREAKFAST SHOP
AREA, and NOBODY AT THE BREAKFAST SHOP WEARS GREEN.

THE LEFT HALF — FIXED METAL BRACES, A GROUP PHOTO
============================================================
Morning at a small Taiwanese breakfast shop under a shop arcade. FIVE friends aged about
seventeen to twenty-two are STANDING together in a tight, casual huddle just outside the
counter, taking a photo of themselves.

HOW THE SELFIE IS TAKEN — THIS WAS DRAWN WRONG BEFORE, SO FOLLOW IT EXACTLY.
The young man with the braces is taking the picture FOR THE WHOLE GROUP, the way anyone takes
a group selfie:
  • HIS ARM IS RAISED UP AND FORWARD, elbow bent, NOT stretched down and NOT reaching into a
    corner of the picture.
  • THE PHONE IS HELD AT ABOUT THE HEIGHT OF HIS OWN FACE — level with his eyes or a little
    above — and CLOSE TO THE GROUP, just in front of them.
  • THE PHONE IS TILTED BACK TOWARD ALL FIVE OF THEM so that everyone is in shot. We see the
    BACK of the phone; its camera faces the friends.
  • His hand and the whole phone are inside the picture, near the upper left of the cluster.
HE IS NOT HANDING THE PHONE TO ANYONE, NOT passing it, NOT offering it, NOT holding it out
away from the group, NOT pointing it at the viewer, NOT reaching toward the bottom corner of
the picture. HIS OTHER HAND IS EMPTY AND RELAXED — he is not holding a drink, a cup or
anything else.

THE COMPOSITION:
  • THE FIVE FRIENDS STAND TOGETHER AS ONE TIGHT CLUSTER IN THE LEFT-CENTRE FOREGROUND, seen
    from about the waist or the thigh up, their heads around the middle height of the area,
    their faces clearly readable but drawn at the same simplified scale as the people in the
    other two areas. ALL FIVE FACES ARE VISIBLE, none hidden behind another.
  • EVERY FACE IS TURNED TOWARD THE RAISED PHONE, so nobody looks out at the viewer.
  • THERE IS EXACTLY ONE COUNTER IN THIS SCENE. It runs away from the group toward the UPPER
    LEFT, with the owner behind it. NO second stall, NO second counter, NO counter in the
    foreground, NO griddle in the bottom corner, and nothing between us and the friends.
  • BEHIND AND TO THE RIGHT the arcade recedes into the distance, ending in a strip of sunlit
    street along the upper right with the road beyond.

THIS IS A GROUP PHOTO AND EVERY ONE OF THEM IS POSING FOR IT: two of them make a peace sign
near the face or chest, one leans in over another's shoulder to get into the shot, one holds a
drink up beside her head, one tilts her head against a friend's. They press together into a
tight bunch, heads at different heights, all angled toward the phone.
THE ENERGY OF THIS MOMENT IS THE MOST IMPORTANT THING IN THE PICTURE — this is the loudest
second of their morning. EVERY ONE OF THEM IS ACTIVELY LAUGHING: mouths open, eyes crinkled or
squeezed shut, cheeks up, shoulders raised. NOBODY stands upright with a closed, polite little
smile; NOBODY is still. Feet stay on the ground — nobody kicks a leg up, nobody jumps, nobody
throws an arm high in the air.

WHAT THEY ARE HOLDING — Taiwanese breakfast-shop drinks and nothing else: TALL SEALED PLASTIC
CUPS OF ICED TEA OR SOY MILK WITH A STRAW PUSHED THROUGH THE LID, held by two of the friends
(never by the one taking the photo). NO coffee cup, NO mug, NO cup and saucer, NO teacup, NO
paper coffee cup anywhere in this area.

THE COLOURS THEY WEAR — only ONE person wears a strong colour:
  • ONE mustard-yellow hooded sweatshirt — the young man taking the photo.
  • THE OTHERS: a WHITE TEE WITH FINE PALE-BLUE STRIPES, a plain white T-shirt, a cream
    knitted jumper, a soft off-white blouse.
  • Below the waist: faded PALE BLUE DENIM JEANS and pale beige trousers.
NO RED, BRICK, RUST, TERRACOTTA, CORAL, PINK, ORANGE OR GREEN anywhere on these five people.

THE ONE COUNTER — along it, in a single row: a hot griddle at the far end with the owner
working at it, a tray of TOASTED SANDWICHES cut into triangles, a tray of egg crepe rolls, two
or three clear plastic boxes of green salad, a stack of clean cups, a jug, and several sealed
plastic cups of tea and soy milk with straws. TWO OR THREE PALE BLUE ROUND STOOLS stand along
it. All packaging is completely plain; no menu boards and no signs anywhere.

THE BACKGROUND — behind the group, EXACTLY THREE living things and nothing else: the OWNER
behind the counter in a blue apron with his head down at the griddle; ONE person walking away
along the arcade, seen from behind; ONE scooter on the road beyond.

WHO THEY ARE:

  1. THE ONE TAKING THE PHOTO — at the left of the cluster, a young man of about eighteen with
     SOFT, THICK, SLIGHTLY TOUSLED SHORT HAIR, in the MUSTARD-YELLOW HOODED SWEATSHIRT with a
     drawstring and a front pocket, his raised hand holding the phone as described above.
     HIS EYES — HE IS LAUGHING SO HARD HIS EYES HAVE SQUEEZED ALMOST SHUT. Draw them as TWO
     UPWARD-CURVING CRESCENTS, closed happy arcs, with the cheeks pushed up underneath. NOT
     round open eyes, NOT dots, NOT wide, NOT staring.
     HIS SMILE — HIS MOUTH IS NOT OPEN. HIS UPPER AND LOWER TEETH ARE TOUCHING, LIGHTLY BITING
     TOGETHER, and his lips are pulled back hard and wide across them in a broad grin. Because
     the teeth are closed, BOTH THE UPPER ROW AND THE LOWER ROW ARE SHOWN, meeting along a
     clean horizontal line in the middle of the smile. THERE IS NO DARK GAP INSIDE HIS MOUTH —
     no open mouth, no tongue, no throat, no head tipped back. Think of someone saying a long
     "eeee" for a photograph while laughing with their eyes shut.
     HIS BRACES ARE ON BOTH ARCHES AND BOTH MUST BE EQUALLY VISIBLE. Upper front teeth: FIVE OR
     SIX DISTINCT LITTLE SQUARES, one per tooth, each with its own outline and a visible gap of
     clean white tooth between neighbours, joined by ONE THIN STRAIGHT HORIZONTAL WIRE. Lower
     front teeth: THE SAME AGAIN, drawn just as clearly, immediately below the line where the
     teeth meet.
     IF ONLY ONE ROW OF BRACKETS IS VISIBLE, OR HIS MOUTH IS OPEN, OR HIS EYES ARE ROUND AND
     OPEN, THE DRAWING IS WRONG.
     BESIDE HIS HEAD, PUT A GROUP OF WHITE HAND-DRAWN STROKES — three or four short strokes
     fanning out from just beside his temple and cheek, on the side away from the phone.
     NEVER draw the braces as dots, specks or stippling; never let the squares merge into a
     grey band; never shiny metal. Draw them at the same delicate line weight as everything
     else — if they cannot be drawn clearly at this size, draw FEWER squares, slightly larger,
     but never heavier or blacker.

  2. THE ONE WHO HAS NOT STARTED — near the middle of the cluster, a girl of about seventeen,
     hair in a high ponytail, a bag strap across her chest, in a soft OFF-WHITE blouse. SHE IS
     GENUINELY LAUGHING — eyes crinkled into happy curves, cheeks raised, shoulders up — BUT
     THE BACK OF ONE HAND IS RAISED IN FRONT OF HER MOUTH, covering her teeth completely, an
     automatic habit she does not notice. Happy, not sad.

  3. THE SECOND ONE IN TREATMENT — a little further back on the right, a young woman of about
     twenty in a CREAM KNITTED JUMPER, laughing hard with her mouth open, also in FIXED BRACES
     on both arches, drawn exactly like his.
     THAT IS ALL SHE HAS — nothing else in or around her mouth: no elastic, no rubber band, no
     thread, no line joining upper teeth to lower teeth, nothing hanging, nothing on her lips,
     cheeks, chin or jaw. Keep her smaller and less prominent than the young man at the left.

  4. A young man of about nineteen in a plain WHITE T-SHIRT, mouth open in a laugh, making a
     peace sign beside his cheek.

  5. A young woman of about twenty-two in the WHITE TEE WITH FINE PALE-BLUE STRIPES, mid-laugh,
     holding a tall sealed plastic cup of iced tea with a straw up beside her head.

HOW THEY STAND — friendly but not clingy. The two young men may stand shoulder to shoulder or
rest an arm across each other's shoulders. NO ONE PUTS AN ARM AROUND ANY OF THE YOUNG WOMEN.

THE TOP RIGHT AREA — A CLEAR ALIGNER COMES OUT BEFORE DINNER
============================================================
Early evening in a BUSY neighbourhood eating house, seen from slightly ABOVE at a raised

THIS ROOM IS FULL AND ALIVE — behind them, drawn smaller and softer but clearly there: TWO OR

THE BOTTOM RIGHT AREA — THE SAME ALIGNER, WORN OVERNIGHT
============================================================
The middle of the night, a small bedroom, quiet and dim. The walls and bedding fall into a soft
neutral grey-blue dusk. Low key but clean and luminous, never murky, never flat black.

THE ARRANGEMENT — build this scene in exactly this order, left to right:
  1. THE BEDSIDE TABLE at the LEFT, close to us. On it: the SMALL LAMP at the BACK, the CLOSED
     ROUND CASE in FRONT of the lamp — directly below the lampshade, nearest to us — and a

  2. IMMEDIATELY TO THE RIGHT is THE HEAD OF THE BED: the pillow lies right there next to the
     table, and THE HEADBOARD IS ON THAT SAME LEFT SIDE, rising behind the pillow. THE
     HEADBOARD IS NOT ON THE RIGHT.
  3. THE BED runs away to the RIGHT across the rest of the area, the quilt covering it.
  4. THE SLEEPING MAN lies with HIS HEAD ON THAT PILLOW at the left and HIS BODY EXTENDING TO
     THE RIGHT under the quilt.
We see the whole of him from the top of his head to about his waist, and HIS BODY IS WHOLE AND
CONTINUOUS.

THE LAMP AND ITS LIGHT — the only light source, SHINING DOWNWARD. A soft warm cone of light
falls from UNDER the shade onto the table top, lighting the closed case and the glass, and

THE SLEEPING MAN IS THE SAME PERSON AS THE ONE AT THE DINNER TABLE: the SAME medium-short thick

He is asleep on his side in an ORDINARY COMFORTABLE POSTURE: cheek resting directly on the

HIS EXPRESSION IS PEACEFUL AND CONTENT — brows smooth and level, the faintest soft upward curve
at the corner of his mouth.
HIS MOUTH IS CLOSED and his lips sit slightly FULL AND PUSHED FORWARD, a little rounded. That is

THE CLOSED CASE — one smooth blue disc, a shut compact seen from slightly above, its lid down,

THE WHITE STROKES
============================================================
HAND-DRAWN WHITE STROKES laid ON TOP of the colour, and there must be PLENTY OF THEM. They read
as chalk or white pencil: solid where each stroke begins and thinning to dry flecks at its end.
Always a GROUP of three to five short strokes together, running the same way, evenly spaced.
They may curve gently — but never straighten into a ruled line and never grow into one long
continuous ribbon, spiral, loop or wisp.
WHERE THEY GO — the breakfast shop needs the most: FIVE OR SIX separate groups, at different
angles and sizes. ONE OF THOSE GROUPS MUST SIT DIRECTLY BESIDE THE HEAD OF THE YOUNG MAN IN THE
MUSTARD HOODIE. The others go beside the other laughing heads, at the raised drink and along a
swinging bag strap. THREE OR FOUR more in the dinner area — across the table, beside the staff
member's tray, and rising above the soup pot as steam, drawn as the same short curved strokes.
The night area is SILENT and has NO white strokes at all.
They must NOT cross the dividing lines, NOT cross anyone's face, and NEVER look like smoke, mist
or a spirit. No musical notes, stars, exclamation marks or speed lines anywhere.

ACROSS ALL THREE AREAS
============================================================
PEOPLE — Simplified illustrative people, all East Asian: minimal features (small dot or
short-line eyes, a simple line mouth, a small nose), natural realistic head-to-body proportions,
believable ages. Not photorealistic, not faceless, no oversized heads, no noodle limbs, NO large
glossy anime eyes. Everyone is between about fifteen and twenty-five, except the shop owner and
the restaurant staff, who may be a little older but never elderly. No children.
EVERY FACE IS FRIENDLY OR CONTENT. NOBODY LOOKS AT THE VIEWER.
KEEP THE FACES SIMPLE: eyes as small dots, short curved lines or closed happy crescents, a
simple line mouth, a small nose, cheeks as a soft flat blush at most. NO eyelashes, NO shine in
the eyes, NO detailed irises, NO shading blended over the skin, NO nose shadow, NO lip line, NO
cheekbone modelling, NO realistic hair strands. EVERY PERSON IN ALL THREE AREAS IS DRAWN WITH
EXACTLY THE SAME SIMPLIFIED FACE STYLE AND THE SAME LINE WEIGHT.

CLOTHING — clothes are NEVER flat single-tone shapes. Model each garment with two or three tones
of its own colour: soft coloured-pencil shading in the folds, along the sleeves, under the
collar and at the hem, with real details drawn — collar, cuffs, sleeve seam, ribbing, the knit
of a jumper, the drawstring and pocket of a hoodie. Where several people wear pale neutrals,
tell them apart by texture, cut and neckline rather than by colour.

TEETH — Every tooth is clean and near-white: no yellow, no beige, no decay, no dark spots, no
missing teeth, no red or swollen gums, no blood. Draw teeth simply and illustratively — a soft
row, never a photorealistic mouth interior, never individually outlined in heavy black.

STYLE — Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration. Linework in warm dark brown or
soft charcoal, NEVER pure black, and always THIN AND LIGHT: hand-drawn, the weight varies a
little, strokes taper and sometimes break, but the line never becomes bold, thick or graphic.
Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light marker, flat fills with two or three tones
per hue and no smooth decorative gradients, FILLED IN rather than left as a pale wash, with bare
paper only at the very edges. Colour edges a little loose, not always meeting the line. A fine
even paper grain over the whole image. Nothing is glossy, nothing is rendered in 3D, nothing
carries a photographic highlight. The airy high-key treatment applies to the breakfast shop and
the dinner; the night area is genuinely dark and low key — clean and luminous, but plainly
darker, never murky or black.

COLOUR — Warm, muted and gentle everywhere the signature blue is not: pale cream and off-white
as the ground, warm sand, soft warm grey, pale butter yellow. MUTED BUT FULLY LAID IN: every hue
sits one step deeper than a pastel, warm and solid, never a pale line drawing with a thin wash
over it. SKIN STAYS VERY PALE AND LIGHT, and the SIGNATURE BLUE STAYS LIGHT. No neon, no harsh
colour, no dark heavy shadows. Colour throughout, never greyscale.

CRITICAL — NO WRITING ANYWHERE IN THE IMAGE. No text, letters, words, numbers, logos, signage,
captions, panel labels, corner tags, watermarks, price tags, menus, screen interfaces, app
icons, buttons or numeral clock faces, in any language.

bigger heads, coarser faces, a more cartoonish look, or figures at a larger scale than the
