Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape. One single scene, no panels, no borders, no frames.

STYLE — READ THIS FIRST; IT MATTERS MORE THAN ANY OTHER SECTION.
Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration, hand-made throughout. Linework in
warm dark brown or soft charcoal, NEVER pure black: thin, hand-drawn, the weight visibly
varies along a stroke, strokes taper and sometimes break and run dry at the end — never an
even mechanical vector line. Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light marker;
colour edges a little loose and not always meeting the line. Flat fills with two or three
tones per hue — no smooth decorative gradients, no airbrush, no glow, no neon, no
cel-shading. A fine even paper grain over every surface of the whole image, including the
background. The picture is high-key and warm: plenty of pale cream paper showing through,
but every named object still carries a real colour. This is NOT anime, NOT manga, NOT a
screenshot from a cartoon, NOT 3D rendering.

FACES AND HANDS — READ THIS SECOND.
SKIN IS THE ONE EXCEPTION to the two-or-three-tones rule: each face and each hand is ONE
single flat warm tone, with no modelling, no blush, no shading, no cheekbones and no
jawline. On a face, draw ONLY these six things: the outline of the head, the eyes, the
eyebrows, the nose, the mouth and the ears. Nothing else at all — no wrinkles, no lines
beside the nose, no lines under the eyes, no cheek shadow, no neck shading, no highlight
in the eye. Eyes are small solid dots or short lines — never large glossy cartoon eyes,
never white eyeballs with a shine. Hands are simple: four fingers and a thumb, no knuckle
lines, no nails.
BOTH PEOPLE'S HAIR IS THE SAME WARM DARK BROWN WITH A RED CAST — #483837, its shadowed
side #392928, its lit edge #4f4040 — however long the hair is. It is never pure black,
never neutral grey-black, and never blonde, caramel, light brown, auburn, grey, silver or
white.
THE TWO PEOPLE ARE CLEARLY TWO DIFFERENT PEOPLE: different face, different hairstyle,
different age, different expression. Both are East Asian. Both are drawn by the same hand
at the same level of simplicity — nobody is a flat grey silhouette, a shadow or an empty
outline.

THE SCENE — A BRIGHT, CALM CONSULTATION CORNER OF A SMALL DENTAL CLINIC. A woman dentist
and a man in his forties sit at the same side-on desk, turned towards each other and
towards a screen that stands on the desk between them, angled so that BOTH of them and WE
can see its face. They are looking at the screen together. The camera is a little to the
side and slightly above the desk, so both people are seen three-quarters from behind the
shoulder and NEITHER OF THEM LOOKS AT THE VIEWER. The mood is unhurried, warm and
explanatory — two people working something out together. This is not a treatment scene:
nobody is lying down, there is no rubber dam, no microscope, no gloves and no mask.

THE SCREEN IS THE SUBJECT OF THE PICTURE. It is a plain rectangular monitor on a simple
stand, its bezel a soft pale grey with no buttons and no markings, standing slightly LEFT
of the centre of the frame and filling roughly a third of the width of the whole picture.
Its face is warm cream, lit gently from within, and it is the brightest rectangle in the
image.
ON THE SCREEN, SIDE BY SIDE, ARE THREE DRAWINGS OF THE SAME SINGLE TOOTH SEEN IN
CROSS-SECTION — the same tooth at three different times, in the same position and at the
same size in all three, like three frames of one sequence. COPY THE SHAPES FROM THE
ATTACHED REFERENCE IMAGE EXACTLY, BUT NOT ITS FLAT DIAGRAM FINISH: redraw them in this
picture's own hand-made line and coloured-pencil colour, with the same paper grain as
everything else.
  • Each tooth is a lower molar with a rounded crown and TWO roots, its enamel warm white
    with a hand-drawn brown outline, set into a band of pale pink gum above a block of
    cream-coloured bone.
  • INSIDE each tooth is a soft rose-pink shape: a chamber inside the crown and two fine
    canals running from it down the whole length of both roots to the root tips.
  • THIS ROSE-PINK SHAPE IS WIDE IN THE FIRST DRAWING, CLEARLY NARROWER IN THE SECOND, AND
    ONLY A FINE THREAD IN THE THIRD. IT GETS THINNER ALONG ITS WHOLE LENGTH IN EVERY
    DRAWING — the chamber, the middle of each root and the tip all narrow together. It is
    never the case that only the upper part narrows while the lower part stays wide.
  • The space the pink used to occupy is filled in around it with a slightly warmer
    off-white, so that in the second and third drawings you can see new material has grown
    inwards from the walls.
  • IN THE THIRD DRAWING ONLY, the top of the chamber is roofed over by that same
    off-white, and where the two canal entrances used to be there are TWO SMALL SHALLOW
    DIMPLES IN A SLIGHTLY DARKER TONE — the entrances are COVERED OVER, not broken, not
    cracked, not decayed, and NOT black holes.
  • On the biting surface: the first tooth has one tiny brown pit; the second has a small
    pale grey filling; the third has a LARGE pale grey filling that reaches deep towards
    the chamber. Nothing else is drawn on any of the three teeth.
  • The three drawings sit on the warm cream screen with clear space between them. There
    is nothing else on the screen at all — no border, no panel, no toolbar, no icons, no
    scale bar, no arrows, no numbers and no writing of any kind.

THE TWO PEOPLE — both hands of each person are accounted for, and only one mouth is open.
  1. THE DENTIST — a woman in her thirties with her hair tied back, in SOFT DUSTY-ROSE
     SCRUBS (#d7b7b7, folds and shadows #b89999). She sits at the LEFT of the desk, leaning
     slightly forward towards the screen. HER RIGHT HAND is raised with the index finger
     extended, pointing at the THIRD, RIGHT-HAND drawing on the screen; HER LEFT HAND rests
     flat and relaxed on the desk. She looks at the screen. SHE IS THE ONLY PERSON IN THE
     PICTURE WITH AN OPEN MOUTH — a small open shape, mid-sentence, eyebrows relaxed, warm
     and matter-of-fact. She is explaining, not warning.
  2. THE PATIENT — a man in his forties with short neat hair, in a pale blue shirt
     (#a9bcd4, folds #8fa3bd) with the collar open. He sits at the RIGHT of the desk, turned
     towards the screen, shoulders down and relaxed, leaning in a little to look. BOTH HANDS
     are loosely clasped together on the desk in front of him. He looks at the screen. HIS
     MOUTH IS CLOSED, a small upward curve at one corner, and HIS EYEBROWS ARE SLIGHTLY
     RAISED — he has just understood something. HE IS NOT WORRIED, NOT FROWNING, NOT
     ANXIOUS, NOT IN PAIN AND NOT SAD.

THE ROOM — a real small clinic, not an empty white box. Draw all of these, kept simple and
low-contrast so they never compete with the screen: a pale wooden desk; two simple chairs;
behind them a wall in a soft off-white with a low sage-green painted band along the bottom;
a shelf of tidy folders in muted colours; a wall poster that carries A SIMPLE DRAWING OF A
TOOTH AND NOTHING ELSE — no words on it; a clear plastic model of a jaw on a small stand;
one potted plant with broad green leaves; a tall window at the far left with daylight
coming in. On the desk beside the screen: one small white tooth model and one plain pen
lying flat. Nothing else on the desk. Through a doorway at the right edge, the pale
mint-green arm of a dental chair is just visible.

LIGHT AND COLOUR — bright, even, midday daylight from the window at the left; one soft
warm light source only, no hard shadows, no lamplight, no sunset, no long orange shadows.
The coloured areas of the picture sit in the range HSL saturation 30–50 and lightness
70–85: clearly coloured, never washed out to near-white, never heavy or muddy. AT LEAST
SIX DIFFERENT COLOURS MUST BE VISIBLE AND EACH BELONGS TO A NAMED OBJECT: the dusty-rose
scrubs, the pale blue shirt, the sage-green wall band, the pale wooden desk, the green
plant, the cream screen, the mint-green chair arm. THE TWO PEOPLE DO NOT WEAR THE SAME
COLOUR AS EACH OTHER, and neither wears the colour of the wall behind them. The warm cream
screen is the lightest thing in the picture and the eye should go to it first.

THE ATMOSPHERE LINES — THREE OR FOUR LONG, THIN, GENTLY CURVED WHITE CHALK LINES drifting
horizontally across the UPPER part of the picture, well above both heads, solid where they
begin and thinning to dry flecks. They are decoration in the air of the room. NONE OF THEM
TOUCHES A PERSON: not one of them begins at anyone's mouth, nose, hand or body, none
crosses a face, and none looks like smoke, steam, breath or a spirit leaving a body. They
never loop, never curl back and never close into a ring.

MOTION MARKS — EXACTLY ONE GROUP, and nowhere else: TWO OR THREE very short, straight,
same-angle hand-drawn strokes just beside the dentist's pointing fingertip, no longer than
a third of a palm's width, solid where they begin and fading out, never curled, never
looping. There are no speed lines anywhere else, no radiating focus lines, no sparkles, no
stars, no hearts, no musical notes, no emotion symbols above anyone's head, and no speech
bubbles or thought bubbles of any kind in this picture.

CRITICAL — THERE IS NO WRITING ANYWHERE IN THIS PICTURE, IN ANY LANGUAGE OR ALPHABET, and
no numerals at all. THE SCREEN IS THE MOST IMPORTANT PLACE FOR THIS RULE: it carries the
three tooth drawings and nothing else — no patient name, no date, no reference number, no
menu bar, no tabs, no icons, no buttons, no ruler and no caption under the drawings. Leave
the surface plain on the poster, the folders on the shelf, the desk, the pen, the scrubs,
the shirt, the screen bezel and the jaw model. There is no name badge, no logo, no
watermark, no signature and no title. Do NOT draw rows of short dashes, squiggles or wavy
lines anywhere to imitate lines of writing.

AVOID — writing, letters, numbers or symbols on the screen; a toolbar or window frame
around the drawings on the screen; an X-ray look, a dark or black screen, a glowing or
backlit blue screen; only the upper part of the canal narrowing while the lower part stays
wide; a canal drawn as a black hole, a crack or a cavity in the third drawing; three
different teeth instead of one tooth at three times; arrows, circles, callouts or pointers
drawn on the screen; a magnifying glass; a microscope; a rubber dam; gloves; a face mask;
syringes, drills, files or piles of instruments; blood; a close-up of an open mouth; teeth
drawn inside either person's mouth; the patient frowning, worried, anxious, sad or in
pain; anyone looking at the viewer; both people with open mouths; a third person; an empty
white room with bare walls; anime or manga styling; cel shading; glossy digital rendering;
large shiny eyes; wrinkles, cheek shadow or facial modelling; blonde, caramel, light brown,
auburn, grey, silver or white hair; pure black linework; neon; glowing edges; chrome,
metallic or iridescent gradients; greyscale; thick uniform black outlines; even mechanical
vector linework; flat untextured single-tone clothing; white lines that start at a person's
mouth, nose or hands.
