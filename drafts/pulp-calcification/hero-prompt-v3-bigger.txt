Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape. One single scene, no panels, no borders.

STYLE — READ THIS FIRST; IT MATTERS MORE THAN ANY OTHER SECTION.
Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration, hand-made throughout. Linework in
warm dark brown or soft charcoal, NEVER pure black: thin, hand-drawn, the weight visibly
varies along a stroke, strokes taper and sometimes break and run dry at the end — never an
even mechanical vector line. Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light marker;
colour edges a little loose and not always meeting the line. Flat fills with two or three
tones per hue — no smooth decorative gradients, no airbrush, no glow, no neon, no
cel-shading. A fine even paper grain over every surface of the whole image, including the
background. The picture is high-key and warm, with plenty of pale cream paper showing
through, but every named object still carries a real colour. This is NOT anime, NOT manga,
NOT a screenshot from a cartoon, NOT 3D rendering.

FACES AND HANDS — READ THIS SECOND.
SKIN IS THE ONE EXCEPTION to the two-or-three-tones rule: each face and each hand is ONE
single flat warm tone, with no modelling, no blush, no shading, no cheekbones and no
jawline. On a face, draw ONLY these six things: the outline of the head, the eyes, the
eyebrows, the nose, the mouth and the ears. Nothing else at all — no wrinkles, no lines
beside the nose, no lines under the eyes, no cheek shadow, no neck shading, no highlight
in the eye. Eyes are small solid dots or short lines — never large glossy cartoon eyes,
never white eyeballs with a shine. Eyebrows are one short thin arc set well away from the
eye, and THE EYEBROWS ARE WHAT CARRY THE FEELING: every eyebrow in this picture is raised.
Hands are simple: four fingers and a thumb, no knuckle lines, no nails.
EVERY SINGLE PERSON'S HAIR IS THE SAME WARM DARK BROWN WITH A RED CAST — #483837, its
shadowed side #392928, its lit edge #4f4040 — however many people there are and however
long their hair is. It is never pure black, never neutral grey-black, and never blonde,
caramel, light brown, auburn, grey, silver or white, including the oldest explorer: his age
is shown by his posture, his glasses and his clothes, not by white hair.
EVERY PERSON IS A DIFFERENT PERSON: no two share a face, a hairstyle, an age or an
expression. Every figure has a drawn face, drawn hands and coloured clothes — nobody is a
flat grey silhouette, a shadow or an empty outline.

opens a second, much smaller world.

THE GIANT TOOTH occupies the right-hand HALF of the frame: a lower molar seen from
above and slightly in front, so that we look down onto its whole biting surface and also

stains, no cracks, no filling, no blood, and it has NO FACE, no eyes and no mouth — it is a
tooth, not a character.
SOMEWHERE ALONG THAT GROOVE, ALMOST AT THE CENTRE OF THE BITING SURFACE, THERE IS ONE
SINGLE TINY DARK PIT. THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT MEASUREMENT IN THE PICTURE: THE PIT IS

SURFACE, a dark speck you could easily miss. It is a shallow dimple, not a hole punched
through the tooth. There is exactly one of them. Do not enlarge it, do not draw a second
one, and do not open it into a wide cavern on the tooth itself.
