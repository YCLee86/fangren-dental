Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape. One single scene, no panels, no borders.

STYLE — READ THIS FIRST; IT MATTERS MORE THAN ANY OTHER SECTION.
Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration, hand-made throughout. Linework in
warm dark brown or soft charcoal, NEVER pure black: thin, hand-drawn, the weight visibly
varies along a stroke, strokes taper and sometimes break and run dry at the end — never an
even mechanical vector line. Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light marker;
colour edges a little loose and not always meeting the line. Flat fills with two or three
tones per hue — no smooth decorative gradients, no airbrush, no glow, no neon, no
cel-shading. A fine even paper grain over every surface of the whole image, including the
background. The picture is high-key and warm, with plenty of pale cream paper showing
through, but every named object still carries a real colour: warm and cool colours both
appear, and no two people wear the same colour. This is NOT anime, NOT manga, NOT a
screenshot from a cartoon, NOT 3D rendering.

FACES AND HANDS — THIS SECTION IS ABOUT THE TWO HUMANS ONLY. The teeth and the bacteria
inside the big speech bubble are cartoon characters with their own rules further down.
Each human face and hand is ONE single flat warm skin tone, with no modelling, no blush, no
cheek shadow and no wrinkles. On a human face draw only the outline of the head, the eyes,
the eyebrows, the nose, the mouth and the ears. Human eyes are small solid dots or short
lines — never large glossy eyes, never white eyeballs. The eyebrows carry the feeling.
Hands have four fingers and a thumb, no knuckle lines, no nails. Both people are East
Asian. BOTH PEOPLE'S HAIR IS THE SAME VERY DARK COLOUR WITH A FAINT COOL TEAL CAST, taken
from this clinic's periodontal colour: #374847, its shadowed side #283938, its lit edge
#404f4e. It is never pure black, never neutral grey-black, never brown, chestnut or auburn,
never visibly green, and never grey or white. Neither person looks at the viewer.

The two bubbles are drawn over the upper part of this room and cover part of it; the room

THERE ARE EXACTLY TWO SPEECH BUBBLES IN THE PICTURE. Both are smooth rounded speech bubbles

BUBBLE ONE — THE PATIENT'S, SMALL. It sits in the upper-left area above the patient, its tail
pointing down to his mouth. It is about one quarter of the width of the picture. Inside it:
a short row of FIVE plain teeth standing in a pale pink strip of gum, seen from the front,
drawn simply. THESE FIVE TEETH HAVE NO FACES, NO ARMS AND NO BACTERIA — this is just what
the patient feels. The MIDDLE tooth — the third of the five, with two teeth on each side of it — is the sore one: the gum around it is
visibly swollen, bulging up over the edge of the tooth and one clear step redder than the
pale pink of the rest, and the tooth itself is tilted slightly to one side with two or three
short curved wobble strokes beside it, as if it is rocking. The other four teeth stand
straight and look perfectly fine.

BUBBLE TWO — THE DENTIST'S, BIG. THIS IS THE HEART OF THE PICTURE. It fills most of the
upper-right and centre of the frame — about three fifths of the width of the picture and
about two thirds of its height — with its tail pointing down and to the left to the dentist's mouth. Inside

what is really going on.
THE FIVE TEETH ARE CHARACTERS, drawn the way the tooth is drawn in the first reference
image: clean warm ivory, rounded, each with a simple face (small eyes, eyebrows, a mouth) and
two thin arms. No legs. They stand side by side in one row with their lower part sunk into
a band of pink gum that runs across the whole bubble. The teeth are clean — no brown stains,
no dirt patches, no cracks.
THE MIDDLE TOOTH IS THE SORE ONE AGAIN — the third of five, with two teeth on each side. Its gum is swollen, bulging and one
clear step redder than the rest, and the tooth leans to one side with short wobble strokes

raised and waving above its head. It is dramatic in a comic way, never in real pain or
terror.
UNDER AND AROUND THE SORE TOOTH THERE IS A SWARM OF BACTERIA — EIGHTEEN OF THEM, CLEARLY
MORE THAN FIFTEEN AND NEVER FEWER. THEY ARE SMALL AND THERE ARE MANY: a dense, busy, crowded

squeezing into the gap between the tooth and the gum, several layers deep, overlapping one
another. Count them: this swarm alone holds more bacteria than all the rest of the picture

down on the swollen gum; some scrambling up the tooth over each other's backs; a couple

THE OTHER FOUR TEETH HAVE BACTERIA TOO — FEWER THAN THE SORE TOOTH, AND A DIFFERENT NUMBER
