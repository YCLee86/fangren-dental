Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape. One single scene, no panels, no borders.

STYLE — READ THIS FIRST; IT MATTERS MORE THAN ANY OTHER SECTION.
Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration, hand-made throughout. Linework in
warm dark brown or soft charcoal, NEVER pure black: thin, hand-drawn, the weight visibly
varies along a stroke, strokes taper and sometimes break and run dry at the end — never an
even mechanical vector line. Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light marker;
colour edges a little loose and not always meeting the line. Flat fills with two or three
tones per hue — no smooth decorative gradients, no airbrush, no glow, no neon, no
cel-shading. A fine even paper grain over every surface of the whole image, including the
background. The picture is high-key and warm, with plenty of pale cream paper showing
through, but every named object still carries a real colour: warm and cool colours both
appear, and no two people wear the same colour. This is NOT anime, NOT manga, NOT a
screenshot from a cartoon, NOT 3D rendering.

FACES AND HANDS — THIS SECTION IS ABOUT THE TWO HUMANS ONLY. The teeth and the bacteria
inside the big speech bubble are cartoon characters with their own rules further down.
Each human face and hand is ONE single flat warm skin tone, with no modelling, no blush, no
cheek shadow and no wrinkles. On a human face draw only the outline of the head, the eyes,
the eyebrows, the nose, the mouth and the ears. Human eyes are small solid dots or short
lines — never large glossy eyes, never white eyeballs. The eyebrows carry the feeling.
Hands have four fingers and a thumb, no knuckle lines, no nails. Both people are East
Asian. Both people's hair is warm dark brown with a red cast (#483837, shadow #392928),
never pure black, never grey or white. Neither person looks at the viewer.

TWO REFERENCE IMAGES ARE ATTACHED AND EACH ONE HAS EXACTLY ONE JOB.
THE FIRST shows a tooth character and a crowd of bacteria characters drawn earlier for this
same clinic. It gives ONLY THE WAY THE TOOTH CHARACTERS AND THE BACTERIA ARE DRAWN — their
build, their faces, their arms and legs, their outline weight and their colour range. Take
nothing else from it: not the water jet, not the mound of earth, not the dirt stains on the
tooth, not the cabinets, not the composition. Never reproduce that drawing.
THE SECOND is an earlier illustration for this clinic. It gives ONLY THE FINISH — the line,
the coloured-pencil colour, the paper grain and the way people and clothes are drawn. Take
nothing else from it: not its tooth, not its circles, not its tunnel, not its people, not its
composition. Never reproduce that drawing.

THE SCENE — A BRIGHT, CALM CONSULTATION CORNER IN A SMALL NEIGHBOURHOOD DENTAL CLINIC. Two
people are talking, and each has ONE speech bubble. The bubbles are where the story is told.
The room is a gentle backdrop: a pale warm wall with one horizontal stripe of muted teal
(#317d78) running along it at waist height, a low pale-wood cabinet with two rounded drawer
pulls, a small potted plant with rounded leaves on the cabinet, and a window at the far left
letting in soft morning light. The patient sits in a pale sage dental chair; the edge of a
dental lamp on its arm is visible above him. Nothing on the walls — no posters, no charts,
no framed pictures, no screens.

THE PATIENT is on the LEFT of the picture, in the lower-left quarter. A man in his fifties,
short neat hair, slightly heavy build, in a mustard-yellow knitted cardigan over a white
shirt and grey trousers. He sits turned towards the dentist, leaning forward a little, the
hand nearer the viewer pressed flat against his cheek, the other hand resting on the arm of
the chair. His eyebrows are drawn up and together — uncomfortable and a bit worried, not in
agony. His mouth is a small open shape because he is speaking.

THE DENTIST is on the RIGHT of the picture, in the lower-right corner, drawn at the same
modest size as the patient. A woman in her late thirties, hair in a low ponytail, in a
dusty-rose scrub top with a short white coat over it. She sits on a low round stool turned
towards the patient, one hand open and raised towards the big bubble above her as she
explains, the other resting on her knee. Her expression is calm, kind and serious — eyebrows
level, mouth a small open shape because she is speaking.
NEITHER PERSON IS TALLER THAN ABOUT HALF THE HEIGHT OF THE PICTURE. The two bubbles are the
main subject, not the people.

THERE ARE EXACTLY TWO SPEECH BUBBLES IN THE PICTURE. Both are smooth rounded speech bubbles
with a clean hand-drawn outline and a short pointed tail — never cloud-shaped thought
bubbles, never a circle with a handle, never a magnifying glass. Both are filled with pale
cream paper.

BUBBLE ONE — THE PATIENT'S, SMALL. It sits in the upper-left area above the patient, its tail
pointing down to his mouth. It is about one quarter of the width of the picture. Inside it:
a short row of FIVE plain teeth standing in a pale pink strip of gum, seen from the front,
drawn simply. THESE FIVE TEETH HAVE NO FACES, NO ARMS AND NO BACTERIA — this is just what
the patient feels. The SECOND tooth from the left is the sore one: the gum around it is
visibly swollen, bulging up over the edge of the tooth and one clear step redder than the
pale pink of the rest, and the tooth itself is tilted slightly to one side with two or three
short curved wobble strokes beside it, as if it is rocking. The other four teeth stand
straight and look perfectly fine.

BUBBLE TWO — THE DENTIST'S, BIG. THIS IS THE HEART OF THE PICTURE. It fills most of the
upper-right and centre of the frame — about three fifths of the width of the picture and
about two thirds of its height — with its tail pointing down to the dentist's mouth. Inside
it is THE SAME ROW OF FIVE TEETH, in the same order, now drawn large, and now we can see
what is really going on.
THE FIVE TEETH ARE CHARACTERS, drawn the way the tooth is drawn in the first reference
image: clean warm ivory, rounded, each with a simple face (small eyes, eyebrows, a mouth) and
two thin arms. No legs. They stand side by side in one row with their lower part sunk into
a band of pink gum that runs across the whole bubble. The teeth are clean — no brown stains,
no dirt patches, no cracks.
THE SECOND TOOTH FROM THE LEFT IS THE SORE ONE AGAIN. Its gum is swollen, bulging and one
clear step redder than the rest, and the tooth leans to one side with short wobble strokes
beside it. IT IS CALLING FOR HELP: mouth wide open, eyebrows pulled up high, both arms
raised and waving above its head. It is dramatic in a comic way, never in real pain or
terror.
UNDER AND AROUND THE SORE TOOTH THERE IS A BIG CROWD OF BACTERIA — about eight of them,
packed together at its gumline and on its sides, all making trouble: two are pulling down
the edge of the swollen gum like a curtain and peeking under it; one is digging into the gum
with both hands so crumbs fly up; two are hanging off the tooth's side and rocking it back
and forth; one is bouncing on the swollen gum as if it were a trampoline; one is climbing up
the tooth; one is sitting on the gum pulling a cheeky face with its tongue out.
THE OTHER FOUR TEETH HAVE BACTERIA TOO, BUT FEWER, AND A DIFFERENT NUMBER EACH: the first
tooth (far left) has two; the third tooth has three; the fourth tooth has one; the fifth
tooth (far right) has two. These smaller groups are playing and fooling about at the
gumline of their tooth — one sliding down the gum, one peeking out from under the gum edge,
one swinging from the tooth's arm, one doing a handstand, one tugging at the gum edge, one
waving at the others, one sitting and kicking its legs, one poking the tooth.
THE FOUR OTHER TEETH EACH REACT, AND NO TWO REACT THE SAME WAY:
the first tooth, right beside the sore one, has turned its eyes and head towards the sore
tooth, eyebrows tilted up with worry, one hand reaching out towards it;
the third tooth, on the sore tooth's other side, also looks towards the sore tooth, worried,
both hands pressed together in front of it;
the fourth tooth looks down at the single bacterium on its own gumline, eyebrows raised,
nervous, arms pulled in close;
the fifth tooth looks down at its own two bacteria, sweating a single small drop, mouth a
tight wobbly line, nervous.
No tooth in the bubble is smiling.

THE BACTERIA — DRAW THEM EXACTLY THE WAY THEY ARE DRAWN IN THE FIRST REFERENCE IMAGE. Each is
a little standing character, not a blob or a dot: a plump bean-shaped or capsule-shaped
body, some with short bristly hairs round the edge, and every one has FOUR THIN STICK LIMBS —
two arms and two legs with small rounded hands and feet. Faces: two round white eyeballs
with dark pupils (the bacteria are the one exception to the eye rule — the two humans and
the teeth keep small dot eyes), and mischievous, cheeky, pleased-with-themselves
expressions — grins, smirks, tongues out. They are troublemakers having fun, not monsters:
no fangs, no claws, no angry red eyes, nothing frightening. Each has a thick, confident warm
dark-brown outline, heavier than any other line in the picture, and a solid muted but clearly
saturated colour with a little speckled texture — plum purple, brick red, dark mustard,
olive green, muddy teal. No two neighbours are the same colour. The bacteria are the most
strongly coloured things in the picture. EACH BACTERIUM IS ABOUT ONE QUARTER OF THE HEIGHT
OF A TOOTH — big enough to see its face and limbs, never tiny specks.
THE BACTERIA ARE ONLY INSIDE THE DENTIST'S BIG BUBBLE. There are none in the patient's
small bubble, none in the room, none on the people.

MOTION MARKS: short groups of two or three parallel strokes, very short, tapering — beside
the sore tooth's wobble in both bubbles, beside the bacteria that are bouncing, swinging and
rocking, and beside the sore tooth's waving arms. Never one long curving line.
LIGHT: one warm light source, the morning window on the far left; soft white atmosphere
strokes (a small group of short parallel lines) slanting in from that window. The rest of
the room sits in soft neutral light. The inside of both bubbles is evenly lit.

NO TEXT ANYWHERE. No letters, no words, no numbers, no symbols, no exclamation marks, no
question marks, no musical notes, no hearts, no "HELP" — the sore tooth calls for help only
with its face and arms. Nothing written inside or outside either bubble, on the walls, on
the cabinet, on the clothes or on the chair.
