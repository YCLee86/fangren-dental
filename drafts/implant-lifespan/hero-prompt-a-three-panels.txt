Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape.

STYLE — READ THIS FIRST; IT MATTERS MORE THAN ANY OTHER SECTION.
Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration, hand-made throughout. Linework in
warm dark brown or soft charcoal, NEVER pure black: thin, hand-drawn, the weight visibly
varies along a stroke, strokes taper and sometimes break and run dry at the end — never an
even mechanical vector line. Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light marker;
colour edges a little loose and not always meeting the line. Flat fills with two or three
tones per hue — no smooth decorative gradients, no airbrush, no glow. A fine even paper
grain over every surface of the whole image. Plenty of warm paper showing through in the
quiet areas, but the picture is clearly coloured, never washed out to near-white.

FACES AND HANDS — READ THIS SECOND.
SKIN IS THE ONE EXCEPTION to the two-or-three-tones rule: each face and each hand is ONE
single flat tone, with no modelling, no blush, no shading, no cheekbones and no jawline.
On a face, draw ONLY these six things: the outline of the head, the eyes, the eyebrows,
the nose, the mouth and the ears. Nothing else at all — no wrinkles, no lines beside the
nose, no lines under the eyes, no cheek shadow, no neck shading, no highlight in the eye.
Eyes are small solid dots or short lines — never closed crescents, never large glossy
cartoon eyes. Hands are simple: four fingers and a thumb, no knuckle lines, no nails.
HAIR IS ALWAYS A WARM DARK BROWN — #3f3330, its shadowed side #2e2523, its lit edge
#4d403c. NEVER grey, silver, white, ash, steel or blue-grey hair on anyone, including the
older version of the man in the third panel.
EVERY PERSON IS DRAWN BY THE SAME HAND, at the same level of simplicity: every figure has
a drawn face, drawn hands and coloured clothes. NOBODY is a flat grey silhouette, a
shadow, a blob or an empty outline. Nobody looks at the viewer.

STRUCTURE — ONE single illustration divided by TWO THIN HAND-DRAWN VERTICAL LINES into
THREE PANELS OF EQUAL WIDTH, read left to right as one story. NO ARROWS ANYWHERE, no
numbers on the panels, no captions.
  • LEFT PANEL — THE DAY IT WAS FINISHED, in a dental surgery.
  • MIDDLE PANEL — EVERY ORDINARY MORNING IN BETWEEN, at his own bathroom basin.
  • RIGHT PANEL — A CHECK-UP MANY YEARS LATER, in the same dental surgery.
The picture is warm and calm throughout: nobody is frightened, nobody is in pain, there is
no blood, and there is no close-up of an open mouth anywhere.

HOW THE READER SEES THAT YEARS HAVE PASSED — THREE THINGS ONLY, all quiet:
  1. ONE POTTED PLANT stands in the same corner of the surgery in the LEFT and RIGHT
     panels: in the left panel it is small, in a small terracotta pot, with about four
     leaves. In the right panel it is the SAME plant grown large — a bigger pot, many more
     leaves, one leaf reaching past the edge of the cabinet.
  2. THE MAN'S BUILD is a little fuller in the right panel than in the left.
  3. IN THE RIGHT PANEL ONLY, ONE SMALL PATCH OF HAIR AT EACH TEMPLE is one step lighter
     than the rest of his hair (#4d403c against #3f3330). It is a small patch, not a
     streak, and it is still brown — NOT grey, NOT white, NOT silver.
Nothing else marks the time: no calendar, no clock hands, no dates, no numbers.

THE SAME MAN APPEARS IN ALL THREE PANELS — East Asian, in his forties in the left panel.
Same face, same simple short haircut and same clothing colour family (warm blue-grey) in
all three, so he is recognisably one person. What changes from panel to panel is ONLY what
this list says, and BOTH of his hands are accounted for in every panel:
  • LEFT PANEL — he SITS in the dental chair, upright, turned three-quarters towards the
    right of the frame. HIS RIGHT HAND holds a small round hand mirror up in front of him;
    HIS LEFT HAND rests flat on the arm of the chair. He looks into the mirror. HIS MOUTH
    IS A CLOSED UPWARD CURVE — a small, pleased, closed-mouth smile. He is not speaking.
    He wears a sage-green open shirt over a cream tee.
  • MIDDLE PANEL — he STANDS at the basin, body turned three-quarters to the left,
    leaning in slightly. HIS RIGHT HAND holds a small INTERDENTAL BRUSH up to the back
    corner of his mouth; HIS LEFT HAND rests on the front edge of the basin. He looks down
    and slightly sideways, concentrating. HIS LIPS ARE ONLY JUST PARTED at one corner — a
    small simple opening, WITH NOTHING DRAWN INSIDE IT: no teeth, no gums, no tongue, no
    dark cavity. He wears a plain warm-grey tee.
  • RIGHT PANEL — he SITS in the same dental chair, reclined a little, head tilted back
    and turned away from us towards the dentist. HIS RIGHT HAND rests on his own stomach;
    HIS LEFT HAND rests on the arm of the chair. HIS MOUTH IS ONE SHORT STRAIGHT CLOSED
    LINE, his eyebrows level, his expression relaxed and unbothered. He is not speaking.
    He wears a soft-blue shirt.

THE SAME DENTIST APPEARS IN THE LEFT AND RIGHT PANELS — an East Asian woman in her
thirties with her hair tied back in a low ponytail, in a short-sleeved clinic top. Both of
her hands are accounted for in each panel:
  • LEFT PANEL — she STANDS behind and to the left of the chair, leaning slightly in. HER
    RIGHT HAND points with one finger towards the hand mirror the man is holding; HER LEFT
    HAND holds A SMALL CLEAR PLASTIC MODEL OF A LOWER JAW at chest height. SHE IS THE ONLY
    PERSON IN THE WHOLE PICTURE WHOSE MOUTH IS OPEN — a small open shape, mid-sentence,
    eyebrows slightly raised, friendly.
  • RIGHT PANEL — she is SEEN FROM BEHIND in the near foreground at the lower right,
    sitting on a rolling stool, her back and one shoulder towards us, her head turned in
    profile towards the man. HER RIGHT HAND holds a small round mouth mirror near the man;
    HER LEFT HAND rests on the back of the chair. Her mouth is a closed level line: she is
    concentrating, not speaking, and her face is only just visible in profile.

THE CLEAR JAW MODEL (left panel only) — a small hand-held model, about the length of her
palm: a simple pale translucent arch with a row of plain simple tooth shapes on it, ONE of
which has a short grey-blue root shape (#465885) below the gum line. It is a real object
being held in a real hand, catching the room light. IT IS NOT a technical diagram, NOT a
cross-section, NOT a poster, NOT labelled, NOT exploded into parts, and there are NO
callout lines, NO measurements and NO letters anywhere on or near it.

LEFT PANEL — PROPS, ALL DRAWN: a dental chair with a padded headrest and simple armrests;
a round overhead lamp on a jointed arm, switched off; a small instrument tray on a swing
arm with two simple instruments lying flat on it; a cup in a holder at the side of the
chair; a low cabinet with plain drawer fronts; a framed picture on the wall showing a
single simple tooth shape and NO writing; a tall window on the left letting in clean
daylight; and THE SMALL POTTED PLANT in the corner beside the cabinet.

MIDDLE PANEL — PROPS, ALL DRAWN: a basin with a plain tap; a mirror above it whose glass
shows ONLY a soft pale reflection of the window — NO reflected face, NO reflected room; a
tumbler holding TWO toothbrushes; a small open box of interdental brushes on the counter
with A COMPLETELY BLANK FACE; a flat coil of dental floss in a plain round case; a plain
toothpaste tube with NO writing on it; a folded towel on a rail in the deep blue #465885;
a small window at the side letting in ordinary morning daylight; a tiled wall drawn with
light seams, not a blank white wall. THIS PANEL MUST NOT LOOK EMPTY, and it must not be
dark: it is an ordinary bright bathroom in the morning.

RIGHT PANEL — PROPS, ALL DRAWN: the same dental chair and the same jointed lamp, this time
switched on and pointing away from us; a wall-mounted light box or small flat screen
showing ONE SINGLE SIMPLE ROW OF PALE TOOTH SHAPES and nothing else — no numbers, no
letters, no scale, no detail; the same low cabinet; a rolling stool; a tissue box; and THE
SAME POTTED PLANT, now much larger, in the same corner.

VARY THE PLACEMENT so the three panels are not symmetrical: in the LEFT panel the man sits
to the RIGHT of the panel and the dentist stands to the left; in the MIDDLE panel he
stands LEFT OF CENTRE with the basin running across the panel; in the RIGHT panel we look
from the OTHER SIDE OF THE ROOM, with the dentist's back in the near foreground at the
lower right and the man further away and slightly left. Nobody stands in the exact centre
of a panel, and no two panels put the main figure at the same height in the frame.

PEOPLE — Simplified illustrative people: minimal features, natural realistic head-to-body
proportions, believable ages, East Asian faces. Not photorealistic. Not faceless. No
oversized heads, no noodle limbs. The man's height in the frame is about half the panel
height; the figures are people in a room, not portraits.
CLOTHING — the dentist wears a short-sleeved clinic top in #b7d7d3 with its folds and
shadows in #99b8b4. Clothes are NEVER flat single-tone shapes: model every garment with
two or three tones of its own colour, with soft coloured-pencil shading in the folds,
along the sleeves, under the collar and at the hem, and draw the real details — collar,
cuff, sleeve seam, hem, a pocket.

LIGHT AND COLOUR — Ordinary bright indoor daytime in all three panels, ONE light source
per panel: clean daylight from a window, warm and even. No sunset, no lamplight as the
main light, no long orange shadows, no spotlight, no screen lighting a face from below, no
dark room, no night.
Most coloured areas sit in the range HSL saturation 30–50 and lightness 70–85 — clearly
coloured and readable, never washed out into near-white pastels and never heavy or muddy.
Use AT LEAST FIVE clearly different colours in each panel and give each one to a named
object: the clinic top, his shirt, the cabinet, the chair upholstery, the towel, the
plant pot, the tiles, the curtains.
ACCENT — the deep blue #465885 and its darker step #2a4677 belong to a few small named
things only: the towel, the chair upholstery, the root shape in the jaw model and the pale
tooth shapes on the light box. #c28229 (amber) appears only once, as the handle of the
interdental brush. Do NOT wash the image in either colour, do NOT tint the skin, the
walls, the floor or the paper with them, and do NOT make them vivid.
THE WHITE ATMOSPHERE LINES ARE REQUIRED — DO NOT LEAVE THEM OUT. Across the upper part of
the LEFT and RIGHT panels only, above the people and not touching them, draw FOUR OR FIVE
long, thin, white hand-drawn lines running the same way at the same shallow angle, evenly
spaced, solid where they begin and thinning to dry flecks — soft chalk quality, clearly
hand-made, clearly visible against the wall. These lines must never begin at anyone's
mouth, nose, hand or body, must never loop or curl back on themselves, must never cross
the panel dividing lines, and must never resemble smoke, steam or breath.

MOTION AND SPEECH MARKS — EXACTLY FOUR GROUPS OF MARKS IN THE WHOLE PICTURE AND NOWHERE
ELSE. Every mark is the same kind: TWO OR THREE very short, straight, same-angle
hand-drawn strokes, no longer than a third of a palm's width, solid where they begin and
fading out, never curled, never looping, never bigger than the thing they belong to.
  • LEFT PANEL: two short strokes beside the dentist's pointing fingertip;
  • LEFT PANEL: two short diagonal strokes beside the dentist's mouth, because she is the
    only person speaking;
  • MIDDLE PANEL: two short strokes beside the head of the interdental brush in his hand —
    NONE at his mouth, because he is not speaking;
  • RIGHT PANEL: two short strokes beside the mouth mirror in the dentist's hand — NONE at
    anyone's mouth in this panel, because nobody here is speaking.
NO speech bubbles of any kind, no thought bubbles, no speed lines across a background, no
radiating focus lines, no sparkles, no musical notes, no hearts.

CRITICAL — THERE IS NO WRITING ANYWHERE IN THIS PICTURE, IN ANY LANGUAGE OR ALPHABET.
Not one letter, not one digit, not one punctuation mark. Specifically: nothing on the
toothpaste tube, nothing on the box of interdental brushes, nothing on the floss case,
nothing on the tissue box, nothing on the cabinet, nothing on the wall picture, nothing on
the light box or screen, nothing on the jaw model, nothing on the clinic top, no badge, no
name tag, no logo, no watermark, no calendar, no clock face, no signs on the walls and no
labels on any drawer. Do NOT draw rows of short dashes, squiggles or wavy lines anywhere
to imitate lines of writing. Where writing would normally appear, leave the surface plain.

AVOID — grey, silver, white, ash or blue-grey hair on anyone; wrinkles, cheek shadow or
facial modelling; the man looking like a different person in each panel; the man and the
dentist sharing one face; anyone looking at the viewer; grey silhouette people, shadow
people, faceless figures or empty outlines; a bare undecorated room with blank walls in
any panel; a dark or night-time bathroom; a face reflected in the bathroom mirror;
greyscale; photorealism; a photorealistic mouth, gum or tooth; a close-up of an open
mouth; teeth, gums or tongue drawn inside anyone's mouth; a cross-section or cutaway of a
jaw; a labelled anatomical diagram; a poster of teeth with detail on it; a dense pile of
dental instruments, needles, drills or burs; a syringe; blood, wounds or red inflamed
gums; decayed, dark, broken or missing teeth; a frightened, sad or pained face; anyone
shouting or celebrating; arrows of any kind; ticks, crosses, plus or minus signs; speech
bubbles; thought bubbles; charts or graphs; a calendar, a clock, a date or any number;
glowing screens; neon; chrome, metallic or iridescent gradients; glow, sparkle or lens
flare; thick uniform black outlines; even mechanical vector linework; flat untextured
single-tone clothing; a whole image tinted blue.
