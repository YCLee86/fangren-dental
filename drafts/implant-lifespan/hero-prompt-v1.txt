Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape. One single scene, no panels, no borders.

STYLE — READ THIS FIRST; IT MATTERS MORE THAN ANY OTHER SECTION.
Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration, hand-made throughout. Linework in
warm dark brown or soft charcoal, NEVER pure black: thin, hand-drawn, the weight visibly
varies along a stroke, strokes taper and sometimes break and run dry at the end — never an
even mechanical vector line. Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light marker;
colour edges a little loose and not always meeting the line. Flat fills with two or three
tones per hue — no smooth decorative gradients, no airbrush, no glow, no neon. A fine even
paper grain over every surface of the whole image, including the background.
THE WHOLE PICTURE IS BRIGHT AND HIGH-KEY, on a warm cream paper ground. This is a
daylight-bright hand-drawn picture, not a dark glowing sci-fi image.
This is NOT anime, NOT manga, NOT cel-shaded animation, NOT a screenshot from a cartoon.

FACES AND HANDS — READ THIS SECOND.
SKIN IS THE ONE EXCEPTION to the two-or-three-tones rule: each face and each hand is ONE
single flat warm tone, with no modelling, no blush, no shading, no cheekbones and no
jawline. On a face, draw ONLY these six things: the outline of the head, the eyes, the
eyebrows, the nose, the mouth and the ears. Nothing else at all — no wrinkles, no lines
beside the nose, no lines under the eyes, no cheek shadow, no neck shading, no highlight
in the eye. Eyes are small solid dots or short lines — never closed crescents, never large
glossy cartoon eyes, never white eyeballs with a shine. Hands are simple: four fingers and
a thumb, no knuckle lines, no nails.
HAIR IS ALWAYS A WARM DARK BROWN — #3f3330, its shadowed side #2e2523, its lit edge
#4d403c. NEVER grey, silver, white, ash, steel or blue-grey hair on anyone, including the
oldest passenger: her age is shown by her posture, her glasses and her clothes, not by
white hair.
EVERY PERSON IS DRAWN BY THE SAME HAND, at the same level of simplicity: every figure has
a drawn face, drawn hands and coloured clothes. NOBODY is a flat grey silhouette, a
shadow, a blob or an empty outline. EVERY PERSON IS A DIFFERENT PERSON: no two share a
face, a hairstyle, an age or an expression.

THE SCENE — A SMALL HOMEMADE FLYING SLED CARRYING SEVEN PEOPLE, travelling through a wide
swirling tunnel of time with clocks floating past it. The sled is seen from the front and
slightly to the side, moving diagonally towards the lower left of the frame, so that the
people are turned three-quarters away from us and NOBODY LOOKS AT THE VIEWER. The sled and
its passengers together fill roughly the middle half of the picture and sit slightly right
of centre; the tunnel opens away behind them towards the upper right. The mood is warm,
funny and companionable — a crowded family outing, not an action shot and not a rescue.

THE SLED IS BUILT OUT OF DENTAL-CLINIC FURNITURE — this is the joke of the picture, and
every part of it must be drawn:
  • THE DECK: a flat pale-wood board like a small sled, its front edge curling up, with
    FOUR CREAM-COLOURED BALL FEET underneath, one at each corner. Its side edge is a
    simple pale blue band (#465885).
  • THE CONTROL DESK at the front of the deck: a mobile dental instrument cart with a
    plain pale front panel, a slim chrome-free metal frame and, on its top surface, TWO
    SIMPLE ROUNDED HAND LEVERS and three small plain round buttons. No screens, no dials
    with markings, no readouts.
  • THE LAMP: a dental treatment lamp on a long jointed metal arm rising from the back of
    the deck and curving forward over the front of the sled, its round pale lamp head
    facing the direction of travel like a headlight, lit with a soft warm glow that stays
    inside the lamp head.
  • THE SEAT: a single dental chair bolted to the middle of the deck, with a padded
    headrest, padded armrests and upholstery in the deep blue #465885.
  • A SMALL SWING-ARM TRAY beside the chair holding one plain cup and two simple
    instruments lying flat.
This craft is our own invention, assembled from clinic furniture. It is not a vehicle from
any existing cartoon, comic, film or franchise.

THE SEVEN PASSENGERS — three clinic staff and four members of the public, all East Asian,
crowded onto the small deck so that they are almost spilling off it. Both hands of every
person are accounted for. Only one person's mouth is open.
  1. THE DRIVER — a woman in her thirties with her hair tied in a ponytail, in a
     short-sleeved clinic top (#b7d7d3, folds #99b8b4). She STANDS behind the control desk,
     feet apart, leaning forward into the wind. BOTH HANDS grip the two hand levers. She
     looks ahead along the direction of travel. HER MOUTH IS A CLOSED UPWARD CURVE — a
     confident, closed-mouth smile.
  2. THE DENTIST — a man in his forties with short neat hair, in the same clinic top WITH A
     WHITE COAT OVER IT, the hem of the coat blown back behind him. He STANDS to the
     driver's left. HIS RIGHT HAND grips the lamp arm above his head; HIS LEFT ARM is
     stretched out pointing ahead past the lamp. HE IS THE ONLY PERSON IN THE PICTURE WITH
     AN OPEN MOUTH — a small open shape, mid-shout, eyebrows raised, delighted.
  3. THE ASSISTANT — a young woman in her twenties with a short bob, in a clinic top. She
     CROUCHES at the front edge of the deck, knees together. BOTH HANDS hold the curled
     front rim of the deck. She looks out and down at the clocks going past. HER MOUTH IS A
     SMALL CLOSED SMILE and her eyebrows are raised.
  4. THE TEENAGER — a boy of about fifteen in a school uniform shirt with a satchel across
     his body. He STANDS at the left edge of the deck. HIS LEFT HAND grips the lamp arm;
     HIS RIGHT HAND points up at one large clock drifting past. He looks up at that clock.
     HIS MOUTH IS A SMALL CLOSED SMILE, eyebrows high with surprise.
  5. THE YOUNG WOMAN — in her twenties, long hair streaming back. She STANDS just behind
     the dental chair. HER RIGHT HAND rests on the back of the chair; HER LEFT HAND presses
     her own hair down at the side of her head. She looks ahead. HER MOUTH IS A CLOSED
     SMILE, eyes narrowed with laughter.
  6. THE MIDDLE-AGED MAN — in his fifties, slightly heavier build, short hair, in a
     mustard-yellow polo shirt. He STANDS to the right of the chair, leaning back a little
     against the wind. HIS LEFT HAND holds the chair's armrest; HIS RIGHT HAND holds the
     back edge of the deck behind him. He looks down at the passing clocks. HIS MOUTH IS
     ONE SHORT CLOSED LINE, his eyebrows slightly raised: cautious, but enjoying it.
  7. THE OLDEST PASSENGER — a woman in her seventies with dark hair pinned up, small round
     glasses, a clay-rose blouse and a cardigan. SHE IS THE ONLY ONE SITTING: she sits
     calmly and upright IN THE DENTAL CHAIR, BOTH HANDS resting on its armrests, her
     shoulders relaxed. She looks up and to the side at a clock floating above her. HER
     MOUTH IS A CLOSED UPWARD CURVE — a settled, contented smile. She is not frightened and
     not being helped: she is the most comfortable person on board.

THE TUNNEL — a wide, calm, hand-drawn spiral of time, NOT a dark neon vortex. Its vanishing
point sits in the upper right of the frame. Build it from long concentric hand-drawn arcs
of flat colour sweeping from the lower left round to the vanishing point, each band a
different flat tone: pale sage, pale teal (#b7d7d3), soft blue (a light tint of #465885),
warm cream and a little pale amber (#c28229 heavily tinted). The bands are wide and calm,
their edges drawn with the same dry hand-drawn line as everything else, with paper grain
across all of them. Warm cream paper shows through between the bands. The centre of the
spiral is the LIGHTEST part of the picture, not the darkest.

THE FLOATING CLOCKS — clocks of many kinds drift through the tunnel around the sled: round
wall clocks, small pocket watches on chains, one or two sand timers, a couple of clocks
gently bent and softened as if made of cloth. They are larger near the sled and smaller
towards the vanishing point, tilted at many different angles, each drawn as a simple flat
shape with a clean outline. EVERY CLOCK FACE IN THIS PICTURE IS BLANK EXCEPT FOR ITS HANDS
AND A RING OF PLAIN SHORT TICK MARKS — however many clocks there are, and wherever they
are. The hands of each clock point a different way from the others. The clocks are drawn
in the same palette as the tunnel, never metallic, never glowing.

LIGHT AND COLOUR — bright, even, daylight-like light with no single hard source: the
picture reads as an illustration on paper, not as a lit room. Most coloured areas sit in
the range HSL saturation 30–50 and lightness 70–85 — clearly coloured and readable, never
washed out to near-white and never heavy or muddy. Give each named object its own colour:
the clinic tops, the white coat, the school shirt, the polo shirt, the cardigan, the chair
upholstery, the wooden deck, the lamp head. NOBODY wears the same colour as the person
beside them. #c28229 (amber) is used only in small amounts: the lamp's warm glow, one or
two tick rings, one clock rim.
THE WHITE ATMOSPHERE LINES ARE REQUIRED — DO NOT LEAVE THEM OUT. Draw SIX OR SEVEN long,
thin, white hand-drawn lines sweeping through the tunnel in the same direction as the
spiral bands, passing behind and beside the sled but never touching any person: solid where
they begin and thinning to dry flecks, soft chalk quality, clearly hand-made. They must
never begin at anyone's mouth, nose, hand or body, never loop or curl back on themselves,
and never look like smoke, steam or breath.

MOTION MARKS — EXACTLY THREE GROUPS, and nowhere else. Each is TWO OR THREE very short,
straight, same-angle hand-drawn strokes, no longer than a third of a palm's width, solid
where they begin and fading out, never curled, never looping, never larger than the thing
they belong to: beside the driver's hands on the levers; beside the pointing man's
fingertip; beside the ball feet under the front of the deck. There are no speed lines
across the background, no radiating focus lines, no sparkles, no stars, no musical notes,
no hearts, and no speech bubbles or thought bubbles of any kind.

CRITICAL — THERE IS NO WRITING ANYWHERE IN THIS PICTURE, IN ANY LANGUAGE OR ALPHABET, and
no numerals at all. Every clock face carries hands and plain tick marks only. Leave the
surface plain on the instrument cart, the control desk, the cup, the tray, the white coat,
the clinic tops, the satchel, the school shirt, the chair and every clock. There is no name
badge, no logo, no watermark, no signature, no caption and no title. Do NOT draw rows of
short dashes, squiggles or wavy lines anywhere to imitate lines of writing.

AVOID — any recognisable cartoon, anime or comic character; any mascot; any animal
character; any character with a large round blue head; any vehicle copied from an existing
cartoon, comic or film; anime or manga styling; cel shading; glossy digital rendering;
large shiny eyes; a dark background; a black or midnight-blue void; neon; glowing edges;
electric streaks; lens flare; chrome, metallic or iridescent gradients; a deep saturated
purple and magenta vortex; numerals on clock faces; digital clock displays; grey, silver or
white hair; wrinkles, cheek shadow or facial modelling; anyone looking at the viewer;
anyone falling, screaming or frightened; anyone being held up or rescued; the oldest
passenger looking frail, sad or ill; a wheelchair; a hospital bed; a drip stand; blood;
teeth drawn inside anyone's mouth; a close-up of an open mouth; syringes, drills or piles
of instruments; arrows of any kind; speech bubbles; greyscale; thick uniform black
outlines; even mechanical vector linework; flat untextured single-tone clothing.
