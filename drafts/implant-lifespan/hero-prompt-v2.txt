Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape. One single scene, no panels, no borders.

STYLE — READ THIS FIRST; IT MATTERS MORE THAN ANY OTHER SECTION.
Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration, hand-made throughout. Linework in
warm dark brown or soft charcoal, NEVER pure black: thin, hand-drawn, the weight visibly
varies along a stroke, strokes taper and sometimes break and run dry at the end — never an
even mechanical vector line. Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light marker;
colour edges a little loose and not always meeting the line. Flat fills with two or three
tones per hue — no smooth decorative gradients, no airbrush, no glow, no neon. A fine even
paper grain over every surface of the whole image, including the background.
THE ONE EXCEPTION IS THE BACKGROUND TUNNEL, which is painted in loose wet watercolour that
blooms and bleeds between colours; the people, the sled and the clocks stay flat and
hand-drawn exactly as described above. The people and the sled are always the brightest,
highest-key part of the picture.
This is NOT anime, NOT manga, NOT cel-shaded animation, NOT a screenshot from a cartoon.

FACES AND HANDS — READ THIS SECOND.
SKIN IS THE ONE EXCEPTION to the two-or-three-tones rule: each face and each hand is ONE
single flat warm tone, with no modelling, no blush, no shading, no cheekbones and no
jawline. On a face, draw ONLY these six things: the outline of the head, the eyes, the
eyebrows, the nose, the mouth and the ears. Nothing else at all — no wrinkles, no lines
beside the nose, no lines under the eyes, no cheek shadow, no neck shading, no highlight
in the eye. Eyes are small solid dots or short lines — never closed crescents, never large
glossy cartoon eyes, never white eyeballs with a shine. Hands are simple: four fingers and
a thumb, no knuckle lines, no nails.
EVERY SINGLE PERSON'S HAIR IS THE SAME WARM DARK BROWN — #3f3330, its shadowed side
#2e2523, its lit edge #4d403c — however many people there are and however long their hair
is. No blonde, no caramel, no light brown, no auburn, and never grey, silver, white, ash,
steel or blue-grey, including the oldest passenger: her age is shown by her posture, her
glasses and her clothes, not by white hair.
EVERY PERSON IS DRAWN BY THE SAME HAND, at the same level of simplicity: every figure has
a drawn face, drawn hands and coloured clothes. NOBODY is a flat grey silhouette, a
shadow, a blob or an empty outline. EVERY PERSON IS A DIFFERENT PERSON: no two share a
face, a hairstyle, an age or an expression.

THE SCENE — A SMALL HOMEMADE FLYING SLED CARRYING EIGHT PEOPLE, travelling through a wide
swirling tunnel of time with clocks floating past it. The sled is seen from the front and
slightly to the side, moving diagonally towards the lower left of the frame, so that the
people are turned three-quarters away from us and NOBODY LOOKS AT THE VIEWER. The sled and
its passengers together fill roughly the middle half of the picture and sit slightly right
of centre; the tunnel opens away behind them towards the upper right. The mood is warm,
funny and companionable — a crowded family outing, not an action shot and not a rescue.
THE DECK IS LONG ENOUGH FOR ALL EIGHT: every person has both feet planted on the deck or
is sitting on it, and nobody hangs over the edge or floats beside it.

THE SLED IS BUILT OUT OF DENTAL-CLINIC FURNITURE — this is the joke of the picture, and
every part of it must be drawn:
  • THE DECK: a long flat pale-wood board like a small sled, its front edge curling up,
    with FOUR CREAM-COLOURED BALL FEET underneath, one at each corner. Its side edge is a
    simple pale blue band (#465885).
  • THE CONTROL DESK at the front of the deck: a mobile dental instrument cart with a
    plain pale front panel, a slim chrome-free metal frame and, on its top surface, TWO
    SIMPLE ROUNDED HAND LEVERS and three small plain round buttons. No screens, no dials
    with markings, no readouts.
  • THE LAMP: a dental treatment lamp on a long jointed metal arm rising from the back of
    the deck and curving forward over the front of the sled, its round pale lamp head
    facing the direction of travel like a headlight, lit with a soft warm glow that stays
    inside the lamp head.
  • THE SEAT: a single dental chair bolted to the middle of the deck, with a padded
    headrest, padded armrests and upholstery in the deep blue #465885.
  • A SMALL SWING-ARM TRAY beside the chair holding one plain cup and two simple
    instruments lying flat.
This craft is our own invention, assembled from clinic furniture. It is not a vehicle from
any existing cartoon, comic, film or franchise.

THE EIGHT PASSENGERS — three clinic staff and five members of the public, all East Asian,
crowded onto the deck so that they are almost spilling off it. Both hands of every person
are accounted for. Only one person's mouth is open. EACH OF THE THREE CLINIC STAFF WEARS A
DIFFERENT COLOUR OF SCRUBS — they are not a matching uniform.
  1. THE DRIVER — a woman in her thirties with her hair tied in a ponytail, in PALE TEAL
     SCRUBS (#b7d7d3, folds #99b8b4). She STANDS behind the control desk, feet apart,
     leaning forward into the wind. BOTH HANDS grip the two hand levers. She looks ahead
     along the direction of travel. HER MOUTH IS A CLOSED UPWARD CURVE — a confident,
     closed-mouth smile.
  2. THE DENTIST — a man in his forties with short neat hair, in DUSTY BLUE SCRUBS
     (#a9bcd4, folds #8fa3bd) WITH A WHITE COAT OVER THEM, the hem of the coat blown back
     behind him, his scrub collar and sleeves clearly visible. He STANDS to the driver's
     left. HIS RIGHT HAND grips the lamp arm above his head; HIS LEFT ARM is stretched out
     pointing ahead past the lamp. HE IS THE ONLY PERSON IN THE PICTURE WITH AN OPEN MOUTH
     — a small open shape, mid-shout, eyebrows raised, delighted.
  3. THE ASSISTANT — a young woman in her twenties with a short bob, in SOFT SAGE-GREEN
     SCRUBS (#bccfae, folds #a3b894). She CROUCHES at the front edge of the deck, knees
     together. BOTH HANDS hold the curled front rim of the deck. She looks out and down at
     the clocks going past. HER MOUTH IS A SMALL CLOSED SMILE and her eyebrows are raised.
  4. THE TEENAGER — a boy of about fifteen in a plain white school shirt with a satchel
     across his body. He STANDS at the left edge of the deck. HIS LEFT HAND grips the lamp
     arm; HIS RIGHT HAND points up at one large clock drifting past. He looks up at that
     clock. HIS MOUTH IS A SMALL CLOSED SMILE, eyebrows high with surprise.
  5. THE YOUNG WIFE — a woman of about thirty with long hair streaming back, in a
     clay-rose blouse. She STANDS just behind the dental chair, close beside her husband.
     HER LEFT HAND is tucked through her husband's arm; HER RIGHT HAND presses her own
     hair down at the side of her head. She looks ahead. HER MOUTH IS A CLOSED SMILE, eyes
     narrowed with laughter.
  6. THE YOUNG HUSBAND — a man of about thirty, slim, short hair, in a cream shirt with
     the sleeves rolled up. He STANDS immediately to his wife's right, shoulder to
     shoulder with her, leaning slightly into the wind. HIS LEFT HAND holds the back rail
     of the dental chair; HIS RIGHT HAND holds his wife's hand where it rests on his arm.
     He looks up at the clocks. HIS MOUTH IS A CLOSED SMILE, eyebrows relaxed.
     THESE TWO ARE CLEARLY ONE COUPLE: they stand touching, they lean the same way, and

  7. THE MIDDLE-AGED MAN — in his fifties, slightly heavier build, short hair, in a

     AND HE IS NOT FROWNING, GRUMPY, TIRED OR DISAPPROVING.
  8. THE OLDEST PASSENGER — a woman in her seventies with dark hair pinned up, small round
     glasses, a plum blouse and a cardigan. SHE IS THE ONLY ONE SITTING: she sits calmly
     and upright IN THE DENTAL CHAIR, BOTH HANDS resting on its armrests, her shoulders
     relaxed. She looks up and to the side at a clock floating above her. HER MOUTH IS A
     CLOSED UPWARD CURVE — a settled, contented smile. She is not frightened and not being
     helped: she is the most comfortable person on board.

THE TUNNEL — a wide swirling tunnel of time, painted in LOOSE WET WATERCOLOUR: broad bands
of colour spiralling from the lower left round to a vanishing point in the upper right,
their edges soft and bleeding into one another with visible blooms, backruns and granulated
pigment, on a paper ground whose grain shows through everywhere. It is colourful and
saturated, but it is PAINT, never light: no glow, no neon, no electric streaks, no lens
flare, no airbrush, no digital blur.
  • THE OUTER RINGS, at the four corners and along the edges of the frame, are the DEEPEST
    AND MOST SATURATED: deep indigo blue (#2a4677), violet and plum purple (#784e84 and
    #8e6299), and deep teal green (#2a6d69), bleeding into each other.
  • THE MIDDLE RINGS step up in lightness through mid blue (#465885), teal (#317d78) and
    soft lavender.
  • THE INNERMOST RINGS, around the vanishing point, fade to pale teal, pale cream and
    warm paper white. THE CENTRE OF THE SPIRAL IS THE LIGHTEST PART OF THE BACKGROUND, so
    the eye travels into it.
  • A FEW SMALL AMBER ACCENTS (#c28229) float in the wash, no more than a handful.
THE SLED AND EVERYONE ON IT STAY LIGHT AND BRIGHT AGAINST THIS: the deep colour belongs to
the tunnel only. Do not tint the faces, the hands, the clothes, the white coat, the wooden
deck or the dental chair with the background colours, and do not darken anyone.

THE FLOATING CLOCKS — clocks of many kinds drift through the tunnel around the sled: round
wall clocks, small pocket watches on chains, one or two sand timers, a couple of clocks
gently bent and softened as if made of cloth. They are larger near the sled and smaller
towards the vanishing point, tilted at many different angles, each drawn as a simple flat
shape with a clean hand-drawn outline. THEIR FACES ARE WARM CREAM AND THEIR RIMS ARE A
PALE WOODY AMBER, so that every clock stays clearly readable against the deep painted
rings behind it. EVERY CLOCK FACE IN THIS PICTURE IS BLANK EXCEPT FOR ITS HANDS AND A RING
OF PLAIN SHORT TICK MARKS — however many clocks there are, and wherever they are. The hands
of each clock point a different way from the others. The clocks are never metallic and
never glowing.

LIGHT AND COLOUR — the people and the sled are lit by bright, even, daylight-like light
with no single hard source, and they read as a high-key hand-drawn illustration sitting on
top of the painted tunnel. Their coloured areas sit in the range HSL saturation 30–50 and
lightness 70–85 — clearly coloured, never washed out to near-white and never heavy or
muddy. Give each named object its own colour: the three different sets of scrubs, the
white coat, the school shirt, the rose blouse, the cream shirt, the polo shirt, the plum
blouse and cardigan, the chair upholstery, the wooden deck, the lamp head. NOBODY wears
the same colour as the person beside them.
THE WHITE ATMOSPHERE LINES ARE REQUIRED — DO NOT LEAVE THEM OUT. Draw SIX OR SEVEN long,
thin, white hand-drawn lines sweeping through the tunnel in the same direction as the
spiral bands, passing behind and beside the sled but never touching any person: solid where
they begin and thinning to dry flecks, soft chalk quality, clearly hand-made. They must
never begin at anyone's mouth, nose, hand or body, never loop or curl back on themselves,
and never look like smoke, steam or breath.

MOTION MARKS — EXACTLY THREE GROUPS, and nowhere else. Each is TWO OR THREE very short,
straight, same-angle hand-drawn strokes, no longer than a third of a palm's width, solid
where they begin and fading out, never curled, never looping, never larger than the thing
they belong to: beside the driver's hands on the levers; beside the pointing man's
fingertip; beside the ball feet under the front of the deck. There are no speed lines
across the background, no radiating focus lines, no sparkles, no stars, no musical notes,
no hearts, and no speech bubbles or thought bubbles of any kind.

CRITICAL — THERE IS NO WRITING ANYWHERE IN THIS PICTURE, IN ANY LANGUAGE OR ALPHABET, and
no numerals at all. Every clock face carries hands and plain tick marks only. Leave the
surface plain on the instrument cart, the control desk, the cup, the tray, the white coat,
the scrubs, the satchel, the school shirt, the chair and every clock. There is no name
badge, no logo, no watermark, no signature, no caption and no title. Do NOT draw rows of
short dashes, squiggles or wavy lines anywhere to imitate lines of writing.
