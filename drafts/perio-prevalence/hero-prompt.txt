Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape.

STYLE — READ THIS FIRST; IT MATTERS MORE THAN ANY OTHER SECTION.
Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration, hand-made throughout. Linework in
warm dark brown or soft charcoal, NEVER pure black: thin, hand-drawn, the weight visibly
varies along a stroke, strokes taper and sometimes break and run dry at the end — never an
even mechanical vector line. Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light marker;
colour edges a little loose and not always meeting the line. Flat fills with two or three
tones per hue — no smooth decorative gradients, no airbrush, no glow. A fine even paper
grain over every surface of the whole image.
SKIN IS THE ONE EXCEPTION to the two-or-three-tones rule: each face and each hand is ONE
single flat tone, with no modelling, no blush, no shading, no cheekbones and no jawline.
On a face, draw ONLY these six things: the outline of the head, the eyes, the eyebrows,
the nose, the mouth and the ears. Nothing else at all — no wrinkles, no lines beside the
nose, no lines under the eyes, no cheek shadow, no neck shading.
HAIR IS ALWAYS A WARM DARK BROWN — #3f3330, its shadowed side #2e2523, its lit edge
#4d403c. NEVER grey, silver, white, ash, steel or blue-grey hair on anyone.
EVERY PERSON IN THIS PICTURE IS DRAWN BY THE SAME HAND, at the same level of simplicity:
every single figure — including anyone waiting in a queue and anyone appearing on a screen
— has a drawn face, drawn hands and coloured clothes. NOBODY is a flat grey silhouette,
a shadow, a blob or an empty outline.

STRUCTURE — ONE single illustration divided by THIN HAND-DRAWN LINES into a large middle
panel and four small side panels. NO ARROWS ANYWHERE.
  • THE MIDDLE PANEL fills the central 56% of the width and the full height.
  • TWO SMALL PANELS are stacked in the left 22% of the width, and TWO SMALL PANELS are
    stacked in the right 22%.

THE MIDDLE PANEL — A MODERN CONNECTED OPERATIONS AND VIDEO-CONFERENCE ROOM: the room a
public-health team works in while a nationwide survey is running, with colleagues in other
cities joined in by video. IT MUST NOT LOOK LIKE A SCHOOL CLASSROOM OR A LECTURE ROOM. It
is bright and daylit, but it is clearly a technical working room, not a teaching room.
BUILD THE ROOM FROM THESE NAMED THINGS, all of them drawn:
  • THE MAIN DISPLAY: a large wall-mounted flat display with a THIN DARK BEZEL and a slim
    metal mounting arm behind it, hung slightly proud of the wall — a piece of equipment,
    never a whiteboard in a wooden frame and never a projection screen on a tripod.
  • A SLIM CAMERA BAR mounted directly above the main display, with one small round lens.
  • A ROW OF THREE SMALL SCREENS mounted in a line on the wall to one side of the main
    display, all the same size, each with the same thin dark bezel. EACH ONE SHOWS ONE
    REMOTE COLLEAGUE, head and shoulders only, facing forward, drawn exactly as simply as
    everyone else in the picture: three different people, three different clothing colours.
    There is NO name bar, NO caption and NO text under or around them.
  • A LONG LOW MEETING TABLE in pale wood with slim metal legs, running across the lower
    middle of the room, with THREE OPEN LAPTOPS on it and ONE SMALL ROUND GREY
    SPEAKERPHONE PUCK in the centre of the table with three tiny dots on its top face.
  • A GLASS PARTITION WALL with a slim dark metal frame along the right-hand side of the
    room; through the glass a second, quieter room is faintly visible.
  • A SLIM EQUIPMENT RACK standing against the back wall: a narrow cabinet with a few
    horizontal slots and a few small round dots on its front. No lights, no glow.
  • TWO RECESSED LINEAR CEILING LIGHTS running front to back, and a THIN CABLE TRAY running
    horizontally along the upper wall.
  • THE FLOOR IS PALE COOL GREY with a faint seam grid, not wooden boards.
  • ONE POTTED PLANT in a corner and ONE WHEELED OFFICE CHAIR, to keep the room human.
  • A LARGE WINDOW on the left giving clean daylight. A round wall clock whose face is
    completely blank.

ON THE MAIN DISPLAY:
  • IN THE CENTRE, ONE ISLAND — the island of Taiwan, drawn as a single simplified
    silhouette. USE THE ATTACHED REFERENCE IMAGE FOR ITS SHAPE AND PROPORTIONS ONLY, NOT
    FOR ITS STYLE: clearly TALLER THAN IT IS WIDE, about twice as tall as it is wide; its
    western edge smooth and gently curved, its eastern edge straighter and steeper; the
    northern end blunt, the southern end narrowing to a soft point. Draw the island TALL —
    it takes up at least two thirds of the height of the display. It is the single most
    important object in the whole picture and must still read as an island when the picture
    is shrunk to the size of a postage stamp.
  • THE ISLAND IS SHADED LIKE A HEAT MAP in three flat steps of ONE colour: the palest areas
    a light tint of #317d78, the middle areas #317d78 itself, the few deepest patches
    #2a6d69. Soft irregular patches meeting edge to edge. DO NOT draw internal borders,
    administrative regions, county outlines, grid lines or a dotted coastline.
  • FOUR SMALL AMBER DOTS (#c28229) sit on the island, spread from north to south.
  • FOUR NUMBERS float beside the island, two on its left and two on its right, each joined
    to the EDGE of the island by one thin short leader line. They are exactly: 78.7% and
    24.7% on one side, 9.3% and 6.9% on the other — plain, upright, evenly spaced sans-serif
    digits followed by a percent sign, nothing else. EACH NUMBER SITS CLEARLY OUTSIDE THE
    ISLAND with a gap around it: no number touches, overlaps or sits on top of the island.
  • TWO SMALL AMBER WARNING TRIANGLES (#c28229), each no wider than a finger, sit near the
    island — a plain triangle with a short vertical stroke and a dot below it inside.
  • CHARTS SIT ROUND THE ISLAND ON THE SAME DISPLAY, small and simple, clean flat shapes: a
    column chart of FIVE bars of clearly different heights; a line chart rising gently to
    the right with small round dots at its turning points; a horizontal bar chart of FOUR
    bars; a pie chart of just TWO parts, the larger about three quarters of the circle; and
    a five-pointed radar chart with one filled polygon inside. EVERY CHART HAS NO AXIS
    LABELS, NO LEGEND, NO TITLE AND NO NUMBERS — shapes only. Use the pale tint, #317d78 and
    #2a6d69, with #c28229 only once or twice as a single highlighted bar or dot.

FIVE PEOPLE WORK IN THIS ROOM, all East Asian, none looking at the viewer, none standing in

  • TWO PEOPLE STAND TOGETHER IN FRONT OF THE MAIN DISPLAY, seen from behind and slightly to
    the side, much smaller than the display: a woman on the left with a short bob, wearing a

    island, her mouth slightly open because she is speaking; a man beside her holding an
    open paper folder against his chest, looking where she points.
  • ONE MAN SITS AT THE MEETING TABLE in the lower left, leaning forward, both hands on a

  • ONE WOMAN WALKS ACROSS the lower right carrying a stack of papers, her head turned

  • ONE MAN STANDS at the equipment rack on the right wearing a SLIM HEADSET, one hand

Their expressions are calm, alert and busy — they are working, mid-task, mid-sentence.
Nobody is alarmed, nobody is celebrating, nobody is pointing at the viewer.
THE LAPTOP SCREENS each show ONE simple shape only: one a small column chart of four bars,
one a single rising line, one a small plain island silhouette. Nothing else on them — no
code, no text, no icons, no logos.

THE FOUR SIDE PANELS — four different places where ordinary people are having their teeth

lived-in room with the named props below, and in each one SOMETHING IS HAPPENING — someone

  • TOP LEFT — A CLINIC RECEPTION DESK. A woman in her fifties stands at the counter handing

  • BOTTOM LEFT — A DENTAL SURGERY. A patient lies back in a dental chair, mouth closed,

PEOPLE — Simplified illustrative people: minimal features (small dot or short-line eyes, a
simple line mouth, a small nose), natural realistic head-to-body proportions, believable
ages, East Asian faces. Not photorealistic. Not faceless. No oversized heads, no noodle
limbs. Nobody looks at the viewer. Roughly half are women and half are men, of mixed ages
from young child to sixties. When someone is speaking, draw the mouth as a small open shape
rather than a closed line — never an exaggerated cartoon shout.
CLOTHING — clinic staff wear short-sleeved clinic tops in #b7d7d3 with their folds and
shadows in #99b8b4. Everyone else wears ordinary everyday clothes, and EVERY PERSON'S
GARMENT IS A DIFFERENT COLOUR from the people next to them — muted clay rose, mustard,
sage, soft blue, cream, warm grey. Clothes are NEVER flat single-tone shapes: model every
garment with two or three tones of its own colour, with soft coloured-pencil shading in the
folds, along the sleeves, under the collar and at the hem, and draw the real details —
collar, cuff, sleeve seam, hem, a pocket.

LIGHT AND COLOUR — Ordinary bright indoor daytime, ONE light source: clean daylight from
the left, warm and even. No long orange shadows, not sunset, not lamplight, no spotlight,
no screens lighting faces from below, no dark room. Across the upper part of the middle
panel only, above the people and not touching them, draw FOUR OR FIVE long, thin, white
hand-drawn lines running the same way at the same shallow angle, evenly spaced, solid where
they begin and thinning to dry flecks — soft chalk quality, clearly hand-made. These lines
must never begin at anyone's mouth, nose, hand or body, must never loop or curl back on
themselves, must never cross the panel dividing lines, and must never resemble smoke, steam
or breath.
Most coloured areas sit in the range HSL saturation 30–50 and lightness 70–85 — clearly
coloured and readable, never washed out into near-white pastels and never heavy or muddy.
About half the picture is chromatic. Use AT LEAST FIVE clearly different colours in each
panel and give each to a named object: the clinic tops, the patients' clothes, the walls,
the table, the potted plant, the chairs, the folders, the supply box, the curtains.
ACCENT — #317d78 and #2a6d69 belong to the island, the charts and the clinic tops. #c28229
appears ONLY in the four small dots, the two warning triangles and one highlighted bar. Do
NOT wash the image in either colour, do NOT tint the skin, the walls, the floor or the paper
with them, and do NOT make them vivid.

MOTION AND SPEECH MARKS — THE WHOLE PICTURE SHOULD FEEL BUSY, NOT STILL. Every mark is the
same kind: TWO OR THREE very short, straight, same-angle hand-drawn strokes, no longer than
a third of a palm's width, solid where they begin and fading out, never curled, never
looping, never bigger than the thing they belong to. Place them in these nine places and
nowhere else:
  • beside the pointing woman's fingertip, and two short diagonal strokes beside her mouth
    because she is speaking;
  • above the seated man's wrists as he types;
  • beside the walking woman's shoulder, and two short strokes beside her mouth;
  • beside the standing man's hand at the equipment rack;
  • TOP LEFT PANEL: two short strokes beside the receptionist's mouth, and two beside the
    card as it changes hands;
  • BOTTOM LEFT PANEL: two short strokes beside the dentist's mouth;
  • TOP RIGHT PANEL: two short strokes beside the dentist's raised hand and two beside her
    mouth;
  • BOTTOM RIGHT PANEL: two short strokes beside the dentist's raised hand.
NO speech bubbles of any kind, no thought bubbles, no speed lines across a background, no
radiating focus lines, no sparkles, no musical notes, no hearts.

CRITICAL — THE ONLY CHARACTERS ANYWHERE IN THIS PICTURE ARE: the digits and percent signs
78.7% , 24.7% , 9.3% and 6.9% on the main display, and the short exclamation stroke inside
the two small amber warning triangles. THERE IS NO OTHER WRITING OF ANY KIND ANYWHERE — no
place names, no country name, no axis labels, no legends, no chart titles, no headings, no
captions, no name bars under the people on the small screens, no numbers on the clock, no
numbers on the charts, no words on folders, laptops, keyboards, screens, pictures, badges,
boxes, doors or clothing, in any language or alphabet. Do NOT draw rows of short dashes,
squiggles or wavy lines anywhere to imitate lines of writing. Where writing would normally
appear, leave the surface plain. There are no logos and no watermarks.
