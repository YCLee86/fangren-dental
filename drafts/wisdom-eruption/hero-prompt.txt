Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape. ONE single scene — one room, one floor, one
horizon. There are no panels, no borders, no frames, no dividing lines and no arrows
anywhere in this picture.

STYLE — READ THIS FIRST; IT MATTERS MORE THAN ANY OTHER SECTION.
Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration, hand-made throughout. Linework in
warm dark brown or soft charcoal, NEVER pure black: thin, hand-drawn, the weight visibly
varies along a stroke, strokes taper and sometimes break and run dry at the end — never an
even mechanical vector line. Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light marker;
colour edges a little loose and not always meeting the line. Flat fills with two or three
tones per hue — no smooth decorative gradients, no airbrush, no glow, no neon, no
cel-shading. A fine even paper grain over every surface of the whole image, including the
background. The picture is warm and high-key with plenty of pale cream paper showing
through, but every named object still carries a real colour and no two people wear the same
colour. This is NOT anime, NOT manga, NOT a screenshot from a cartoon, NOT 3D rendering,
NOT photographic.

FACES AND HANDS — READ THIS SECOND.
SKIN IS THE ONE EXCEPTION to the two-or-three-tones rule: each face and each hand is ONE
single flat warm tone, with no modelling, no blush, no shading, no cheekbones and no
jawline. On a face, draw ONLY these six things: the outline of the head, the eyes, the
eyebrows, the nose, the mouth and the ears. Nothing else at all — no wrinkles, no lines
beside the nose, no lines under the eyes, no cheek shadow, no neck shading, no highlight
in the eye. Eyes are small solid dots or short lines — never large glossy cartoon eyes,
never white eyeballs with a shine. Eyebrows are one short thin arc set well away from the
eye, and THE EYEBROWS ARE WHAT CARRY THE FEELING. Hands are simple: four fingers and a
thumb, no knuckle lines, no nails.
EVERY SINGLE PERSON IN THIS PICTURE HAS THE SAME WARM DARK BROWN HAIR WITH A RED CAST —
#483837, its shadowed side #392928, its lit edge #4f4040. It is never pure black, never
neutral grey-black, and never blonde, caramel, light brown, auburn, grey, silver or white.
Everyone in this picture is East Asian. Every figure has a drawn face, drawn hands and
coloured clothes — nobody is a flat grey silhouette, a shadow or an empty outline.

WHAT IS HAPPENING — A YOUNG MAN HAS COME IN BECAUSE THE TOOTH RIGHT AT THE BACK IS SWOLLEN
AND SORE, AND HE IS TELLING THE DENTIST SO.
THE PATIENT sits in the RIGHT-HAND THIRD of the picture on an ordinary upholstered
consultation chair — he is NOT lying back in a dental chair. He is a man of about twenty in
a rust-red zip jacket over a cream T-shirt and dark indigo jeans. He is turned towards the
right, facing the dentist. THE HAND NEARER THE VIEWER is raised to the side of his own
face, the fingertips resting on his jaw low down and well back, close to the angle of the
jaw below the ear — he is showing her where it hurts. His eyebrows are pulled together a
little and his mouth is a short flat line: uncomfortable, but not in agony and not
frightened.
THE DENTIST stands at the RIGHT-HAND EDGE of the picture, facing left towards him,
listening. She is a woman of about forty with a NEAT CHIN-LENGTH BOB. SHE WEARS MUTED
VIOLET SCRUBS — top and trousers both violet, #8e6299, its shadowed folds #784e84 — WITH AN
OPEN WHITE DOCTOR'S COAT over them, the coat unbuttoned and hanging loose, its collar and
lapels clearly drawn. Her near hand is lifted a little, palm turned slightly up, the way a
person's hand moves when they are listening and about to answer. Her eyebrows are raised
just a little and her mouth is a short calm line — she is attentive, she is not alarmed and
she is not grinning.
Never swap them: THE PATIENT IS SEATED AND IS TO THE LEFT OF THE DENTIST; THE DENTIST IS
STANDING AND IS THE FIGURE CLOSEST TO THE RIGHT-HAND EDGE.

THE SPEECH BUBBLE — WHAT HE IS SAYING, DRAWN AND NOT WRITTEN.
ONE single large speech bubble with soft rounded corners floats in the upper middle of the
picture, above the empty wall and clear of the dentist's head. It has ONE short tail
pointing down towards the patient's mouth. It is a speech bubble, not a thought bubble:
no trailing circles. It has NO dividing lines inside it, no arrows and NO WRITING OF ANY
KIND — it contains ONE drawing and nothing else.
INSIDE THE BUBBLE, draw the back corner of his own lower jaw the way he would see it in a
mirror: a short row of THREE back teeth seen from the side and slightly above, clean and
pale off-white, sitting in pink gum. THE LAST TOOTH IN THE ROW — the one at the back, with
NOTHING behind it — is only HALF OUT: a flap of gum still lies over the back half of its
crown. THAT FLAP IS PUFFED UP, its edge rounded and raised so that it visibly overlaps the
edge of the tooth, and it is ONE CLEAR TONE PINKER than the calm pale pink gum beside it.
There is NO blood, no red anywhere else, nothing glistening and nothing alarming.
Keep it simple and hand-drawn, NOT a textbook diagram: no roots, no cross-section, no
dashed lines, no measuring lines, no circles, no crosses, no pointer and no labels.

COLOUR.
Many colours, but every one of them held low and slightly dusty: brick red, mustard, sage
green, muted teal, grey-blue, clay, warm grey and cream white. Nothing is vivid and nothing
is neon, but nothing is washed out to near-white either — each named object keeps a real,
readable colour. Any white line work is drawn ON TOP of the colour, not left as bare paper.
THE VIOLET #8e6299 BELONGS TO THE DENTIST'S SCRUBS AND TO NOTHING ELSE. It is the strongest
single colour in the picture and it appears in that one place only. Never wash the whole
picture in violet, never use a bright or candy purple, and never give anyone violet skin,
violet hair, violet walls or a violet floor. The walls are warm off-white, the floor is pale
warm grey, the ribbon is clay.
KEEP THE TEETH OUT OF IT: the teeth in the bubble and on the film stay a clean pale bone
white and take none of this colour. The gum is pale pink, and the only stronger pink in the
picture is the swollen flap inside the bubble.

LIGHT AND AIR.

From that window draw THREE OR FOUR SHORT, PARALLEL, EVENLY SPACED light strokes, all
