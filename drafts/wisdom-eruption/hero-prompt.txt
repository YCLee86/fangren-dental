Editorial illustration for a dental clinic's article, 16:9 landscape. ONE single scene in
one room. No panels, no borders, no frames.

STYLE — THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT SECTION, AND IT MATTERS MORE THAN EVERYTHING BELOW IT.
The attached reference image "HOUSE STYLE" is an existing illustration from the same
website. COPY ITS FINISH EXACTLY — its line, its colour, its softness, its texture. COPY
NOTHING OF ITS CONTENT: not its layout, not its bubble, not its street scene, not its
people, not its props.
The finish to copy, in words:
• LINE: a soft hand-drawn contour in warm grey-brown, THIN and gentle, very slightly
  wobbly, occasionally doubled where a fold or a hem is. NEVER black, NEVER thick, NEVER a
  crisp even mechanical vector outline and NEVER a bold inked comic outline.
• COLOUR: pale milky pastels — soft mint and sage green, dusty lavender, mustard yellow,
  warm cream, soft blue-grey, pale pink, muted brick red. Everything is light and
  desaturated. A LOT of white paper is left showing, especially on the walls and the floor.
• SHADING: soft and pencil-like, laid inside the shape with a slightly soft edge, one or
  two steps only. No hard cel shading, no black shadows, no glossy highlights, no glow.
• TEXTURE: a fine soft pencil-and-paper grain over the whole picture, background included.
• FACES: round and friendly, drawn small and simple — small dot eyes, one short eyebrow
  stroke, a tiny nose, a small calm mouth, and a soft round blush on each cheek. No
  detailed features, no cheekbones, no jawline, no eyelashes, no shine in the eye.
• Every surface is drawn: the walls, the floor and the furniture all carry their own gentle
  line and pale colour. Nothing is left as a blank white box.
This is NOT anime, NOT manga, NOT a flat vector illustration, NOT a clean infographic, NOT
3D rendering and NOT photographic.
EVERY PERSON IN THIS PICTURE IS EAST ASIAN AND HAS THE SAME WARM DARK BROWN HAIR — never
black, never blonde, never grey. Hands are simple: four fingers and a thumb, no knuckle
lines, no nails.

WHAT IS HAPPENING — A YOUNG MAN HAS COME IN BECAUSE THE TOOTH RIGHT AT THE BACK IS SWOLLEN
AND SORE, AND HE IS TELLING THE DENTIST SO.
THE DENTIST stands at the LEFT-HAND EDGE of the picture, facing RIGHT, listening. She is a
woman of about forty with a NEAT CHIN-LENGTH BOB. SHE WEARS MUTED VIOLET SCRUBS — top and
trousers both violet, #8e6299 — WITH AN OPEN WHITE DOCTOR'S COAT over them, unbuttoned and
hanging loose, its collar and lapels clearly drawn. Her near hand is lifted a little, palm
turned slightly up, the way a hand moves when someone is listening and about to answer.
Her eyebrows are raised just a little; her mouth is a small calm line.
THE PATIENT sits just to her RIGHT on an ordinary upholstered consultation chair — he is
NOT lying back in a dental chair. He is a man of about twenty in a rust-red zip jacket over
a cream T-shirt and dark indigo jeans. He is turned to his LEFT, towards the dentist. THE
HAND NEARER THE VIEWER is raised to the side of his own face, the fingertips resting low
and well back on his jaw, close to the angle of the jaw below the ear — he is showing her
where it hurts. His eyebrows are pulled together a little and his mouth is a short flat
line: uncomfortable, but not in agony and not frightened.
NEVER SWAP THEM: the dentist is standing and is the figure closest to the LEFT edge; the
patient is seated and is to the RIGHT of her.

THE SPEECH BUBBLE — WHAT HE IS SAYING, DRAWN AND NOT WRITTEN.
ONE single large speech bubble with soft rounded corners floats in the UPPER LEFT of the
picture, above the two adults and clear of both their heads. It has one short tail pointing
down towards the patient. It is a speech bubble, not a thought bubble: no trailing circles.
It has no dividing lines inside it and NO WRITING OF ANY KIND — it holds ONE drawing and
nothing else.
INSIDE THE BUBBLE, draw the back corner of his own lower jaw the way he would see it in a
mirror: a short row of THREE back teeth seen from the side and slightly above, clean and
pale off-white, sitting in pale pink gum. THE LAST TOOTH IN THE ROW — the one at the back,
with NOTHING behind it — is only HALF OUT: a flap of gum still lies across the back half of
its crown. THAT FLAP IS PUFFED UP, its edge rounded and raised so that it visibly overlaps
the edge of the tooth, and it is ONE CLEAR TONE PINKER than the calm gum beside it. There
is NO blood and nothing alarming.
Draw it soft and hand-drawn in the house style, NOT as a textbook diagram: no roots, no
cross-section, no dashed lines, no measuring lines, no circles, no crosses, no pointer.

THE GROWING-UP ARROW.
ONE thick wide arrow shaped like a long S sweeps in from the RIGHT-HAND EDGE of the
picture, dips down through the middle of the room, rises again and ENDS IN A SINGLE
ARROWHEAD POINTING LEFT, at the seated patient beside his chair. It is the only arrow in
the picture: one head, at the left end, and no head at the tail.
IT IS NOT LYING ON THE FLOOR. IT IS NOT A CARPET, NOT A RUG AND NOT A PATH. It stands up in
the picture, flat and face-on, like a wide painted ribbon floating in front of the room: the
room's floor and skirting are visible underneath it and behind it, and the arrow never takes
the floor's perspective and never has anything of the room lying on top of it.
THIS ARROW IS THE ONE THING IN THE PICTURE THAT IS ALLOWED A GRADIENT: it fades smoothly
from very pale warm sand at its tail on the right to a deeper warm clay at the arrowhead on
the left, and the band grows wider as it approaches the head. It has no outline, no pattern,
no stripes, no texture and no writing on it.
FIVE FIGURES STAND ON THE ARROW, their feet on the band and NOT on the floor of the room.
THEY ARE ALL THE SAME BOY at five ages, and THE SIXTH AND LAST STAGE IS THE SEATED PATIENT
HIMSELF at the point of the arrow — do not draw a sixth standing figure. All six share the
same hair, the same face, the same nose and the same eyes. Each stage wears a different
colour.

THE X-RAY LIGHT BOX — IT HAS TO READ AS A LIGHT BOX, NOT AS A PICTURE ON THE WALL.
A slim viewing box fixed flat to the wall, about as wide as two hands, with a THIN PALE

than as a framed picture, and all three must be drawn:
  · THE PANEL GLOWS. A soft warm-white light sits behind the film, the lit panel is visibly

  · A THIN CLIP OR LIP ALONG ITS TOP EDGE holds the film, and the film hangs from it — the

It is NEVER a picture frame, NEVER a framed artwork, painting or poster, NEVER a television
