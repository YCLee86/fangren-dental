Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape.

REFERENCE IMAGES — Two reference pictures are attached. Take ONE thing from each and
nothing else.
  • THE PHOTOGRAPH OF A CHIPPED CROWN — take ONLY the shape of the tooth cap and the way
    the porcelain has broken off it: the large irregular area of exposed DARK GREY metal
    covering most of the front face, with only a narrow rim of pale porcelain surviving
    around its edges. Do NOT copy anything else from it — not the photographic look, not
    the mouth, the lips, the gums, the neighbouring teeth or the yellow staining.
  • THE DRAWING OF A CLINIC CONSULTATION ROOM — take ONLY the kind of room and its
    furniture: the ring binders standing in rows on shelving, the pale desk, the wheeled
    office chairs, the window blinds, the framed picture, the potted plant. Do NOT copy its
    colours — that room is pink and warm, and this one must follow the COLOUR section
    below instead.

STYLE — THIS MATTERS MORE THAN ANY OTHER SECTION.
Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration, hand-made throughout. Linework in
warm dark brown or soft charcoal, NEVER pure black: thin, hand-drawn, the weight visibly
varies along a stroke, strokes taper and sometimes break and run dry at the end — never an
even mechanical vector line. Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light marker;
colour edges a little loose and not always meeting the line. Flat fills with two or three
tones per hue — no smooth decorative gradients, no airbrush, no glow. A fine even paper
grain over every surface of the whole image.
SKIN IS THE ONE EXCEPTION to the two-or-three-tones rule: each face and each hand is ONE
single flat tone, with no modelling, no blush, no shading, no cheekbones and no jawline.
On a face, draw ONLY these six things: the outline of the head, the eyes, the eyebrows,
the nose, the mouth and the ears. Nothing else at all.
HAIR IS ALWAYS A WARM DARK BROWN — #3f3330, its shadowed side #2e2523, its lit edge
#4d403c. NEVER grey, silver, white, ash, steel or blue-grey hair.

STRUCTURE — ONE single illustration, no panels and no dividing lines. The left two-thirds
of the frame is a dental clinic consultation room where a dentist is showing something to
a patient across a desk. Floating in the upper right of the frame is ONE large
rounded-rectangle speech bubble with a thin hand-drawn outline and a short tapering tail
pointing back towards the dentist. The bubble is about two-fifths of the frame wide and
reaches nearly to the top edge. There is NO arrow anywhere and never more than one bubble.

THE CROWN IN HER HAND — the first thing the reader must see. The dentist holds it between
the thumb and forefinger of the hand nearer the viewer, lifted to about the height of her
chin, turned so its broken face is square to the viewer. DRAW IT MUCH LARGER THAN A REAL
TOOTH — about the size of a walnut in her fingers — so that every part of it is clearly
readable; this change of scale is deliberate and must not be corrected.
IT MUST READ INSTANTLY AS A TOOTH, so give it the shape of a FRONT TOOTH, not a lumpy cap:
a flat blade-like tooth, wider at its straight biting edge, with two soft rounded corners
there, the sides tapering gently down to a narrower neck. Not a pebble, not a bean, not a
shell, not a blob.
MOST OF ITS FRONT FACE IS BROKEN AWAY. Following the attached photograph: a large irregular
patch of DARK GREY METAL, taking up roughly two thirds of the tooth's visible face and
reaching the biting edge, with only a NARROW RIM of pale warm off-white porcelain left
around it — a band down each side and a thin lip along the bottom. The boundary between
metal and porcelain is a clearly drawn irregular line, and along that line the porcelain
shows as a visible THIN LAYER seen edge-on, a fine pale band sitting on top of the metal.
The dark metal must be strongly dark against the pale porcelain — not grey-white, not
silver, not shiny. Two or three small pale porcelain flakes lie in her other open palm.

THE BUBBLE — Inside it, drawn simply and cleanly: ONE ordinary Taiwanese apartment block
seen from the street below at a slight upward angle, five or six storeys, still lived in.
Its whole facade is faced with SMALL SQUARE WALL TILES in a regular grid — pale warm
off-white tiles, crossed by TWO OR THREE HORIZONTAL BANDS of tiles in #335b8b running the
full width of the building between floors, the way Taiwanese apartment blocks use a second
tile colour as a stripe. Keep the facade simple: plain window openings, a rooftop water
tank, a few potted plants and a laundry pole on a balcony. DO NOT draw window grilles or
security bars on the windows.
THE TILES HAVE BEEN FALLING OFF FOR YEARS, and this must show in THREE ways at once:
  • ONE LARGE CONTINUOUS PATCH high on the wall, roughly the size of a window, where the
    tiles are completely gone and the rough darker grey cement bed underneath is exposed,
    with a ragged fringe of half-attached tiles around its edge;
  • MANY SINGLE TILES MISSING ALL OVER THE REST OF THE FACADE — thirty or forty small dark
    square gaps scattered irregularly across the grid, some alone, some in twos and threes,
    heaviest near the large patch and thinning out lower down, so the whole wall reads
    speckled and pitted, like a picture with pixels knocked out of it;
  • THREE OR FOUR TILES CAUGHT IN MID-AIR, falling away from the wall below the large
    patch, tumbling at different angles — it is still happening right now.
Add faint pale streaks of rain-staining running down from the gaps. On the pavement at the
foot of the building: a scatter of fallen tiles, two of them snapped in half, and TWO
ORANGE-AND-WHITE TRAFFIC CONES with a simple plain barrier rail between them. The building
is weathered but plainly still someone's home — NOT a ruin, NOT abandoned, NOT collapsing;
no crack through the structure, no scaffolding, no people underneath, nobody in danger.
KEEP THE BUILDING INSIDE THE BUBBLE and the tooth outside it: no tiles anywhere in the
room, and no tooth shape anywhere inside the bubble.

THE TWO PEOPLE — On the RIGHT side of the desk sits the DENTIST, a woman in her thirties
with short hair, seen three-quarters from the front, sitting upright, wearing clinic
scrubs. Her expression is friendly, attentive and matter-of-fact — she is explaining an
ordinary thing, not delivering bad news, and she looks towards the patient.
On the LEFT sits the PATIENT, a man of about forty-five, TURNED SO THAT WE CLEARLY SEE HIS
FACE — three-quarters towards the viewer, not from behind and not in profile. He is looking
at the broken crown in her fingers. HIS EXPRESSION IS THE SECOND MOST IMPORTANT THING IN
THE PICTURE: he is TAKEN ABACK — mildly astonished that such a thing happens at all. Both
eyebrows are raised and level, his eyes are open a little wider than usual, his mouth is
slightly open, and one hand has come up a few inches off the desk, palm turned upward in a
small unfinished gesture. He is surprised and interested, NOT frightened, NOT upset, NOT in
pain, NOT comical, NOT open-mouthed in shock. Never put the dentist on the left and never
put the patient on the right. Neither looks at the viewer.

THE ROOM — A REAL DENTAL CLINIC CONSULTATION OFFICE, not a domestic dining room. It must
NOT look homely: no wooden dining table, no wooden dining chairs, no rug, no wooden
floorboards, no kitchen furniture. Instead: a pale smooth desk with a plain rounded edge;
two wheeled office chairs with low backs; behind the dentist, open shelving with a row of
upright ring binders and a couple of plain storage boxes; a computer monitor on the desk
turned away from us so its screen is not visible; a slim window with pale venetian blinds
on the left giving clean daylight; ONE framed picture on the wall whose image is fixed and
must be exactly this — soft rounded hills in three flat bands of colour, nothing else; a
small potted plant; a pale smooth floor. The walls and floor carry colour and are never
left blank white. Do NOT add any poster, chart, diagram, X-ray, plaster model, visible
screen content or picture of teeth or buildings to the room.

PEOPLE — Simplified illustrative people: minimal features (small dot or short-line eyes, a
simple line mouth, a small nose), natural realistic head-to-body proportions, believable
ages, East Asian faces. Not photorealistic. Not faceless. No oversized heads, no noodle
limbs. Hands are drawn at a normal size, never splayed and never larger than a face.
CLOTHING — the dentist wears short-sleeved clinic scrubs in #b7c5d7 with folds and shadows
in #99a7b8. The patient wears a muted clay-rose knitted top with warm grey trousers — a
clearly different colour family. Clothes are NEVER flat single-tone shapes: model every
garment with two or three tones of its own colour, with soft coloured-pencil shading in the
folds, and draw the real details — collar, cuff, sleeve seam, hem, a pocket.

LIGHT AND COLOUR — Ordinary bright indoor daytime, ONE light source: clean daylight through
the blinds on the left, warm and even. No long orange shadows, not sunset, not lamplight,
no spotlight on the crown. Across the upper part of the frame, above the two people and
clear of the bubble, draw FOUR OR FIVE long, thin, white hand-drawn lines running the same
way at the same shallow angle, evenly spaced, solid where they begin and thinning to dry
flecks — soft chalk quality, clearly hand-made. They must never begin at anyone's mouth,
nose, hand or body, must never cross a face, must never loop or curl back on themselves,
and must never resemble smoke, steam or breath.
THIS PICTURE MUST NOT GO GREY. Most coloured areas sit in the range HSL saturation 30–50
and lightness 70–85, and at least half the picture is chromatic. Use AT LEAST FIVE clearly
different colours and give each to a named thing: the scrubs, the patient's knitted top,
the room wall, the ring binders on the shelf, the potted plant, the framed hills, the sky
and the balcony plants inside the bubble. Colour throughout — never greyscale.
ACCENT — the blue #335b8b appears in exactly three places and nowhere else: the horizontal
tile bands on the building inside the bubble, one panel of the shelving unit, and a narrow
horizontal band along the room wall. Do NOT wash the image in that colour, do NOT tint the
skin, the crown, the desk or the paper with it, and do NOT make it vivid. There is NO pink,
red or warm rose anywhere except the patient's top.

CRITICAL — NO WRITING ANYWHERE IN THE IMAGE. No text, letters, words, numbers, logos, shop
signs, house numbers, labels on the ring binders, captions or watermarks, in any language
or alphabet. The ring binder spines are completely blank. The monitor screen is not
visible. Inside the bubble every wall and shutter is blank. The traffic cones and barrier
carry no writing. There are no arrows, ticks, crosses, plus or minus signs or dotted lines
anywhere. Where writing would normally appear, leave the surface plain.

AVOID — greyscale; a picture that reads as mostly grey; photorealism; a photorealistic
mouth, gum or hand; faceless or noodle-limbed figures; thick uniform black outlines; even
mechanical vector linework; chrome, metallic or iridescent gradients; glow, sparkle or lens
flare; grey, silver, white or blue-grey hair; wrinkles, cheek shadow or facial modelling;
the patient drawn from behind or with his face hidden; a frightened, upset, pained or
cartoonishly shocked patient; the crown drawn at true life size, small, or as a rounded
pebble, bean or shell; the broken area drawn small or in the middle of the face only; the
exposed metal drawn pale, silver, shiny or the same colour as the porcelain; a homely
dining room, wooden dining table, wooden dining chairs, rug or wooden floorboards; window
grilles or security bars on the building; more than one bubble; any arrow, pointer or
comparison mark; a second building or a before-and-after comparison inside the bubble; a
ruined, abandoned or collapsing building, structural cracks, scaffolding, workers, an
accident, an injured person, blood, an ambulance, flashing lights or barrier tape; people
standing under the falling tiles; blood, wounds, redness, gums or a mouth anywhere; any
tooth shape inside the bubble; any tile anywhere in the room; anyone looking at the viewer;
a whole image tinted blue; flat untextured single-tone clothing; a bare empty room.
