Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape.

STYLE — READ THIS FIRST; IT MATTERS MORE THAN ANY OTHER SECTION.
Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration, hand-made throughout. Linework in
warm dark brown or soft charcoal, NEVER pure black: thin, hand-drawn, the weight visibly
varies along a stroke, strokes taper and sometimes break and run dry at the end — never an
even mechanical vector line. Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light marker;
colour edges a little loose and not always meeting the line. Flat fills with two or three
tones per hue — no smooth decorative gradients, no airbrush, no glow. A fine even paper
grain over every surface of the whole image.
SKIN IS THE ONE EXCEPTION to the two-or-three-tones rule: each face and each hand is ONE
single flat tone, with no modelling, no blush, no shading, no cheekbones and no jawline.
On a face, draw ONLY these six things: the outline of the head, the eyes, the eyebrows,
the nose, the mouth and the ears. Nothing else at all — no line beside the nose, no line
under the eye, no shadow under the cheekbone, no shading on the neck or the hands.
HAIR IS ALWAYS A WARM DARK BROWN — #3f3330, its shadowed side #2e2523, its lit edge
#4d403c. NEVER grey, silver, white, ash, steel or blue-grey hair.

STRUCTURE — ONE single illustration, no panels and no dividing lines. The left two-thirds
of the frame is a dental consultation corner where a dentist is showing something small to
a patient. Floating in the upper right of the frame, over the wall behind them, is ONE
large rounded-rectangle speech bubble with a thin hand-drawn outline and a short tapering
tail pointing back towards the dentist. The bubble is about two-fifths of the frame wide
and reaches nearly to the top edge. There is NO arrow anywhere and never more than one
bubble.

THE CROWN IN HER HAND — This is the first thing the reader must see. The dentist holds it
between the thumb and forefinger of the hand nearer the viewer, lifted to about the height
of her chin, turned so its damaged side faces the viewer. DRAW IT MUCH LARGER THAN A REAL
TOOTH — about the size of a walnut in her fingers — so that every part of it is clearly
readable; this deliberate change of scale is intended and must not be corrected. It is a
tooth-shaped cap, a hollow bell shape with a rounded biting top. Its surface is a pale warm
off-white ceramic, and A LARGE PIECE OF THAT CERAMIC HAS BROKEN AWAY from the upper corner
nearest the viewer — an irregular patch with a slightly jagged edge, big enough to take a
clear bite out of the cap's outline. Through the break, DARK GREY-BLUE METAL is exposed
underneath, strongly dark against the pale ceramic. Along the edge of the break, draw the
ceramic as a visible THIN LAYER seen edge-on — a fine pale band sitting on top of the dark
metal — so it is plain that this thing is two materials and the outer one came off. One or
two small pale ceramic flakes rest in her open other palm below.

THE BUBBLE — WHAT SHE IS DESCRIBING. Inside it, drawn simply and cleanly with plenty of
room around it: ONE ordinary four-storey Taiwanese townhouse seen straight on from the
street, some years old and still lived in — laundry poles, a rooftop water tank, window
grilles, an air-conditioning unit, a covered arcade at the bottom. Its whole front is faced
with SMALL RECTANGULAR WALL TILES in a regular grid with thin grout lines. THE TILES HAVE
BEEN COMING OFF FOR YEARS, and this must be shown in TWO different ways at once:
  • ONE LARGE CONTINUOUS PATCH high on the wall, roughly the size of a window, where the
    tiles are completely gone and the rough darker grey cement bed underneath is exposed,
    with a ragged fringe of half-attached tiles around its edge;
  • AND, spread over the rest of the facade, MANY SINGLE TILES MISSING HERE AND THERE —
    fifteen or twenty small dark gaps scattered irregularly across the grid, some alone,
    some in twos and threes, thinning out towards the lower floors. The grid must read as
    gappy and uneven all over, not damaged in only one place.
Add faint pale streaks of rain-staining running down from the gaps. On the pavement at the
foot of the building lie a handful of fallen tiles, one or two snapped in half. The house
is weathered but plainly still someone's home — it is NOT a ruin, NOT abandoned, NOT
collapsing, and there is no crack through the structure, no scaffolding and no cordon.
KEEP THE BUILDING INSIDE THE BUBBLE and keep the tooth outside it: there are no tiles
anywhere in the room, and no tooth shape anywhere inside the bubble.

THE TWO PEOPLE — On the RIGHT of the small table sits the DENTIST, a woman in her thirties
with short hair, seen three-quarters from the front, sitting upright. Her expression is
friendly, attentive and matter-of-fact — she is explaining an ordinary thing, not
delivering bad news, and she looks towards the patient, never at the viewer.
On the LEFT sits the PATIENT, a man of about forty-five, seen from behind and to one side
so we read his shoulder, the back of his head and a sliver of his cheek. He leans forward
towards the crown in her fingers, one forearm on the table, plainly looking at it. Never
put the dentist on the left and never put the patient on the right. Neither looks at the
viewer, and neither is taller than half the frame.

THE ROOM — a small, warm, real consultation corner in a dental clinic, plainly furnished:
a wooden table with rounded corners, two low chairs, a low cabinet along the wall with a
plain ceramic jar and a small potted plant on top, a folded cloth over a chair back, a soft
rug, a window on the left giving clean daylight. The wall carries a colour and the floor is
a real wooden surface — neither is left blank white. Do NOT add any poster, chart, diagram,
screen, X-ray, plaster model or picture of teeth or buildings to the room.

PEOPLE — Simplified illustrative people: minimal features (small dot or short-line eyes, a
simple line mouth, a small nose), natural realistic head-to-body proportions, believable
ages, East Asian faces. Not photorealistic. Not faceless. No oversized heads, no noodle
limbs. Hands are drawn at a normal size, never splayed and never larger than a face.
CLOTHING — the dentist wears a short-sleeved clinic top in #b7c5d7 with its folds and
shadows in #99a7b8. The patient wears a muted clay-rose knitted top with warm grey
trousers — a clearly different colour family, so the two never blur together. Clothes are
NEVER flat single-tone shapes: model every garment with two or three tones of its own
colour, with soft coloured-pencil shading in the folds, along the sleeves, under the collar
and at the hem, and draw the real details — collar, cuff, sleeve seam, hem, a pocket.

LIGHT AND COLOUR — Ordinary bright indoor daytime, ONE light source: clean daylight from
the window on the left, warm and even. No long orange shadows, not sunset, not lamplight,
no spotlight on the crown. Across the upper part of the frame, above the two people and
clear of the bubble, draw FOUR OR FIVE long, thin, white hand-drawn lines running the same
way at the same shallow angle, evenly spaced, solid where they begin and thinning to dry
flecks — soft chalk quality, clearly hand-made. They must never begin at anyone's mouth,
nose, hand or body, must never cross a face, must never loop or curl back on themselves,
and must never resemble smoke, steam or breath.
THIS PICTURE MUST NOT GO GREY. The tiled wall and the exposed cement take up a lot of the
bubble, so the rest has to carry real colour: most coloured areas sit in the range HSL
saturation 30–50 and lightness 70–85, and at least half the picture is chromatic. Use AT
LEAST FIVE clearly different colours and give each to a named thing: the clinic top, the
patient's knitted top, the room wall, the wooden table and floor, the potted plant, the
rug, the sky and the potted plants on the balconies inside the bubble. Colour throughout —
never greyscale, never a picture that is mostly grey.
ACCENT — #335b8b appears in exactly two small places and nowhere else: one door of the low
cabinet, and a narrow horizontal band along the room wall behind the two people. Do NOT
wash the image in that colour, do NOT tint the skin, the crown, the table or the paper with
it, and do NOT make it vivid.

CRITICAL — NO WRITING ANYWHERE IN THE IMAGE. No text, letters, words, numbers, logos, shop
signs, house numbers, banners, posters, stickers, labels, captions or watermarks, in any
language or alphabet. Inside the bubble, every shopfront, shutter and wall of the building
is completely blank. In the room, the jar, the cabinet and all clothing are blank. The
crown carries no marks or numbers. There are no arrows, ticks, crosses, plus or minus signs
or dotted lines anywhere. Where writing would normally appear, leave the surface plain.

AVOID — greyscale; a picture that reads as mostly grey; photorealism; a photorealistic
mouth, gum or hand; faceless or noodle-limbed figures; thick uniform black outlines; even
mechanical vector linework; chrome, metallic or iridescent gradients; glow, sparkle or lens
flare; grey, silver, white or blue-grey hair; wrinkles, cheek shadow or facial modelling;
the crown drawn at true life size, small or hard to see; the broken area drawn small, faint,
or in the middle of the crown instead of biting into its outline; the exposed metal drawn
pale, grey or the same colour as the ceramic; more than one bubble; any arrow, pointer or
comparison mark; a second building or a before-and-after comparison inside the bubble; a
ruined, abandoned or collapsing building, structural cracks, a leaning wall, scaffolding,
workers, an accident, an injured person, blood, an ambulance, flashing lights or barrier
tape; blood, wounds, redness, gums or a mouth anywhere; decayed, dark or missing natural
teeth; any tooth shape inside the bubble; any tile anywhere in the room; anyone looking at
the viewer; a whole image tinted blue; flat untextured single-tone clothing; a bare empty
room.
