#TITLE: 變體 4 —— 整塊鑿出來的石柱 vs 貼石皮的柱子
#NOTE: 第四輪。修掉清水模的瑕疵（RC 裡有鋼筋、剝落＝結構受損）。整塊石頭是真的單一材料

Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape.

STYLE — READ THIS FIRST; IT MATTERS MORE THAN ANY OTHER SECTION.
Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration, hand-made throughout. Linework in
warm dark brown or soft charcoal, NEVER pure black: thin, hand-drawn, the weight visibly
varies along a stroke, strokes taper and sometimes break and run dry at the end — never an
even mechanical vector line. Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light marker;
colour edges a little loose and not always meeting the line. Flat fills with two or three
tones per hue — no smooth decorative gradients, no airbrush, no glow. A fine even paper
grain over every surface of the whole image.
SKIN IS THE ONE EXCEPTION to the two-or-three-tones rule: each face and each hand is ONE
single flat tone, with no modelling, no blush, no shading, no cheekbones and no jawline.
On a face, draw ONLY these six things: the outline of the head, the eyes, the eyebrows,
the nose, the mouth and the ears. Nothing else at all.
HAIR IS ALWAYS A WARM DARK BROWN — #3f3330, its shadowed side #2e2523, its lit edge
#4d403c. NEVER grey, silver, white, ash, steel or blue-grey hair.

STRUCTURE — ONE single illustration, no panels and no dividing lines. The forecourt of an
old neighbourhood temple in Taiwan, seen straight on. TWO TALL SQUARE COLUMNS STAND SIDE BY
SIDE IN THE MIDDLE OF THE FRAME, holding up the edge of a tiled temple roof above. They are
the same height, the same width and the same age, and they fill roughly two-thirds of the
frame width, reaching almost to the top edge. Two ordinary people stand small at their feet,
looking up. The two columns are the subject; everything else is the temple forecourt.

THE TWO COLUMNS — The ONLY difference between them is how each was made:
  • THE LEFT COLUMN IS CARVED FROM ONE SINGLE BLOCK OF STONE. It is one continuous warm
    pale grey granite from the ground to the top — the same stone all the way through, with
    the faint speckle of the stone running unbroken across the whole surface and around its
    corners. Shallow chisel marks curve over it where it was worked. There is no joint, no
    panel, no seam and no second material anywhere on it. Along one lower edge a corner has
    been knocked over the years and is a little rounded — and where it is knocked, the
    stone underneath is EXACTLY THE SAME STONE, the same colour and the same speckle. It
    has worn, but nothing has come off it.
  • THE RIGHT COLUMN IS A CONCRETE COLUMN FACED WITH THIN STONE SLABS. Its surface is made
    of large flat rectangular stone panels stuck on in a regular pattern, with thin straight
    joints between them, so it plainly reads as separate pieces applied over something
    underneath. ABOUT TWO THIRDS OF THE WAY UP, on the side facing the middle of the
    picture, TWO OF THE SLABS HAVE COME AWAY: the gap left behind shows plain grey concrete
    with the ridged combed lines of dried adhesive still on it, and one neighbouring slab is
    half detached and tilted a little out from the face. On the ground at its foot lie two
    fallen slabs, one cracked across the middle, with a little grey grit around them.
THE CONTRAST TO HOLD ONTO: the left column IS stone all the way through, so there is
nothing on it that can come off; the right column is a skin of stone stuck onto something
else, and the skin is what fell.

THE FORECOURT — an ordinary, warm, everyday Taiwanese temple forecourt, well used and
cared for, not grand and not derelict: worn stone paving, a low step, a plain stone
incense burner to one side, two round red paper lanterns hanging under the eaves, the
carved timber and clay-tiled edge of the temple roof above the columns, a couple of potted
plants, a bicycle leaning at the edge of the frame, a strip of blue sky. Calm and
uncluttered. No crowd, no ceremony, no smoke.

THE TWO PEOPLE — Small, standing together on the paving near the front left, seen from
behind and to one side so we mostly read their backs and the tilt of their heads. A woman
in her thirties and a man of about forty, both East Asian, both looking UP at the gap where
the slabs came off. One has a hand half-raised, pointing loosely up at it; the other has
both hands in their pockets, head tipped back. Unhurried and quietly curious — not alarmed,
not shocked. Neither looks at the viewer. Ordinary passers-by in ordinary clothes, NOT
builders, NOT in uniform, NOT worshippers holding incense.

PEOPLE — Simplified illustrative people: minimal features, natural realistic head-to-body
proportions, believable ages, East Asian faces where a face is visible at all. Not
photorealistic. Not faceless. No oversized heads, no noodle limbs.
CLOTHING — the woman wears a top in #b7c5d7 with its folds and shadows in #99a7b8 and warm
grey trousers; the man wears a muted clay-rose jacket. Clothes are NEVER flat single-tone
shapes: give each garment two or three tones of its own colour with soft shading where the
fabric gathers.

LIGHT AND COLOUR — Ordinary bright daytime, ONE light source: soft even daylight from the
left, warm and gentle. No long orange shadows, not sunset, not night, no spotlight, no
dramatic beam. Across the sky above the roof, well clear of the columns, draw FOUR OR FIVE
long, thin, white hand-drawn lines running the same way at the same shallow angle, evenly
spaced, solid where they begin and thinning to dry flecks — soft chalk quality, clearly
hand-made. They must never begin at anyone's mouth, nose, hand or body, must never touch
the columns, must never loop or curl back on themselves, and must never resemble smoke,
steam or breath.
THIS PICTURE MUST NOT GO GREY. Stone and concrete take up a lot of the frame, so the rest
of it has to carry real colour: most coloured areas sit in the range HSL saturation 30–50
and lightness 70–85, and at least half the picture is chromatic. Use AT LEAST FIVE clearly
different colours and give each to a named thing: the blue sky, the clay-tiled roof, the
red paper lanterns, the carved timber under the eaves, the potted plants, the woman's top,
the man's jacket. Colour throughout — never greyscale, never a picture that is mostly grey.
ACCENT — #335b8b appears in exactly two small places and nowhere else: the temple door
panels behind the columns, and one painted timber beam under the eaves. Do NOT wash the
image in that colour, do NOT tint the skin, the stone or the paving with it, and do NOT
make it vivid.

CRITICAL — NO WRITING ANYWHERE IN THE IMAGE. A temple would normally be covered in
characters and couplets, so be strict: no text, letters, words, numbers, characters,
couplets, plaques, name boards, banners, logos, posters, stickers, captions or watermarks,
in any language or script. The lanterns are blank. Every plaque, panel, door and beam is
blank. Where writing would normally appear, leave the surface plain.

AVOID — greyscale; a picture that reads as mostly grey; photorealism; faceless or
noodle-limbed figures; thick uniform black outlines; even mechanical vector linework;
chrome, metallic or iridescent gradients; glow or lens flare; grey, silver, white or
blue-grey hair; wrinkles, cheek shadow or facial modelling; any suggestion that one column
is richer, newer or better than the other; a ruin, a derelict or abandoned temple; cracks
running through either column, a leaning column, rubble or collapse; an accident, an
injured person, blood, barrier tape or a cordon; scaffolding, workers or hard hats; dragons,
statues, deities, worshippers, incense smoke or a religious ceremony; any arrow, tick,
cross, label or comparison mark between the two columns; teeth, mouths, gums or any dental
object anywhere in the picture; anyone looking at the viewer; a whole image tinted blue;
flat untextured single-tone clothing; an empty white background.
