#TITLE: 變體 1 —— 兩棟並排的台灣街屋
#NOTE: 第三輪，使用者出的梗（貼磁磚 vs 澆注灌漿）。左清水模、右貼二丁掛，右上剝落一片。共鳴最高

Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape.

STYLE — READ THIS FIRST; IT MATTERS MORE THAN ANY OTHER SECTION.
Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration, hand-made throughout. Linework in
warm dark brown or soft charcoal, NEVER pure black: thin, hand-drawn, the weight visibly
varies along a stroke, strokes taper and sometimes break and run dry at the end — never an
even mechanical vector line. Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light marker;
colour edges a little loose and not always meeting the line. Flat fills with two or three
tones per hue — no smooth decorative gradients, no airbrush, no glow. A fine even paper
grain over every surface of the whole image.
SKIN IS THE ONE EXCEPTION to the two-or-three-tones rule: each face and each hand is ONE
single flat tone, with no modelling, no blush, no shading, no cheekbones and no jawline.
On a face, draw ONLY these six things: the outline of the head, the eyes, the eyebrows,
the nose, the mouth and the ears. Nothing else at all.
HAIR IS ALWAYS A WARM DARK BROWN — #3f3330, its shadowed side #2e2523, its lit edge
#4d403c. NEVER grey, silver, white, ash, steel or blue-grey hair.

STRUCTURE — ONE single illustration, no panels and no dividing lines. An ordinary Taiwanese
town street, seen straight on from the pavement across the road. TWO NARROW FOUR-STOREY
TOWNHOUSES STAND SIDE BY SIDE IN THE MIDDLE OF THE FRAME, touching each other, filling
roughly two-thirds of the frame width and reaching almost to the top edge. Two ordinary
people stand small on the pavement in front of them, looking up. The two houses are the
subject of the picture; everything else is street.

THE TWO HOUSES — They are the same age, the same height, the same width, the same number
of floors and the same window openings, and both are plainly lived in and well looked
after: laundry poles, potted plants on the balconies, a rooftop water tank on each, window
grilles, air-conditioning units, a covered ground-floor arcade running across both. NEITHER
HOUSE IS RICHER, NEWER, SMARTER OR MORE RUN-DOWN THAN THE OTHER — this is not a comparison
between a good building and a bad one. The ONLY difference between them is how their front
wall was made:
  • THE LEFT HOUSE IS CAST-IN-PLACE FAIR-FACED CONCRETE. Its whole front is one continuous
    warm pale grey surface, poured as a single mass — no tiles, no panels, no cladding and
    no joints of any applied material. The only marks on it are the marks the pour itself
    left: faint horizontal lines where the formwork boards met, and a regular grid of small
    round tie-holes, evenly spaced across the wall. The surface is smooth, whole and
    unbroken from the ground to the roof.
  • THE RIGHT HOUSE IS FACED WITH SMALL RECTANGULAR WALL TILES. Its whole front is covered
    in a regular grid of small pale warm off-white tiles, each about twice as wide as it is
    tall, laid in even courses with thin grout lines between them, so that the wall plainly
    reads as many separate pieces stuck onto something underneath. HIGH ON THE WALL, on the
    side facing the middle of the picture, A PATCH OF TILES HAS COME AWAY: an irregular
    area roughly the size of a door, where the tiles are gone and the rough darker grey
    cement bed underneath is exposed, with a ragged fringe of half-attached tiles around
    its edge. On the pavement below, five or six loose tiles lie scattered, one or two
    snapped in half, and a single yellow-and-red traffic cone stands beside them. Nothing
    else about the house is damaged.
The contrast to hold onto is: ONE WALL IS THE BUILDING ITSELF; THE OTHER WALL IS A LAYER
STUCK ONTO THE BUILDING, and that layer is what came off.

THE STREET — an ordinary, warm, everyday Taiwanese street, not a slum and not a showpiece:
a covered arcade at ground level with plain shopfronts and roller shutters, a scooter or
two parked at the kerb, a bicycle, potted plants beside a doorway, electricity cables
running across the top of the frame, a low kerb and a paved pavement, a strip of blue sky
above the rooftops. Keep it calm and uncluttered — enough to read as a real street, never
a crowded busy one. No cars in motion, no traffic, no crowd.

THE TWO PEOPLE — Small, standing together on the pavement near the front left, seen from
behind and to one side so we mostly read their backs and the tilt of their heads. A woman
in her thirties and a man of about forty, both East Asian, both looking UP at the patch of
missing tiles. One of them has a hand half-raised, pointing loosely up at it; the other has
both hands in their pockets, head tipped back. They are unhurried and quietly curious — not
alarmed, not shocked, not pointing dramatically. Neither looks at the viewer. They are
ordinary passers-by in ordinary clothes, NOT builders, NOT in uniform, NOT in hard hats.

PEOPLE — Simplified illustrative people: minimal features, natural realistic head-to-body
proportions, believable ages, East Asian faces where a face is visible at all. Not
photorealistic. Not faceless. No oversized heads, no noodle limbs.
CLOTHING — the woman wears a top in #b7c5d7 with its folds and shadows in #99a7b8 and warm
grey trousers; the man wears a muted clay-rose jacket — a clearly different colour family,
so the two never blur together. Even at this small size the clothes are NEVER flat
single-tone shapes: give each garment two or three tones of its own colour with soft
shading where the fabric gathers.

LIGHT AND COLOUR — Ordinary bright daytime, ONE light source: soft even daylight from the
left, warm and gentle. No long orange shadows, not sunset, not night, no spotlight, no
dramatic beam. Across the sky above the rooftops, well clear of the two houses, draw FOUR
OR FIVE long, thin, white hand-drawn lines running the same way at the same shallow angle,
evenly spaced, solid where they begin and thinning to dry flecks — soft chalk quality,
clearly hand-made, describing moving air. They must never begin at anyone's mouth, nose,
hand or body, must never touch the buildings, must never loop or curl back on themselves,
and must never resemble smoke, steam or breath.
Most coloured areas sit in the range HSL saturation 30–50 and lightness 70–85 — clearly
coloured and readable, never washed out into near-white pastels and never heavy or muddy.
About half the picture is chromatic. Use AT LEAST FIVE clearly different colours and give
each to a named thing: the sky, the concrete wall, the tiled wall, the arcade shutters,
the potted plants, the woman's top, the man's jacket, the scooter. Colour throughout —
never greyscale.
ACCENT — #335b8b appears in exactly two small places and nowhere else: the roller shutters
under the arcade, and the rooftop water tanks. Do NOT wash the image in that colour, do NOT
tint the skin, the walls, the pavement or the sky with it, and do NOT make it vivid.

CRITICAL — NO WRITING ANYWHERE IN THE IMAGE. This street would normally be full of signs,
so be strict: no text, letters, words, numbers, logos, shop signs, banners, house numbers,
number plates, posters, stickers, captions or watermarks, in any language or alphabet. Every
shopfront, shutter, awning, sign board, scooter plate and wall is completely blank. The
traffic cone carries no writing. Where writing would normally appear, leave the surface
plain.

AVOID — greyscale; photorealism; faceless or noodle-limbed figures; thick uniform black
outlines; even mechanical vector linework; chrome, metallic or iridescent gradients; glow
or lens flare; grey, silver, white or blue-grey hair; wrinkles, cheek shadow or facial
modelling; any suggestion that one house is richer, newer, cleaner or better than the
other; a luxury villa or an architectural showpiece; a slum, a ruin, a derelict or
abandoned building; cracks running through the structure, a leaning wall, rubble or
collapse; an accident, an injured person, blood, an ambulance, a police car, flashing
lights, barrier tape or a cordon; workers, scaffolding, cranes or hard hats; any arrow,
tick, cross, label or comparison mark between the two houses; teeth, mouths, gums or any
dental object anywhere in the picture; anyone looking at the viewer; a whole image tinted
blue; flat untextured single-tone clothing; an empty white background; a crowded busy
street.
