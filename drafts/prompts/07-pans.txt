#TITLE: 變體 5 —— 廚房：鑄鐵鍋 vs 琺瑯鍋崩了一角
#NOTE: 第四輪。不是比喻是同一種技術——琺瑯就是把瓷粉燒在金屬上，和金屬燒瓷牙冠一樣。共鳴最高，但沒有建築的尺度感

Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape.

STYLE — READ THIS FIRST; IT MATTERS MORE THAN ANY OTHER SECTION.
Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration, hand-made throughout. Linework in
warm dark brown or soft charcoal, NEVER pure black: thin, hand-drawn, the weight visibly
varies along a stroke, strokes taper and sometimes break and run dry at the end — never an
even mechanical vector line. Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light marker;
colour edges a little loose and not always meeting the line. Flat fills with two or three
tones per hue — no smooth decorative gradients, no airbrush, no glow. A fine even paper
grain over every surface of the whole image.
SKIN IS THE ONE EXCEPTION to the two-or-three-tones rule: each face and each hand is ONE
single flat tone, with no modelling, no blush, no shading, no cheekbones and no jawline.
On a face, draw ONLY these six things: the outline of the head, the eyes, the eyebrows,
the nose, the mouth and the ears. Nothing else at all.
HAIR IS ALWAYS A WARM DARK BROWN — #3f3330, its shadowed side #2e2523, its lit edge
#4d403c. NEVER grey, silver, white, ash, steel or blue-grey hair.

STRUCTURE — ONE single illustration, no panels and no dividing lines. WE ARE CLOSE IN, at
worktop height, in an ordinary home kitchen. TWO COOKING POTS STAND SIDE BY SIDE ON A
WOODEN WORKTOP IN THE MIDDLE OF THE FRAME, drawn large: together they fill roughly half the
frame width and reach about two-thirds of the frame height. Behind and above them, a woman
seen from the chest up is reaching in to pick one of them up, her head tilted down, looking
at the pots. The two pots are the subject of the picture; she is at work around them.

THE TWO POTS — Both are the same everyday size and the same simple shape, a round pot with
two small side handles and a lid resting beside it. The ONLY difference is how each one is
made:
  • THE LEFT POT IS ONE SINGLE PIECE OF METAL — plain cast iron, the same dark warm
    grey-brown all over, inside and out, with a soft matte surface. Along its rim and on one
    side it is scuffed and knocked from years of use, and everywhere it is knocked the metal
    underneath IS EXACTLY THE SAME METAL, the same colour all the way through. It is worn,
    but nothing has come off it. No coating, no shine, no second colour anywhere.
  • THE RIGHT POT IS ENAMELLED — a metal pot with a smooth glossy layer of pale warm
    off-white enamel fired onto the outside of it. On the rim nearest the middle of the
    picture, A PIECE OF THE ENAMEL HAS CHIPPED OFF: a small irregular patch with a slightly
    jagged edge, through which the DARK METAL UNDERNEATH shows plainly and unmistakably, in
    clear contrast to the pale enamel around it. Two tiny pale enamel flakes lie on the
    worktop just below it. Everything else about the pot is intact and clean.
THE CONTRAST TO HOLD ONTO: one pot IS metal all the way through, so there is nothing on it
that can come off; the other has a thin layer fired onto metal, and that layer is what
chipped.

THE KITCHEN — an ordinary, warm, well-used Taiwanese home kitchen, plainly furnished and
clearly cooked in: a wooden worktop, a small tiled splashback behind, a gas hob at one side
with a kettle on it, a wooden chopping board, a bowl of vegetables, a jar of cooking
chopsticks and a ladle, a hanging cloth, a small potted herb on the windowsill, a window at
the left giving clean daylight. The walls and worktop are never left blank white. Do NOT
add any packaging, bottles with labels, appliances with control panels or screens.

THE WOMAN — about forty, East Asian, seen from the chest up behind the worktop. Her head is
tilted down and we see her face at a three-quarter angle. She is looking at the two pots,
not at the viewer. Her expression is ordinary and unhurried — she is choosing which one to
use, an everyday moment, not a decision she is anxious about. The hand nearer the viewer is
reaching towards the handle of the left pot; her other hand rests on the worktop.

PEOPLE — Simplified illustrative people: minimal features (small dot or short-line eyes, a
simple line mouth, a small nose), natural realistic head-to-body proportions, a believable
age, an East Asian face. Not photorealistic. Not faceless. No oversized head, no noodle
limbs. She never looks at the viewer, and she is not taller than half the frame.
CLOTHING — a top in #b7c5d7 with its folds and shadows in #99a7b8, and a muted clay-rose
apron over it. Clothes are NEVER flat single-tone shapes: give each garment two or three
tones of its own colour with soft coloured-pencil shading in the folds, and draw the real
details — collar, cuff, apron strap and tie, a pocket.

LIGHT AND COLOUR — Ordinary bright indoor daytime, ONE light source: clean daylight from
the window on the left, warm and even. No long orange shadows, not sunset, not lamplight,
no spotlight on the pots. Across the upper part of the frame, above her and not touching
her, draw FOUR OR FIVE long, thin, white hand-drawn lines running the same way at the same
shallow angle, evenly spaced, solid where they begin and thinning to dry flecks — soft
chalk quality, clearly hand-made. They must never begin at her mouth, nose, hands or body,
must never loop or curl back on themselves, and must never resemble smoke, steam, cooking
vapour or breath.
Most coloured areas sit in the range HSL saturation 30–50 and lightness 70–85 — clearly
coloured and readable, never washed out into near-white pastels and never heavy or muddy.
About half the picture is chromatic. Use AT LEAST FIVE clearly different colours and give
each to a named thing: the splashback tiles, the wooden worktop, her top, her apron, the
vegetables in the bowl, the potted herb, the kettle. Colour throughout — never greyscale.
ACCENT — #335b8b appears in exactly two small places and nowhere else: the splashback tiles
and one cupboard door below the worktop. Do NOT wash the image in that colour, do NOT tint
the skin, the pots, the worktop or the paper with it, and do NOT make it vivid.

CRITICAL — NO WRITING ANYWHERE IN THE IMAGE. No text, letters, words, numbers, logos, brand
marks, packaging, labels, captions or watermarks, in any language or alphabet. The pots
carry no brand mark of any kind, on the side, the handle or the lid. Jars, bottles, tins and
the kettle are completely blank. Where writing would normally appear, leave the surface
plain.

AVOID — greyscale; photorealism; faceless or noodle-limbed figures; thick uniform black
outlines; even mechanical vector linework; chrome, mirror-polished metal, metallic or
iridescent gradients; glow, sparkle or lens flare; grey, silver, white or blue-grey hair;
wrinkles, cheek shadow or facial modelling; any suggestion that one pot is more expensive,
newer or better than the other; a dirty, burnt, greasy or rusty kitchen; rust running down
the enamel pot; food cooking, steam, flames or smoke; a recognisable branded or designer
pot; any arrow, tick, cross, label or comparison mark between the two pots; teeth, mouths,
gums or any dental object anywhere in the picture; her looking at the viewer; a whole image
tinted blue; flat untextured single-tone clothing; a bare empty kitchen.
