#TITLE: 變體 3 —— 施工中的對照
#NOTE: 第三輪。直接畫「工法」本身：左邊正在灌漿、右邊正在一片一片貼磁磚
---
Editorial illustration, 16:9 landscape.

STYLE — READ THIS FIRST; IT MATTERS MORE THAN ANY OTHER SECTION.
Contemporary printed-magazine editorial illustration, hand-made throughout. Linework in
warm dark brown or soft charcoal, NEVER pure black: thin, hand-drawn, the weight visibly
varies along a stroke, strokes taper and sometimes break and run dry at the end — never an
even mechanical vector line. Colour applied like soft coloured pencil and light marker;
colour edges a little loose and not always meeting the line. Flat fills with two or three
tones per hue — no smooth decorative gradients, no airbrush, no glow. A fine even paper
grain over every surface of the whole image.
SKIN IS THE ONE EXCEPTION to the two-or-three-tones rule: each face and each hand is ONE
single flat tone, with no modelling, no blush, no shading, no cheekbones and no jawline.
On a face, draw ONLY these six things: the outline of the head, the eyes, the eyebrows,
the nose, the mouth and the ears. Nothing else at all.
HAIR IS ALWAYS A WARM DARK BROWN — #3f3330, its shadowed side #2e2523, its lit edge
#4d403c. NEVER grey, silver, white, ash, steel or blue-grey hair.

STRUCTURE — ONE single illustration, no panels and no dividing lines. TWO NARROW HOUSES OF
THE SAME HEIGHT AND WIDTH STAND SIDE BY SIDE IN THE MIDDLE OF THE FRAME, both still being
built, filling roughly two-thirds of the frame width and reaching almost to the top edge.
THE PICTURE IS ABOUT THE TWO DIFFERENT WAYS THEIR WALLS ARE BEING MADE, happening at the
same moment on the same street. Two ordinary passers-by stand small on the pavement in
front, watching.

LEFT HOUSE — BEING POURED IN ONE GO. Its front is a tall wall of plywood formwork panels
tied together with rows of small round tie-rods, standing from the ground right up to the
top. At the top of the wall, a concrete pump hose curves down over the edge and thick pale
wet concrete is pouring out of it INTO the space between the boards, filling it as one
continuous mass. A worker stands on a small platform at the top guiding the hose, and a
second worker beside him holds a poker vibrator down into the wet concrete. Where the
formwork has already been struck away at the bottom left of the wall, the finished surface
is revealed: one continuous warm pale grey face, whole and unbroken, marked only by faint
horizontal board lines and a grid of small round tie-holes. It is plainly ONE PIECE, made
in a single pour.

RIGHT HOUSE — BEING TILED PIECE BY PIECE. Its front is a plain grey rendered wall with a
simple metal scaffold standing against it. On the scaffold, at two different heights, two
workers are sticking SMALL RECTANGULAR WALL TILES onto the wall ONE AT A TIME: one holds a
notched trowel and is combing a bed of adhesive across the wall in ridged stripes; the
other presses a single tile into place with both thumbs, a stack of loose tiles in a
shallow tray hooked on the scaffold beside him. About the lower two-thirds of the wall is
already tiled, in even courses with thin grout lines; the top third is still bare grey
render with the combed adhesive stripes showing, and a ragged uneven edge of the last
course laid, so it plainly reads as a surface being ADDED ON, course by course, and not yet
finished. A few spare tiles lie on the pavement below.
THE CONTRAST TO HOLD ONTO: on the left, the wall IS the building and it is being made all
at once; on the right, the wall is a skin being stuck onto a building, one piece at a time.

THE STREET — an ordinary, warm, everyday Taiwanese street: a low kerb and paved pavement, a
parked scooter, electricity cables across the top of the frame, a potted plant by a
doorway, a strip of blue sky above the rooftops, the plain end wall of a neighbouring
building at the frame edge. Calm and uncluttered — enough to read as a real street, never
crowded. No traffic, no crowd, no dust cloud, no chaos.

THE WORKERS AND THE PASSERS-BY — The four workers are calm, competent and unhurried, each
plainly absorbed in the job in their hands; they wear ordinary work clothes and plain
hard hats, and none of them looks at the viewer. Two of them are women. The two passers-by
stand together on the pavement near the front left, seen from behind and to one side, both
East Asian, one in their thirties and one about forty, looking up at the two walls; one has
a hand half-raised, the other has both hands in their pockets. They are quietly curious,
not alarmed. Nobody is in danger, nobody is falling, nothing is going wrong.

PEOPLE — Simplified illustrative people: minimal features, natural realistic head-to-body
proportions, believable ages, East Asian faces where a face is visible at all. Not
photorealistic. Not faceless. No oversized heads, no noodle limbs.
CLOTHING — the passer-by woman wears a top in #b7c5d7 with its folds and shadows in
#99a7b8 and warm grey trousers; the passer-by man wears a muted clay-rose jacket. The
workers wear warm sand, olive and clay work clothes — every person a different colour, so
no two blur together. Clothes are NEVER flat single-tone shapes: give each garment two or
three tones of its own colour with soft shading where the fabric gathers.

LIGHT AND COLOUR — Ordinary bright daytime, ONE light source: soft even daylight from the
left, warm and gentle. No long orange shadows, not sunset, not night, no spotlight, no
dramatic beam. Across the sky above the rooftops, well clear of the two houses, draw FOUR
OR FIVE long, thin, white hand-drawn lines running the same way at the same shallow angle,
evenly spaced, solid where they begin and thinning to dry flecks — soft chalk quality,
clearly hand-made. They must never begin at anyone's mouth, nose, hand or body, must never
touch the buildings, must never loop or curl back on themselves, and must never resemble
smoke, steam or breath.
Most coloured areas sit in the range HSL saturation 30–50 and lightness 70–85 — clearly
coloured and readable, never washed out into near-white pastels and never heavy or muddy.
About half the picture is chromatic. Use AT LEAST FIVE clearly different colours and give
each to a named thing: the sky, the plywood formwork, the grey render, the pale tiles, the
scaffold, the workers' clothes, the potted plant, the scooter. Colour throughout — never
greyscale.
ACCENT — #335b8b appears in exactly two small places and nowhere else: the scaffold tubes
and one shutter at street level. Do NOT wash the image in that colour, do NOT tint the
skin, the walls, the pavement or the sky with it, and do NOT make it vivid.

CRITICAL — NO WRITING ANYWHERE IN THE IMAGE. This street would normally be full of signs,
so be strict: no text, letters, words, numbers, logos, shop signs, banners, safety notices,
house numbers, number plates, brand marks on the pump, the tools, the tile trays, the hard
hats or the clothing, no posters, stickers, captions or watermarks, in any language or
alphabet. Every surface that would normally carry writing is blank. Where writing would
normally appear, leave the surface plain.

AVOID — greyscale; photorealism; faceless or noodle-limbed figures; thick uniform black
outlines; even mechanical vector linework; chrome, metallic or iridescent gradients; glow
or lens flare; grey, silver, white or blue-grey hair; wrinkles, cheek shadow or facial
modelling; any suggestion that one house is richer, newer or better than the other; a
luxury villa or an architectural showpiece; a slum, a ruin or a derelict building; an
accident, a fall, an injury, blood, an ambulance, flashing lights or barrier tape; a dust
cloud, spilled concrete, mess or chaos; a huge crane, a tower crane or heavy machinery
dominating the picture; any arrow, tick, cross, label or comparison mark between the two
houses; teeth, mouths, gums or any dental object anywhere in the picture; anyone looking at
the viewer; a whole image tinted blue; flat untextured single-tone clothing; an empty white
background.
